# Regulation X — Section 8 Kickback Prohibition and Affiliated Business Arrangements

## Overview

Section 8 of RESPA (12 USC 2607), implemented by §§1024.14–1024.15, prohibits payments and receipt of any fee, kickback, or thing of value in connection with referrals of settlement service business in federally related mortgage loan transactions. It is the primary US federal anti-kickback rule for residential mortgage settlement services. The prohibition is structural — it targets the incentive to refer, not just the payment itself — and applies regardless of whether the underlying services are performed.

Section 8 also establishes a safe harbor for affiliated business arrangements (AfBAs), but the safe harbor is narrow and all conditions must be satisfied simultaneously.

---

## Section 8(a) — Prohibition on Referral Fees (§1024.14(b))

### The Core Rule

No person shall give and no person shall accept any **fee, kickback, or thing of value** pursuant to any **agreement or understanding, oral or otherwise**, that business incident to or part of a settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan shall be referred to any person. **(12 USC 2607(a); §1024.14(b))**

### "Thing of Value" — Extremely Broad Definition

"Thing of value" is defined to include any payment, advance, funds, loan, service, or other consideration. The regulation and case law have interpreted this to include:

- Cash payments and commissions
- Stock, stock options, and other equity interests
- Increased equity in a joint venture
- Discounts, credits, or rebates on goods or services
- Special bank account terms (below-market rates)
- Services performed at below-market rates
- Reduced rent for office space
- Travel, entertainment, and trips
- Marketing services, printing, or promotional materials
- Gifts and meals **(§1024.14(d))**

There is no minimum threshold — even nominal payments constitute things of value if given pursuant to a referral agreement or understanding.

### "Agreement or Understanding" — No Written Contract Required

An "agreement or understanding" for referral of business does not need to be written or express. It can be established by a **practice, pattern, or course of conduct**. Where, over time, two settlement service providers consistently refer business to each other, that pattern of conduct is sufficient to establish the requisite agreement or understanding — even without any written contract, verbal agreement, or explicit promise. **(§1024.14(e))**

This is the most critical aspect of the prohibition for fintechs: informal arrangements, co-marketing relationships, and mutual referral practices that are never memorialised in writing can still constitute Section 8 violations if business referrals flow pursuant to them.

### "Referral" Defined

A referral includes any oral or written action that has the effect of affirmatively influencing the selection of a settlement service provider by a borrower. Simply mentioning a provider to a borrower in the context of a transaction can constitute a referral. **(§1024.14(f))**

## Section 8(b) — Prohibition on Fee Splitting and Unearned Fees (§1024.14(c))

No person shall give or accept any portion, split, or percentage of any charge made or received for the rendering of a settlement service in connection with a federally related mortgage loan **other than for services actually performed**. **(12 USC 2607(b); §1024.14(c))**

This prohibition targets:
- Fee splits where one party performs no services
- "Administrative fees" or "processing fees" charged in addition to compensation for actual services, where the additional charge has no corresponding service
- Markups on third-party services where the marked-up amount is not disclosed and does not represent services performed by the marking-up entity

### Services Actually Performed

A payment is permissible under Section 8(b) only if:
1. Services were actually performed
2. The payment does not exceed the reasonable value of those services

A company that performs only nominal services (e.g., "coordination" or "marketing") cannot receive a portion of a settlement service charge that reflects the full value of the underlying service. **(§1024.14(c))**

---

## Section 8 Exceptions — What Is Permitted (§1024.14(g))

The following do not violate Section 8:
1. **Employer-to-employee payments:** Payment by an employer to a bona fide employee of the employer for any referral activity
2. **Cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between real estate agents and brokers:** Where the payment is consistent with a cooperative brokerage arrangement
3. **Affiliated Business Arrangements:** That satisfy all conditions of the AfBA safe harbor under §1024.15
4. **Controlled business arrangements (pre-1996 exemption):** Arrangements that meet the prior HUD controlled business arrangement standards
5. **Normal promotional and educational activities:** That do not involve payment conditioned on referral of business, and do not involve more than nominal value
6. **Fees between a secondary market purchaser and the originating lender** for mortgage servicing rights, if they do not include referral fee components **(§1024.14(g))**

The employer-to-employee exception requires a genuine employment relationship with W-2 status and is not satisfied by independent contractor arrangements structured to look like employment.

## Affiliated Business Arrangements — §1024.15 Safe Harbor

### What an AfBA Is

An "affiliated business arrangement" is an arrangement in which:
- A person is in a position to refer settlement service business (a "referrer")
- A person has an **ownership or franchise interest of more than 1%** in a provider of settlement services
- The person refers business to that provider, or influences or facilitates such a referral **(§1024.15(b))**

"Control" for AfBA purposes exists where one entity has **more than 20% voting interest** in another. **(§1024.15(b)(3))**

### The Three-Condition Safe Harbor

An AfBA is exempt from Section 8(a) liability only if **all three** of the following conditions are satisfied simultaneously:

**Condition 1 — Written disclosure at time of referral**
At or before the time of referral, the referring party must give a written **Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement** to the person being referred. The disclosure must be in substantially the format prescribed by **Appendix D** of Part 1024. The Appendix D format requires:
- Identification of the referring party and the affiliated provider
- Description of the business arrangement and the ownership or other beneficial interest
- Estimated charges of the affiliated provider
- Statement that the person is not required to use the affiliated provider
- Statement that the referring party may receive a benefit from the arrangement **(§1024.15(a)(1))**

**Condition 2 — No requirement to use the affiliated provider**
The referring party may not require the borrower or other person to use the affiliated settlement service provider. The person being referred must retain the right to choose any provider. **(§1024.15(a)(2))**

**Narrow exceptions:** The lender may require the borrower to use:
- A particular attorney
- A credit reporting agency
- A real estate appraiser chosen by the lender

These exceptions apply to the lender's own operational requirements, not to AfBA referrals generally.

**Condition 3 — Only permissible return from the arrangement**
The only thing of value received by the referring party from the AfBA arrangement must be:
- A **return on ownership interest** (dividends, profit distributions, or proceeds from the sale of the ownership interest); **or**
- A **bona fide franchise fee** (for arrangements structured as a franchise relationship) **(§1024.15(a)(3))**

The referring party cannot receive: management fees, administrative fees, enhanced compensation, or other additional payments from the affiliated provider beyond the permitted ownership return. Any such payments destroy the safe harbor.

### Consequence of Failing Any Condition

Failure to satisfy **any one** of the three conditions means the AfBA safe harbor is unavailable entirely. The referral is then evaluated under the Section 8(a) prohibition, and both the referral fee received and the referral itself may constitute violations. There is no partial safe harbor.

## Civil and Criminal Liability

### Criminal (RESPA §8)

Any person who violates Section 8 is subject to:
- A **fine of not more than $10,000** per violation
- **Imprisonment for not more than 1 year** per violation
- Or both **(12 USC 2607(d)(1))**

### Civil (RESPA §8)

A borrower has a private right of action against any person who violates Section 8. Civil damages are:
- **3 times the amount of any charge paid** for the settlement service in which the violation occurred
- Plus costs and **reasonable attorneys' fees** **(12 USC 2607(d)(2))**

Treble damages are measured against the total charge paid, not merely the kickback amount — a $5,000 settlement service charge where a $500 kickback occurred yields $15,000 in civil liability (3 × $5,000).

### Statute of Limitations

The statute of limitations for Section 8 civil actions is **1 year** from the date of the violation. **(12 USC 2614)**

### CFPB Regulatory Enforcement

The CFPB enforces Section 8 as part of its RESPA enforcement authority. Regulatory enforcement actions can seek:
- Civil monetary penalties of up to $1,000,000 per day for knowing violations
- Restitution of charges collected in connection with Section 8 violations
- Injunctive relief and activity restrictions **(12 USC 5565)**

## Fintech Applicability

Section 8 applies to any person involved in a federally related mortgage transaction, including:
- **Mortgage lead generation platforms** — platforms that charge fees for referrals to lenders are within Section 8's scope; the fee must reflect the value of services performed (e.g., marketing) not the referral itself
- **Digital real estate platforms** — platforms that refer users to title companies, settlement agents, or mortgage lenders must comply with disclosure requirements if they have an ownership interest in those providers
- **Bank-fintech joint ventures** — a fintech that receives an ownership stake in a title company or mortgage servicer in exchange for directing customer applications is an AfBA that must satisfy all three conditions
- **Proptech platforms** — real estate platforms that bundle home search, mortgage origination, and title services through affiliated companies must structure all cross-referrals through compliant AfBA disclosures

## Key Points for Agents

- **"Agreement or understanding" requires no written contract.** A pattern of mutual referrals between two companies, even if never reduced to writing, is sufficient to establish Section 8(a) liability. Fintechs with informal co-marketing or referral relationships must analyse whether a pattern of conduct has established an implied agreement. **(§1024.14(e))**
- **"Thing of value" is extremely broad.** Equity grants, reduced-rent office space, below-market service rates, and marketing support received in connection with referral arrangements are all potential Section 8 violations. Cash equivalents and non-cash benefits are treated identically. **(§1024.14(d))**
- **The AfBA safe harbor requires all three conditions — none can be omitted.** Providing written disclosure but maintaining a de facto requirement to use the affiliated provider destroys the safe harbor. Providing disclosure and free choice but paying a management fee in addition to dividends destroys the safe harbor. **(§1024.15(a))**
- **Treble damages are measured against the total charge, not the kickback.** Civil liability under Section 8 is 3 times the entire settlement service charge paid in connection with the violation — not 3 times the referral fee. This makes Section 8 civil exposure disproportionately large relative to the underlying kickback amount. **(12 USC 2607(d)(2))**
- **The statute of limitations for civil claims is only 1 year.** Borrowers must file within 1 year of the date of the violation. CFPB enforcement is not subject to this limitation. **(12 USC 2614)**
