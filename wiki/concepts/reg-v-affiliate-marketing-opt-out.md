# Regulation V — Affiliate Marketing Opt-Out (12 CFR Part 1022, Subpart C)

## Overview

Subpart C (§§1022.20–1022.27) implements FCRA §624 (15 USC 1681s-3). It restricts an affiliate's ability to use eligibility information received from another affiliate to make marketing solicitations to a specific consumer. The rule does not prohibit affiliates from sharing information — it restricts the *downstream use* of that shared information for targeted marketing without consumer consent.

This subpart is particularly relevant to financial groups that share data across banking, insurance, and lending affiliates and that wish to use cross-affiliate data for targeted marketing campaigns.

---

## Who Is Covered

**Coverage (§1022.20):** Any person who receives eligibility information from an affiliate and uses it to make marketing solicitations to a consumer about the person's products or services.

**"Affiliate"** means a company that is related by common ownership or affiliated by corporate control with the person sharing the information. Common corporate ownership or control is generally ≥25% of voting shares or a controlling influence. **(§1022.3(a))**

**"Eligibility information"** means any information the communication of which would be a consumer report under FCRA §603(d) but for the exception in §603(d)(2)(A) — i.e., any information that, if communicated to a non-affiliate, would be a consumer report, but which an affiliate may share without triggering consumer report status. This typically includes: account transaction and experience information (payment history, balances, product usage), as well as information obtained from a consumer report that the sharing affiliate itself received. **(§1022.20(b)(3))**

**"Solicitation"** means a targeted marketing communication to a specific consumer based on eligibility information received from an affiliate, in which the person markets its own products or services. It does **not** include: general public advertising not targeted to specific consumers based on affiliate-shared eligibility information. **(§1022.20(b)(6))**

---

## The Core Opt-Out Requirement

**General rule (§1022.21(a)):** A person may not use eligibility information received from an affiliate to make a solicitation to a consumer unless:

1. The consumer is provided a clear, conspicuous, and concise notice;
2. The consumer is given a reasonable opportunity and a reasonable and simple method to opt out; and
3. The consumer has not opted out.

**Service provider control (§1022.21(b)):** If a person uses a service provider to make solicitations using affiliate-shared eligibility information, the affiliate must: (i) establish specific written terms and conditions; and (ii) establish and maintain reasonable policies and procedures to limit the service provider's use of the eligibility information to the permitted purposes.

---

## Exceptions — When No Opt-Out Notice Is Required

The prohibition does not apply if an exception under §1022.21(c) is available:

| Exception | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Pre-existing business relationship | 18 months from last date a financial product/service contract is in force, last payment, or last deposit; **or** 3 months from an inquiry or application by the consumer **(§1022.21(c)(2))** |
| Employee benefit/contracted services | Performing services for employee benefit programs or consumer-requested contracted services **(§1022.21(c)(3))** |
| Performing services for the affiliate | Acting as a service provider to the affiliate that provided the eligibility information **(§1022.21(c)(4))** |
| Consumer-initiated communication | Consumer initiates contact and requests information about products/services **(§1022.21(c)(5))** |
| Consumer authorisation | Consumer specifically authorises solicitations **(§1022.21(c)(6))** |
| State insurance law | Compliance with state insurance laws that require use of medical record information **(§1022.21(c)(7))** |

**Pre-existing business relationship — key rule:** The 18-month period runs from the most recent triggering event (last payment, last contract date, etc.). The 3-month period for inquiries runs from the inquiry or application date. Once the pre-existing relationship expires, if no new relationship is formed, the exception no longer applies and an opt-out notice is required before soliciting. **(§1022.21(c)(2))**

---

## Scope and Duration of the Opt-Out (§1022.22)

**Coverage of the opt-out:** A consumer's opt-out election covers all affiliates listed in the notice. The notice may list specific affiliates or use a group/family description. **(§1022.22(a))**

**Minimum duration:** 5 years. The opt-out period cannot be shortened by sending an early renewal notice. **(§1022.22(b))**

**Consumer's right to opt out at any time:** A consumer may opt out at any time regardless of whether an opt-out period has started. **(§1022.22(c))**

**New relationship after all relationships terminate:** If a consumer opts out and all existing relationships between the consumer and the affiliates listed in the notice subsequently terminate, a new opt-out notice must be provided when a new relationship is established before using any affiliate-shared eligibility information from that new relationship to solicit. **(§1022.22(d))**

**Menu of alternatives:** A person may offer consumers a choice to limit only some types of eligibility information sharing or limit solicitations from only some affiliates, as long as the consumer is given at minimum the right to limit all solicitations covered by the notice. **(§1022.22(e))**

---

## Notice Contents (§1022.23)

The opt-out notice must clearly and conspicuously disclose, in plain language:

| Item | Required content |
|------|-----------------|
| 1 | The name of the affiliate(s) providing the notice |
| 2 | A list of the affiliates or types of affiliates whose use of eligibility information is covered by the notice |
| 3 | A general description of the types of eligibility information that may be used to make solicitations |
| 4 | That the consumer has the right to limit solicitations |
| 5 | The length of the election period and, if the period has a definite end, that a renewal notice will be provided |
| 6 | If provided as part of an annual notice: that the consumer's prior opt-out election remains in effect |
| 7 | A reasonable and simple method for the consumer to opt out |

**Consolidation with GLB privacy notice:** The affiliate marketing opt-out notice may be consolidated with the GLB Act privacy notice or the FCRA opt-out notice for consumer report information sharing. **(§1022.23(b))**

**Model forms:** Appendix C provides five model forms: C-1 (initial, single-affiliate), C-2 (initial, joint notice), C-3 (renewal, single-affiliate), C-4 (renewal, joint notice), C-5 (voluntary "No Marketing" notice). Use of the applicable model form constitutes compliance with the content requirements.

## Reasonable and Simple Opt-Out Methods (§1022.25)

**Methods that ARE reasonable and simple:**
- A check-off box in a prominent position on the opt-out form
- A reply form with a self-addressed envelope included with the notice
- An electronic form that can be emailed or processed at a website (if the consumer has agreed to electronic delivery)
- A toll-free telephone number consumers may call to opt out
- A consolidated opt-out mechanism for all applicable FCRA/GLB opt-out rights via a single toll-free number

**Methods that are NOT reasonable and simple:**
- Requiring the consumer to write their own letter
- Requiring the consumer to call or write to obtain an opt-out form, rather than providing the form with the notice
- Requiring the consumer who received the notice electronically to opt out only by paper mail or by visiting a different website without a link

**Specific opt-out means:** A person may require use of a specific method, provided that method is reasonable and simple for that consumer. **(§1022.25(c))**

---

## Delivery of Opt-Out Notices (§1022.26)

The notice must be provided so that each consumer can reasonably be expected to receive actual notice.

**Provides reasonable expectation of actual notice:**
- Hand delivery of a printed copy
- Mail to the last known mailing address
- Email to a consumer who has agreed to receive electronic disclosures by email from the affiliate
- Web posting at the site where the consumer obtained a product/service electronically, requiring acknowledgment

**Does NOT provide reasonable expectation of actual notice:**
- Posting on a sign in a branch or publishing in a newspaper
- Email to a consumer who has not agreed to receive electronic disclosures from the affiliate
- Web posting that does not require the consumer to acknowledge receipt

---

## Renewal of Opt-Out (§1022.27)

After a 5-year opt-out period expires, the affiliate may not resume solicitations unless:
- (i) A renewal notice is provided with a reasonable opportunity and method to renew; and the consumer does not renew; or
- (ii) An exception under §1022.21(c) applies.

**Renewal notice contents (§1022.27(b)):** The renewal notice must be clear, conspicuous, and concise and must accurately disclose 8 items:
1. Name of the affiliate(s) providing the notice
2. List of affiliates or types of affiliates covered
3. General description of types of eligibility information
4. That the consumer's prior election expired or is about to expire
5. That the consumer may elect to renew the prior election
6. If applicable, that the renewal election applies for the stated period and may be renewed again
7. (If applicable) statement about the renewal period duration and future renewal opportunity
8. A reasonable and simple method to opt out

**Timing of renewal notice:** Must be provided before solicitations that would have been prohibited by the expired opt-out are made. May be provided at any time before expiry or after expiry but before resuming solicitations.

**Combination with annual GLB privacy notice:** Providing a renewal notice with the last annual privacy notice provided before expiry is deemed a reasonable time before expiry. **(§1022.27(c)(2))**

**Cannot shorten opt-out period:** Sending a renewal notice early does not shorten the current opt-out period. **(§1022.27(d))**

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **The opt-out restriction applies to the *use* of eligibility information for targeted marketing, not to its sharing.** An affiliate group may freely share transaction/experience data for credit decisioning, fraud prevention, and servicing — the restriction bites only when that shared data is used to solicit a specific consumer about the person's products. **(§1022.21(a))**
- **Pre-existing business relationship exception — 18 months / 3 months.** The 18-month clock runs from the last transaction, payment, or deposit, or the last date the contract is still in force. The 3-month clock runs from the date of an inquiry or application. Once the clock runs out, a new opt-out notice is required before soliciting using affiliate-shared eligibility information. **(§1022.21(c)(2))**
- **The opt-out minimum is 5 years, not indefinite.** After 5 years, the affiliate may resume solicitations if a renewal notice is provided and the consumer does not renew the opt-out. A group cannot simply rely on an old opt-out indefinitely. **(§1022.22(b); §1022.27)**
- **Requiring the consumer to write their own letter is not a reasonable and simple opt-out method.** This is explicitly called out in the regulation. Any opt-out mechanism that places a meaningful burden on the consumer beyond a form, checkbox, phone call, or electronic submission will fail. **(§1022.25(b)(2))**
- **The notice may be consolidated with GLB privacy notices but must contain all 7 required items.** Merging the notices reduces compliance burden but the opt-out content cannot be buried or abbreviated to the point of losing substance. **(§1022.23)**
