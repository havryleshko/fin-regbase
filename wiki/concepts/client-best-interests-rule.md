# Client's Best Interests Rule (COBS 2.1.1R)

## What It Is

The client's best interests rule is the foundational conduct standard for all investment business regulated under COBS. It sits at the apex of the COBS hierarchy, operating as the overarching duty that all more specific rules (suitability, appropriateness, inducements, best execution, costs disclosure) are designed to operationalise.

**COBS 2.1.1R** — A firm must act honestly, fairly and professionally in accordance with the best interests of its client.

The rule is stated in three words: **honest, fair, professional.** All three must be satisfied simultaneously.

---

## Scope of Application

The rule applies in three distinct contexts — each with different client perimeter:

| Context | Applies to which clients |
|---|---|
| **Designated investment business** (non-MiFID) | **Retail clients only** |
| **MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business** | **Any client** — retail, professional, and eligible counterparty |
| **Insurance distribution** | **Any client** |

For management companies: applies in relation to any UCITS scheme the firm manages. **(COBS 2.1.1R(3))**

**Eligible counterparty business:** COBS 2.1.1AR requires that even in relation to ECP business a firm must act honestly, fairly and professionally, taking into account the nature of the eligible counterparty and its business. The ECP carve-out in MiFID narrows (but does not eliminate) this duty.

[Note: MiFID article 24(1), IDD article 17(1), UCITS Directive article 14(1)(a) and (b)]

---

## What "Honestly, Fairly and Professionally" Means

**COBS 2.3.3G** — The obligation includes both the client's best interests rule and duties under:
- **Principle 1** (integrity)
- **Principle 2** (skill, care and diligence)
- **Principle 6** (customers' interests)

This means a breach of COBS 2.1.1R is simultaneously a Principle violation. The rule is not self-contained — it synthesises the FCA's conduct principles into a specific, client-facing standard.

The FCA treats "honestly, fairly and professionally" as three cumulative requirements:

- **Honestly** — no deception, no misleading communications, no concealment of material facts or conflicts. This overlaps with Principle 1 (integrity) and the fair, clear and not misleading standard in COBS 4.
- **Fairly** — the client must not be treated inequitably relative to other clients in comparable circumstances, and the firm must not engineer outcomes that disadvantage the client for the firm's benefit. Overlaps with Principle 6 (customers' interests) and Consumer Duty (Principle 12, good outcomes).
- **Professionally** — the firm must apply appropriate skills, knowledge and diligence. A failure of process (inadequate suitability assessment, failure to gather sufficient information) can be a breach of the professional standard even without dishonesty. Overlaps with Principle 2 (skill, care and diligence).

---

## The Exclusion of Liability Prohibition

**COBS 2.1.2R** — A firm must not, in any communication relating to designated investment business, seek to exclude or restrict, or rely on any exclusion or restriction of, any duty or liability it may have to a client under the regulatory system.

**COBS 2.1.3G** — This is reinforced by general law (Unfair Terms Regulations for pre-October 2015 contracts; Consumer Rights Act thereafter) which independently limits a firm's ability to exclude or restrict duties to a consumer. A firm cannot contract out of COBS 2.1.1R.

**Practical implication:** Standard terms of business that purport to limit the firm's responsibility (e.g., "we are not responsible for the outcome of any investment recommendation") are ineffective against the regulatory duty. The duty runs from the rules, not from contract.

---

## AIFM-Specific Standards

**COBS 2.1.4R** — A full-scope UK AIFM must, for all AIFs it manages:
1. Act honestly, fairly and with due skill, care and diligence in conducting activities
2. Act in the best interests of the AIF or investors and the integrity of the market
3. Treat all investors fairly
4. Not allow any investor preferential treatment unless disclosed in the AIF's instrument constituting the fund

[Note: AIFMD article 12(1)(a), (b) and (f)]

The third and fourth elements — equal treatment and prohibition on undisclosed preferential treatment — are additions to the baseline "honest, fair, professional" formulation. They are operationally significant for co-mingled structures where some investors receive preferential liquidity, fee, or reporting terms not disclosed in fund documents.

---

## Relationship to Inducements

**COBS 2.3A.7E** — A firm which fails to comply with the MiFID inducements rule (COBS 2.3A.5R) is to be regarded as not fulfilling its obligations in relation to: (1) conflicts of interest (SYSC 10); and (2) acting honestly, fairly and professionally in accordance with the best interests of its clients (COBS 2.1.1R).

This makes the link explicit: receipt of an impermissible inducement is treated as a breach of the client's best interests rule, not merely a standalone inducements breach. The same evidential provision applies in COBS 2.3 (non-MiFID) — paying commission on non-advised sales of packaged products in a way that distorts advice is treated as likely to breach the client's best interests rule. **(COBS 2.3.9G)**

---

## Relationship to Consumer Duty

The client's best interests rule predates Consumer Duty and operates alongside it. The relationship:

- **Consumer Duty (Principle 12 / PRIN 2A)** is a higher and more demanding standard — it requires firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers, with a forward-looking, proactive orientation. It applies only to retail customers in the UK.
- **COBS 2.1.1R** is the transactional standard — it applies to individual client interactions across all client types within its scope.

For retail clients in the UK, both standards apply simultaneously. Consumer Duty does not replace COBS 2.1.1R — it is an additional, overlapping obligation. Where Consumer Duty applies, COBS 2.1.1R sets the floor; Consumer Duty raises the ceiling.

The FCA's guidance on Consumer Duty (FG22/5) explicitly treats investment services as subject to the Duty: "Consumer Duty requirements overlap with and build on existing FCA rules and guidance" including COBS 2.1.1R. A firm cannot comply with Consumer Duty while breaching COBS 2.1.1R.

---

## Relationship to Suitability, Appropriateness and Best Execution

The client's best interests rule is the source obligation from which several downstream rules derive:

- **Suitability (COBS 9A)** — the requirement to gather sufficient information and make suitable personal recommendations is an operationalisation of acting in the client's best interests in the advisory context.
- **Appropriateness (COBS 10A)** — the warning/refusal obligation for complex products sold without advice is an operationalisation of the professional and fair standards.
- **Best execution (COBS 11.2A)** — the obligation to take all sufficient steps to achieve the best possible result for a client when executing orders is directly grounded in the client's best interests rule.
- **Inducements (COBS 2.3A)** — the prohibition on accepting third party payments that impair the duty to act in clients' best interests is derived from and tied to COBS 2.1.1R. Breach of the inducements rule is evidential of breach of COBS 2.1.1R.

---

## Key Points for Agents

- The client's best interests rule (COBS 2.1.1R) is the foundational conduct standard for investment business — it is the rule from which suitability, appropriateness, best execution, and inducements obligations derive their rationale.
- Three cumulative requirements: honest (no deception), fair (no inequitable treatment), professional (appropriate skill and diligence). All three must be satisfied.
- For MiFID business, the rule applies to any client — retail, professional, and ECP. For non-MiFID designated investment business, it applies to retail clients only.
- Cannot be excluded or restricted by contract (COBS 2.1.2R). Standard terms that purport to limit the firm's responsibility are ineffective against the regulatory duty.
- Breach of the MiFID inducements rule (COBS 2.3A.5R) is treated as a breach of COBS 2.1.1R — they are linked by evidential provision, not merely by inference.
- For retail clients in the UK, Consumer Duty operates alongside and above COBS 2.1.1R. Compliance with both is required; Consumer Duty does not replace the client's best interests rule.
- For AIFMs: equal treatment of investors and prohibition on undisclosed preferential treatment are explicit additional requirements beyond the baseline honest/fair/professional standard.
