# Regulation P — Disclosure Limits, Opt-Out Framework, and Exceptions

## Three-Tier Disclosure Structure

Regulation P creates a tiered framework for NPI disclosures to nonaffiliated third parties. The appropriate tier determines whether notice, opt-out, and contractual requirements apply.

| Tier | Disclosure type | Notice required | Opt-out required | Written contract required |
|------|----------------|-----------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Tier 1** | General sharing with nonaffiliated third parties for marketing or other non-excepted purposes | Yes (initial + opt-out notices) | Yes — consumer must be given 30 days to opt out before disclosure begins | No |
| **Tier 2** | Service provider and joint marketing (§1016.13) | Yes (initial notice only) | **No** | **Yes** — written contract restricting third party's use |
| **Tier 3** | Transaction processing (§1016.14) and other exceptions (§1016.15) | **No** | **No** | **No** |

**Affiliate sharing** is entirely outside Regulation P's opt-out framework — sharing information with affiliates is not restricted by Regulation P. However, the FCRA affiliate marketing opt-out (12 CFR §§1022.20–1022.27, Regulation V Subpart C) applies separately to affiliate eligibility information used for targeted marketing. **(§1016.16)**

---

## Tier 1 — General Disclosure Limits and Opt-Out (§1016.10)

An institution may not disclose NPI to a nonaffiliated third party unless:
1. An initial notice has been provided (§1016.4)
2. An opt-out notice has been provided (§1016.7)
3. The consumer has been given a **reasonable opportunity** to opt out (at least 30 days from mailing or 30 days from electronic acknowledgment)
4. The consumer has not opted out **(§1016.10(a))**

### Scope of the Opt-Out

The opt-out prohibition applies to **all NPI the institution holds about that consumer** — including NPI collected before the opt-out direction was received and regardless of whether the collection occurred before or after the direction. **(§1016.10(b))**

### Partial Opt-Out

Institutions may offer — and consumers may select — a **partial opt-out** covering only specific categories of NPI or only specific nonaffiliated third parties. **(§1016.10(c))**

---

## Tier 2 — Service Provider and Joint Marketing Exception (§1016.13)

The opt-out requirements in §§1016.7 and 1016.10 do **not apply** when NPI is provided to a nonaffiliated third party that:
- Performs services **for or on behalf of the institution**; and
- Is subject to a **written contract** prohibiting the third party from disclosing or using the information for any purpose other than carrying out the purposes for which it was disclosed

The written contract must prohibit: (a) use of the information for the third party's own marketing purposes; and (b) disclosure of the information to any other party except as permitted under exceptions in §§1016.14 or 1016.15 in the ordinary course of business. **(§1016.13(a))**

### Joint Marketing Under the Exception

Services covered by §1016.13 include **joint marketing** — marketing of: (i) the institution's own products or services; or (ii) products or services offered under joint agreements between the institution and one or more other financial institutions. A "joint agreement" must be a written contract. **(§1016.13(b)–(c))**

**Consequence of no written contract:** If an institution shares NPI with a service provider or joint marketer without a qualifying written contract, the disclosure falls into Tier 1 and requires a full notice and opt-out process.

---

## Tier 3 — Transaction Processing Exception (§1016.14)

The initial notice, opt-out notice, service-provider/joint-marketing requirements **all cease to apply** when NPI is shared as necessary to effect, administer, or enforce a consumer-requested transaction, or in connection with:
1. Servicing or processing a financial product or service that a consumer requests or authorises
2. Maintaining or servicing the consumer's account with a private label credit card program or extension of credit on behalf of such entity
3. A proposed or actual securitisation, secondary market sale (including servicing rights), or similar transaction **(§1016.14(a))**

### "Necessary to Effect, Administer, or Enforce" (§1016.14(b))

A disclosure is necessary when it is:
- Required or a lawful/appropriate method to enforce rights or provide the product/service; or
- A usual, appropriate, or acceptable method to carry out the transaction, which includes:
  - Recording, servicing, or maintaining the consumer's account
  - Administering benefits or claims
  - Providing transaction records or confirmations
  - Recognising incentives or bonuses
  - Underwriting insurance at the consumer's request
  - Payment card authorisation, settlement, billing, processing, clearing, transfer, or collection
  - Transfer of receivables or interests therein
  - Audit of debit, credit, or other payment information **(§1016.14(b))**

---

## Tier 3 — Other Exceptions (§1016.15)

The initial notice, opt-out, and service-provider requirements also **do not apply** when NPI is disclosed in the following circumstances:

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Consumer consent | With the consumer's specific consent or at the consumer's direction, where consent has not been revoked **(§1016.15(a)(1))** |
| Security and fraud prevention | To protect confidentiality/security; prevent fraud or unauthorised transactions; for institutional risk control; to persons holding a legal/beneficial interest in the consumer; to fiduciaries/representatives **(§1016.15(a)(2))** |
| Industry compliance | To insurance rate advisory organisations, guaranty funds or agencies, compliance assessors, attorneys, accountants, and auditors **(§1016.15(a)(3))** |
| Law enforcement | As specifically permitted by RFPA 1978 or 12 USC Chapter 21 to law enforcement agencies, federal banking examiners, Treasury (BSA/AML records), state insurance examiners, FTC, self-regulatory organisations, or for public safety investigations **(§1016.15(a)(4))** |
| Consumer reporting | To a consumer reporting agency (FCRA) or from a consumer report **(§1016.15(a)(5))** |
| Corporate transactions | In connection with a proposed or actual sale, merger, transfer, or exchange of all or part of a business **(§1016.15(a)(6))** |
| Legal compulsion | To comply with federal, state, or local laws; judicial process; civil/criminal/regulatory investigations; or government regulatory authority **(§1016.15(a)(7))** |

## Account Number Prohibition (§1016.12)

### The Hard Rule

An institution may **not**, directly or through an affiliate, disclose an account number or similar form of access code to **any nonaffiliated third party** for use in:
- **Telemarketing**
- **Direct mail marketing**
- **Marketing through electronic mail**

This prohibition is absolute — it cannot be cured by an opt-out waiver, consumer consent, or contractual restriction on the recipient. **(§1016.12(a))**

### Two Narrow Exceptions

| Exception | Conditions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Agent/service provider for own marketing** | Disclosure to an agent or service provider solely to perform marketing for **the institution's own products or services**, provided the agent/service provider is **not authorised to directly initiate charges** to the account **(§1016.12(b)(1))** |
| **Private label credit card or affinity program** | Disclosure to a participant in a private label credit card program or an affinity or similar program, where program participants are **identified to the customer** when the customer enters into the program **(§1016.12(b)(2))** |

### Encrypted Account Numbers

A number or code in encrypted form does not fall within this prohibition **as long as the institution does not provide the recipient with a means to decode the number or code.** **(§1016.12(c)(1))**

**Transaction account distinction:** A "transaction account" (an account other than a deposit account, share account, or credit card account, to which third parties cannot directly initiate charges) is not within the prohibition's scope. **(§1016.12(c)(2))**

## Limits on Redisclosure and Reuse (§1016.11)

### Information Received Under an Exception (§1016.11(a))

If a third party receives NPI from a financial institution under a §1016.14 or §1016.15 exception, **that third party** may only:
1. Disclose the information to the originating institution's affiliates
2. Disclose the information to its own affiliates (to the extent the originating institution could)
3. Use the information under a §1016.14 or §1016.15 exception in the ordinary course of business to carry out the purpose for which it received the information

The third party **may not** use the information for its own marketing purposes or disclose it to another party for marketing purposes. **(§1016.11(a))**

*Example:* A financial institution shares a customer list with a processing company under §1016.14. The processor may not sell that list to a marketing company or use it for the processor's own solicitations.

### Information Received Outside an Exception (§1016.11(b))

If a third party receives NPI **outside** an exception (i.e., under Tier 1 with notice and opt-out), it may disclose the information only:
1. To the originating institution's affiliates
2. To its own affiliates (to the same extent)
3. To any other person, **if** the disclosure would have been lawful if made directly by the originating institution (i.e., consistent with the originating institution's own opt-out obligations and exceptions)

### Information the Third Party Discloses Under an Exception (§1016.11(c))

When a third party that received NPI under an exception subsequently discloses it under an exception of its own:
1. The next recipient may disclose to the third party's affiliates
2. The next recipient's affiliates may use and disclose only to the same extent
3. The next recipient may use the information under a §1016.14 or §1016.15 exception to carry out the purpose for which the third party received it

## Opt-Out Duration, Mechanics, and Revocation

| Mechanic | Rule | Citation |
|----------|------|---------|
| Duration | Permanent — effective until the consumer revokes it in writing or (if agreed) electronically | §1016.7(i)(1) |
| Post-relationship | Opt-out continues to apply to NPI collected during or related to the former relationship | §1016.7(i)(2) |
| New relationship | A subsequent new customer relationship does **not** automatically reset or terminate the prior opt-out direction | §1016.7(i)(2) |
| Implementation | As soon as reasonably practicable after receipt | §1016.7(g) |
| Continuing right | Consumer may exercise the opt-out at any time | §1016.7(h) |
| Partial opt-out | Institution may allow consumers to opt out of sharing only for specific NPI categories or only with specific third parties | §1016.10(c) |
| Revocation | Consumer revokes in writing or electronically (if agreed) — revocation returns the institution to the ability to disclose | §1016.7(i)(1) |

## Interaction with FCRA Affiliate Marketing Opt-Out

Regulation P **does not restrict affiliate sharing** — sharing NPI with affiliates is not covered by the Regulation P opt-out. However, if an affiliate receives NPI and uses it for **targeted marketing** (soliciting a specific consumer based on eligibility information shared by the affiliate), the FCRA affiliate marketing opt-out framework (12 CFR §§1022.20–1022.27, Regulation V Subpart C) applies independently:

- Minimum opt-out period under Reg V: **5 years**
- Pre-existing business relationship exception: **18 months** from last transaction or **3 months** from inquiry
- Reg V opt-out notice may be **consolidated** with the Regulation P privacy notice

The two opt-out frameworks are entirely independent — a consumer who opts out under Regulation P has not thereby opted out of affiliate marketing (and vice versa). **(§1016.16; 12 CFR §§1022.20–1022.27)**

## State Law Floor

State statutes and regulations providing **greater consumer protection** than Regulation P are not inconsistent with Regulation P and are preserved. Regulation P is a federal floor, not a ceiling. Many states (California CCPA/CPRA, New York DFS Part 500, Vermont) impose additional privacy obligations beyond Regulation P. **(§1016.17)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **There are three disclosure tiers with different opt-out and notice requirements.** Nonaffiliated third party sharing for marketing requires notice + opt-out (Tier 1). Service-provider/joint-marketing sharing requires an initial notice + written contract but no opt-out (Tier 2). Transaction-processing and legal disclosures require neither notice nor opt-out (Tier 3). **(§§1016.10, 1016.13, 1016.14, 1016.15)**
- **The service-provider/joint-marketing exception requires a written contract — no contract, no exception.** Without a qualifying written contract restricting the third party's use, a service-provider disclosure falls into Tier 1 and requires a full notice and opt-out process. **(§1016.13(a))**
- **The account number prohibition is an absolute rule with no opt-out cure.** Account numbers and access codes may not be disclosed to nonaffiliated third parties for telemarketing, direct mail, or email marketing regardless of consumer consent or opt-out status — subject only to the two narrow express exceptions (own-product agent not authorised to charge; disclosed private-label/affinity program participants). **(§1016.12)**
- **Third parties that receive NPI under exceptions cannot use it for their own marketing.** Information received under a §1016.14 or §1016.15 exception is quarantined to the purpose for which it was shared. **(§1016.11(a))**
- **Regulation P does not restrict affiliate sharing; FCRA Reg V does.** The opt-out under Regulation P applies only to nonaffiliated third parties. Affiliate marketing is separately governed by the FCRA affiliate marketing opt-out (minimum 5-year opt-out period; pre-existing business relationship exception). The two frameworks do not substitute for each other. **(§1016.16; 12 CFR §§1022.20–1022.27)**
