# Regulation X — Mortgage Servicing Rules: Error Resolution, Loss Mitigation, and Early Intervention

## Overview

Subpart C of Regulation X (§§1024.30–1024.41) governs the ongoing servicing of federally related mortgage loans after origination. The servicing rules were substantially expanded by the CFPB in 2013 (effective January 10, 2014) to address systemic failures observed during the 2008–2012 foreclosure crisis, including improper payment processing, failure to communicate with struggling borrowers, and simultaneous pursuit of foreclosure and loss mitigation (dual tracking).

The servicing rules impose specific obligations across the delinquency lifecycle — from the first missed payment through foreclosure proceedings.

---

## Small Servicer Exemption (§1024.30(b))

A **small servicer** is exempt from:
- **§1024.41** — Loss mitigation procedures (in full)
- **§1024.40** — Continuity of contact requirements

A servicer qualifies as a small servicer if it services **5,000 or fewer mortgage loans** in total and all such loans were either originated by the servicer (or an affiliate) or are owned by the servicer (or an affiliate). **(§1024.30(b))**

Servicers that acquire mortgage servicing rights from unaffiliated parties do not qualify for the small servicer exemption, regardless of volume.

## Servicing Transfer Notices (§1024.33)

### Notice Requirements

Mortgage loan servicing rights are frequently transferred between servicers. When a transfer occurs:

| Party | Notice deadline |
|-------|----------------|
| Transferor (outgoing) servicer | At least **15 days before** the effective date of the transfer |
| Transferee (incoming) servicer | No later than **15 days after** the effective date of the transfer |
| Combined notice (by either party) | At least **15 days before** the effective date |

**(§1024.33(b))**

### 60-Day Grace Period

For 60 days following the effective date of a servicing transfer, a payment sent by the borrower to the **transferor (old) servicer** cannot be treated as late for any purpose. The transferor must either forward the payment to the transferee or return it to the borrower with instructions. The 60-day grace period protects borrowers from foreclosure or credit reporting consequences arising from confusion about where to send payments. **(§1024.33(c))**

## Error Resolution (§1024.35)

### What Constitutes a Notice of Error

A borrower may submit a written notice of error to the servicer asserting that the servicer committed an error relating to the servicing of the mortgage loan. The notice must identify the loan and describe the error the borrower believes occurred. No specific form is required. **(§1024.35(a))**

A servicer may designate a **specific address** for receipt of notices of error. If such an address is designated and disclosed to the borrower, the servicer need only comply with §1024.35 for notices sent to that address. **(§1024.35(c))**

### Defined Error Categories (§1024.35(b))

Section 1024.35 defines 11 categories of covered errors:

1. Failure to accept a timely payment made in accordance with a written payment agreement
2. Failure to properly apply an accepted payment to principal, interest, escrow, or other charges as required by the note
3. Failure to credit a payment as of the date received, if received by 5:00 PM on a business day
4. Failure to pay taxes, insurance premiums, or other charges from the escrow account when due
5. Imposition of a fee or charge that the servicer has no reasonable basis to impose
6. Failure to provide an accurate payoff balance within 7 business days of a written request (§1024.36(c))
7. Failure to provide accurate information to a borrower regarding loss mitigation options and foreclosure alternatives
8. Failure to properly transfer accurate information about the loan to a transferee servicer at the time of a servicing transfer
9. Making the **first notice or filing required for foreclosure** in violation of §1024.41(f) or (j)
10. Conducting a **foreclosure sale** in violation of §1024.41(g) or (j)
11. Any other error relating to the servicing of a federally related mortgage loan **(§1024.35(b))**

Categories 9 and 10 make foreclosure-related violations of §1024.41 independently actionable as errors under the error resolution framework, with their own timelines.

### Servicer Response Timeline (§1024.35(e))

| Error type | Response deadline |
|------------|-----------------|
| Payoff balance error (category 6) | Within **7 business days** of notice |
| Errors relating to foreclosure (categories 9 and 10) | Before the **foreclosure sale** date, or within 30 business days of notice, whichever is earlier |
| All other error categories | Within **30 business days** of notice |

Acknowledgment of receipt of any notice of error: **within 5 business days**. **(§1024.35(d))**

A **15-day extension** is available for all categories except payoff errors: if the servicer provides written notice to the borrower **before the deadline** explaining that the investigation requires additional time, the servicer has an additional 15 business days. **(§1024.35(e)(3))**

### Prohibition on Fees

A servicer **cannot charge any fee** to the borrower as a condition of responding to a notice of error or as a result of investigating an error, regardless of the outcome of the investigation. **(§1024.35(g)(1))**

### 60-Day Adverse Credit Reporting Freeze (§1024.35(g)(3))

After receiving a notice of error, the servicer **may not report adverse credit information** to any consumer reporting agency with respect to **any payment that is the subject of the notice of error** for a period of **60 days** following receipt of the notice.

This freeze applies regardless of whether the underlying error is ultimately confirmed. The freeze does not prevent the servicer from reporting the general delinquency of a loan to CRAs for payment periods not covered by the notice, but it blocks adverse reporting on the specific payment(s) about which error was alleged during the 60-day window. **(§1024.35(g)(3))**

## Information Requests (§1024.36)

### Scope

Borrowers may submit written requests for information about the servicing of their mortgage loan. The servicer must:
- **Acknowledge** within 5 business days
- **Respond** within 10 business days (or 30 business days for requests for the identity of the owner of the loan) **(§1024.36(d))**

A servicer may designate a specific address for information requests, separate from the address for error notices. The servicer need not respond to requests not sent to the designated address.

### No Fee for Information

The servicer cannot charge any fee for responding to an information request for information that the servicer is required by applicable law to provide free of charge, or for any information request submitted on an account that is not delinquent. **(§1024.36(g))**

### Inaccessible or Confidential Information

A servicer may decline to provide information that:
- Is not in the servicer's possession, custody, or control
- Is confidential, privileged, or otherwise protected from disclosure
- Would be voluminous, unduly burdensome to compile, or would require reproducing an entire file or set of documents **(§1024.36(f))**

## Force-Placed Insurance (§1024.37)

### Pre-Requisite Notices Before Charging

A servicer may not assess a premium charge or fee for force-placed insurance unless:
1. The servicer has sent a **first written notice** to the borrower at least **45 days before** charging, stating that the servicer has not received evidence of hazard insurance coverage and that the servicer may obtain force-placed insurance
2. The servicer has sent a **second written notice (reminder)** at least **30 days after** the first notice and **at least 15 days before** charging **(§1024.37(c))**

If the borrower provides evidence of existing coverage at any time before the force-placed insurance takes effect, the servicer must cancel the force-placed policy and refund any premiums charged for overlapping coverage periods. **(§1024.37(e))**

### Cost Reasonableness

Force-placed insurance must be "bona fide and reasonable." The CFPB has indicated that force-placed premiums that are substantially higher than available market rates or that include excessive commissions to the servicer's affiliates may violate UDAAP standards even if the Section 37 notice requirements are met. **(§1024.37(d))**

## Early Intervention (§1024.39)

### Live Contact — By Day 36

Within **36 days** after a payment becomes delinquent (i.e., the date on which the first payment became due and unpaid), the servicer must:
- Make **live contact** with the borrower; or
- Make **good-faith efforts** to establish live contact

The purpose of live contact is to inform the borrower about the availability of loss mitigation options. The regulation does not specify the form of live contact — phone, in-person, or video call all satisfy the requirement. **(§1024.39(a))**

A servicer satisfies the good-faith effort requirement by making reasonable attempts to contact the borrower using contact information in the servicer's records. A single unanswered call does not satisfy the requirement — multiple attempts on different days are generally required.

### Written Notice — By Day 45 (§1024.39(b))

If the borrower is still delinquent at **45 days** after the first missed payment, the servicer must send a **written notice** that includes:
- A brief description of available loss mitigation options
- A statement that the servicer may have options available to help the borrower avoid foreclosure
- The contact information for the **continuity of contact personnel** assigned to the borrower
- The CFPB's toll-free phone number and website for homeownership counseling

The 45-day written notice must be sent even if the servicer successfully made live contact during the day 36 window. The two requirements are independent. **(§1024.39(b))**

### Delinquency Cycle Reset

A servicer does not need to send additional early intervention notices for subsequent delinquency periods if the borrower was previously delinquent in the preceding 180 days and received a 45-day written notice in connection with that prior delinquency. **(§1024.39(c))**

## Continuity of Contact (§1024.40)

### Single Point of Contact Assignment

Servicers must maintain policies and procedures reasonably designed to assign to each borrower who becomes delinquent:
- **A single point of contact (SPOC)** — an individual or a team of individuals — accessible by phone
- The SPOC must have **direct access** to all individuals and personnel (including third-party vendors) needed to complete loss mitigation activities
- The SPOC must have authority (or ability to escalate) to **stop foreclosure proceedings** where appropriate while a loss mitigation application is pending **(§1024.40(a))**

### SPOC as a Team

The regulation expressly permits "team" or "functional" continuity of contact — a group of individuals any of whom can assist the borrower — rather than a single designated individual. This accommodation reflects operational realities for large servicers. The key requirement is that any team member must have access to the borrower's complete information and loss mitigation status. **(§1024.40(a)(2))**

### Prohibition on Conflicting Information

SPOC/continuity-of-contact personnel must provide accurate and timely information about loss mitigation options. Providing conflicting information (e.g., one agent tells the borrower a modification is pending while another refers the account to foreclosure) may constitute an error under §1024.35(b)(11) and expose the servicer to UDAAP liability. **(§1024.40(b))**

## Loss Mitigation — Dual Tracking Prohibition (§1024.41)

### Overview

Section 1024.41 is the most significant servicing rule enacted by the CFPB. It prohibits servicers from simultaneously pursuing foreclosure and processing a loss mitigation application — the practice known as "dual tracking" that was endemic during the 2008–2012 foreclosure crisis.

### Application Acknowledgment (§1024.41(b))

Within **5 business days** of receiving any loss mitigation application, the servicer must send the borrower a written acknowledgment stating:
- The date the application was received
- Whether the application is complete or incomplete
- If incomplete, a description of the documents or information needed to complete it
- A reasonable date by which the borrower must submit the missing documents **(§1024.41(b)(2))**

### Reasonable Diligence to Obtain Complete Application (§1024.41(b)(1))

The servicer must exercise **reasonable diligence** in obtaining documents and information to complete a loss mitigation application. The servicer cannot simply wait for the borrower to submit documents spontaneously — if the servicer knows what documents are needed and the borrower has not submitted them, the servicer must notify the borrower and take steps to obtain the information. **(§1024.41(b)(1))**

### The 37-Day Bright Line — Foreclosure Prohibition (§1024.41(f))

A servicer **may not make the first notice or filing required to initiate any judicial or non-judicial foreclosure process** if:
- The servicer has received a **complete loss mitigation application** from the borrower
- The complete application was received **37 or more days before a scheduled foreclosure sale** **(§1024.41(f)(1))**

The prohibition applies to the **first notice or filing** — the very first step in the foreclosure process. A servicer cannot argue that foreclosure was already underway if no first notice or filing had been made at the time the complete application was received.

**What "complete" means:** An application is complete when the servicer has received all the information it requires from the borrower. The servicer's own standards determine completeness — the servicer cannot strategically avoid completion by withholding the list of required documents. **(§1024.41(b)(1))**

### Evaluation Timeline — 30 Days (§1024.41(c))

Within **30 days** of receiving a complete loss mitigation application, the servicer must:
1. Evaluate the borrower for all available loss mitigation options
2. Provide the borrower with a written notice of the servicer's determination, identifying the options available (if any) or stating the reasons for denial of each loss mitigation option **(§1024.41(c)(1))**

The evaluation must consider all options the servicer makes available to borrowers in similar situations — the servicer cannot evaluate the borrower for only one option without considering others.

### Pending Application — Additional Foreclosure Prohibitions (§1024.41(g))

Even if a complete application is received fewer than 37 days before the scheduled foreclosure sale (and therefore the initial prohibition of §1024.41(f) does not apply), the servicer still may not:
- **Move for a foreclosure judgment** or **order of sale**
- **Conduct a foreclosure sale**

while a complete loss mitigation application is pending review and the borrower has not been notified of the decision, unless an exception applies. **(§1024.41(g))**

### Borrower's Appeal Right (§1024.41(h))

If the servicer denies a borrower's request for a **loan modification** (not other loss mitigation options), the borrower has the right to appeal the denial. The servicer must:
- Allow the borrower **14 days** from the date of the denial notice to appeal
- Evaluate the appeal and provide a written determination **within 30 days** of receiving the appeal **(§1024.41(h)(3)–(4))**

The appeal right applies only to loan modification denials, not to denials of short sales, deeds in lieu, or repayment plans.

### Foreclosure Sale Prohibition — Pending Appeal (§1024.41(h)(4))

If the borrower timely submits an appeal, the servicer may not conduct a foreclosure sale until:
- The servicer provides the borrower with the appeal determination; or
- 30 days after receiving the appeal, if no further extension applies

### Servicer's Foreclosure Options When Loss Mitigation Is Denied

Once the servicer has evaluated a complete application, provided the required denial notice, and the borrower's appeal rights are exhausted, the servicer may proceed with foreclosure. If the only loss mitigation option(s) were denied or the borrower rejected an offered option, the servicer may make the first foreclosure notice or filing even if more than 37 days remain before the sale. **(§1024.41(f)(2))**

## Timing Summary — Delinquency Lifecycle

| Day (from first delinquency) | Required action |
|------------------------------|----------------|
| Day 1 | Delinquency begins (first payment due and unpaid) |
| Day 36 | Live contact (or good-faith efforts) required |
| Day 36+ | Loss mitigation application may be submitted at any time |
| Day 45 | Written early intervention notice required |
| Within 5 days of application receipt | Written acknowledgment; complete/incomplete status |
| Day 37 before scheduled foreclosure sale | If complete application received, no first foreclosure notice/filing permitted |
| Within 30 days of complete application | Evaluate all options; provide written determination |
| 14 days after loan modification denial | Borrower may submit appeal |
| Within 30 days of appeal | Servicer must provide appeal determination |

## Key Points for Agents

- **The 60-day adverse credit reporting freeze is automatic upon receipt of a notice of error.** The servicer cannot report adverse credit on any payment covered by an error notice for 60 days — even if the error investigation has not yet concluded and even if the error is ultimately not confirmed. **(§1024.35(g)(3))**
- **Error categories 9 and 10 make foreclosure-related §1024.41 violations independently actionable as errors.** Conducting a foreclosure notice or sale in violation of §1024.41 is itself a defined error under §1024.35, triggering the full error resolution timeline. A single foreclosure violation can generate both a §1024.41 claim and a separate §1024.35 error resolution claim. **(§1024.35(b)(9)–(10))**
- **The 37-day dual tracking bright line applies to the first foreclosure notice or filing — not first contact with an attorney.** Engaging foreclosure counsel before a complete application is received does not trigger the prohibition; making the first required legal notice or filing does. **(§1024.41(f)(1))**
- **"Complete" application is defined by the servicer's own standards, but the servicer must exercise reasonable diligence to obtain documents.** A servicer cannot avoid the dual tracking prohibition by maintaining an incomplete application through passive inaction. The diligence obligation runs throughout the application process. **(§1024.41(b)(1))**
- **The appeal right applies only to loan modification denials.** If the servicer denies a short sale or deed in lieu but the only modification the servicer offers is also denied, only the modification denial triggers the 14-day appeal window. The borrower has no comparable appeal right for non-modification loss mitigation options. **(§1024.41(h))**
