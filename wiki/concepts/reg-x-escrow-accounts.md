# Regulation X — Escrow Account Limits and Requirements

## Overview

Section 1024.17 governs escrow accounts maintained by servicers for the payment of taxes, insurance, and other charges related to federally related mortgage loans. The rule establishes quantitative caps on the amount a servicer may hold in escrow, mandates aggregate accounting as the computation method, sets deadlines for annual analysis and statement delivery, and prescribes how surpluses, shortages, and deficiencies must be handled.

The escrow account rules apply to all servicers of federally related mortgage loans, with no small servicer exemption. **(§1024.17)**

---

## Escrow Account Limits — Cushion Cap

### Maximum Cushion

At any point during the life of the escrow account, the servicer may not maintain an escrow balance that exceeds the amount necessary to pay charges when due, plus a cushion of no more than **1/6 of the estimated total annual disbursements** from the account. **(§1024.17(c)(1))**

**Practical effect:** 1/6 of annual disbursements is approximately **2 months of escrow payments** (annual disbursements ÷ 6 = 2/12 of annual disbursements = ~2 months). A servicer collecting for a $3,600 annual tax bill ($300/month) may hold a cushion of up to $600 (1/6 of $3,600).

**No pre-accrual:** The servicer may not collect amounts from the borrower before the amounts are needed to pay charges. Pre-accrual of escrow funds ahead of their disbursement date is prohibited. **(§1024.17(c)(2))**

**Permissible reductions:** A servicer may require a smaller cushion or no cushion at all — the cap is a maximum, not a required amount.

---

## Aggregate Accounting — Mandatory Method (§1024.17(d))

### The Aggregate Accounting Requirement

Servicers must use **aggregate accounting** when computing escrow account balances. Under aggregate accounting, the escrow account is treated as a single pool — all anticipated disbursements (taxes, insurance, HOA fees, etc.) are totalled together, and the required escrow balance is computed based on the aggregate projected disbursements, not on each individual item separately. **(§1024.17(d))**

### Why Aggregate Accounting Matters

Under single-item accounting (which is prohibited), each escrow item would be analysed independently. Aggregate accounting allows the servicer to offset the timing of different disbursements against each other — if a tax disbursement falls in month 3 and an insurance disbursement falls in month 9, the aggregate account may run low before month 3 but recover before month 9, and the servicer may not use the low pre-tax period to justify a larger cushion.

Appendix E to Part 1024 provides the required aggregate accounting computation methodology and worked examples. **(§1024.17(d); Appendix E)**

## Initial Escrow Statement (§1024.17(g))

At or before settlement (or within **45 days** of settlement for loans with escrow accounts established after settlement), the servicer must deliver to the borrower an **initial escrow account statement**. The statement must include:

- The amount of the borrower's monthly payment
- The portion going to the escrow account
- Each anticipated disbursement from the escrow account during the first 12-month period
- The anticipated disbursement dates
- The cushion amount
- The starting balance required

The initial escrow statement serves as the computation baseline for the first escrow analysis period. **(§1024.17(g))**

## Annual Escrow Account Analysis (§1024.17(c)(3))

### Timing of Analysis

Servicers must conduct an **annual escrow account analysis** at the conclusion of the escrow account computation year (the 12-month period used for escrow calculations). The analysis determines whether the escrow account is in surplus, shortage, or deficiency. **(§1024.17(c)(3))**

### Annual Escrow Statement Delivery

The servicer must deliver the **annual escrow account statement** to the borrower **within 30 days** of the end of the computation year. The statement must include:

- The amount of the borrower's current monthly payment
- The amount received from and deposited to the escrow account during the year
- Each disbursement made from the account during the year, with dates
- The balance in the account at the end of the year
- A projection of the next year's activity
- A statement of the new monthly payment **(§1024.17(i))**

**Delinquency exception:** If the borrower is **more than 30 days delinquent**, the servicer is not required to provide the annual escrow account statement. This exception does not reduce the servicer's obligation to conduct the underlying analysis. **(§1024.17(i)(2))**

---

## Surplus, Shortage, and Deficiency (§1024.17(f))

### Surplus

A **surplus** exists when the projected lowest monthly balance in the escrow account exceeds the permissible cushion by **$50 or more**. If a surplus exists after the annual analysis:

- The servicer must **refund the surplus amount to the borrower within 30 days** of the analysis **(§1024.17(f)(2)(i))**

If the surplus is less than $50, the servicer may either refund the amount or credit it against future escrow payments. There is no requirement to refund surpluses under $50.

A borrower who is **more than 30 days delinquent** when the surplus is identified is not entitled to the surplus refund — the servicer may apply the surplus to the delinquency. **(§1024.17(f)(2)(ii))**

### Shortage

A **shortage** is the amount by which the current escrow account balance falls below the required balance at a given point in the computation year. If the annual analysis identifies a shortage of less than 1 month's escrow payment, the servicer need not require repayment. If the shortage equals or exceeds 1 month's escrow payment:

- The servicer must allow the borrower to **repay the shortage over at least 12 months** by adding a pro-rated amount to each monthly payment **(§1024.17(f)(3))**

The shortage spread must be at least 12 months — the servicer cannot demand immediate lump-sum payment of a shortage. This protects borrowers from sudden large escrow demands.

### Deficiency

A **deficiency** is the amount by which the escrow account has a negative balance because the servicer paid out more than it collected. Deficiencies arise when taxes or insurance bills are higher than projected or when the escrow account starts with insufficient funds. The servicer may:

- Collect the deficiency over **at least 2 months** if the deficiency is less than 1 month's escrow payment; or
- Collect the deficiency over **at least 12 months** if it equals or exceeds 1 month's escrow payment **(§1024.17(f)(4))**

## Delivery of Disclosures

| Disclosure | Timing |
|------------|--------|
| Initial escrow account statement | At or within 45 days of settlement |
| Annual escrow account statement | Within 30 days of end of computation year |
| Surplus refund | Within 30 days of annual analysis |
| Shortage repayment plan | Over at least 12 months from analysis |

## Key Points for Agents

- **The cushion cap is 1/6 of estimated total annual disbursements — approximately 2 months of escrow payments.** A servicer that holds more than this amount in escrow is in violation of §1024.17, regardless of the reason for the excess. **(§1024.17(c)(1))**
- **Aggregate accounting is mandatory — single-item accounting is prohibited.** The escrow account must be modelled as a single pool. Servicers that compute escrow requirements item by item (one computation for taxes, another for insurance) are using a non-compliant method. **(§1024.17(d))**
- **Surpluses of $50 or more must be refunded within 30 days.** Retaining a surplus above the $50 threshold after the annual analysis is a violation. The 30-day refund clock runs from the completion of the analysis, not from the end of the computation year. **(§1024.17(f)(2)(i))**
- **Shortage repayment must be spread over at least 12 months.** The servicer cannot require a lump-sum repayment of an escrow shortage. The minimum repayment period protects borrowers from unaffordable payment spikes when actual disbursements exceed projections. **(§1024.17(f)(3))**
- **No annual statement is required for borrowers more than 30 days delinquent.** The delinquency exception suspends the annual statement obligation but does not eliminate the underlying analysis requirement. The analysis must still be conducted; delivery is simply not required. **(§1024.17(i)(2))**
