# Regulation V — Risk-Based Pricing Notices (12 CFR Part 1022, Subpart H)

## Overview

Subpart H (§§1022.70–1022.75) implements FCRA §615(h), which requires persons who use consumer reports in connection with credit applications — and who grant credit on materially less favourable terms than the most favourable terms they offer — to notify the consumer. The underlying purpose is to alert consumers that information in their consumer report caused them to receive worse credit terms than other consumers and to prompt them to review their credit files for errors.

**Business credit excluded:** Subpart H applies only to credit extended primarily for personal, family, or household purposes. Applications for or grants of credit primarily for a business purpose are entirely excluded. **(§1022.70(a)(2))**

---

## Who Must Provide a Risk-Based Pricing Notice (§1022.72(a))

A person must provide a risk-based pricing notice if it both:
1. **Uses a consumer report** in connection with an application for, or a grant, extension, or other provision of, credit that is primarily for personal, family, or household purposes; and
2. **Based in whole or in part on the consumer report**, grants, extends, or otherwise provides credit to the consumer on **material terms that are materially less favourable** than the most favourable material terms available to a substantial proportion of consumers from or through that person.

**Account review:** The obligation also applies when a person uses a consumer report in connection with a review of an existing account and, based in whole or in part on that review, increases the annual percentage rate (or, for credit cards, the purchase APR). **(§1022.72(d))**

---

## Definitions (§1022.71)

**Material terms:** The primary pricing dimension for each credit type:
- **Open-end credit plans:** The APR required to be disclosed under 12 CFR 1026.6(a)(1)(ii) or 1026.6(b)(2)(i), excluding temporary initial rates lower than the ongoing rate and penalty rates triggered by specific events; any fixed APR
- **Credit cards (purchase APR):** The purchase APR — the APR that applies to purchases and has the most significant financial impact on consumers
- **Closed-end credit:** The APR required to be disclosed under 12 CFR 1026.17(c) and 1026.18(e)
- **Credit without an APR:** The financial term that varies based on a consumer report and has the most significant financial impact on consumers — e.g., a deposit required for a telephone utility account, or an annual membership fee for a charge card

**Materially less favourable:** The terms granted differ from the most favourable terms granted to a substantial proportion of consumers such that the cost of credit to the consumer would be significantly greater. Factors include: the type of credit product, the term of the credit extension, and the extent of the difference between the material terms offered to the two consumers. **(§1022.71(o))**

---

## Determining Which Consumers Receive a Notice (§1022.72(b))

Rather than requiring case-by-case comparison, the regulation provides two safe-harbour determination methods:

### Method 1 — Credit Score Proxy Method (§1022.72(b)(1))

A person that sets material credit terms based in whole or in part on a credit score may:

1. **Identify the "cutoff score"** — the score below which approximately 40% of consumers receive higher scores and approximately 60% receive lower scores. In other words, the score at or above which the most favourable terms are offered.
2. **Provide a risk-based pricing notice** to every consumer whose credit score is below the cutoff score.

**Cutoff score recalculation:** Must be recalculated at least every **2 years** using a representative sample of the consumers to whom the person grants, extends, or provides credit for that specific type of credit product. New entrants and acquirers may initially use market research or third-party data; they must transition to own-consumer data within one year of beginning to grant credit if they accumulate sufficient data. **(§1022.72(b)(1)(iii))**

**Multiple credit scores:** If a person uses two or more credit scores and consistently uses the low (or high, or median, or average) score to set terms, it must use the same method to determine the cutoff. A consistent method must be used; ad hoc selection of different scores in different decisions requires a reasonable means of determining the cutoff (e.g., consistently using any of the methods the person regularly uses). **(§1022.72(b)(1)(iv))**

**Credit score not available:** If a consumer report is obtained but no credit score is available for the consumer, the person must assume the consumer receives materially less favourable terms and must provide a risk-based pricing notice. **(§1022.72(b)(1)(iv))**

### Method 2 — Tiered Pricing Method (§1022.72(b)(2))

A person that places consumers into a discrete number of pricing tiers based on a consumer report may:

- **4 or fewer pricing tiers:** Provide a risk-based pricing notice to each consumer who does not qualify for the top tier (i.e., the lowest-priced tier)
- **5 or more pricing tiers:** Provide a risk-based pricing notice to each consumer who does not qualify for the top two tiers **and** any other tiers that, together with the top tiers, comprise no less than the top 30% but no more than the top 40% of the total number of tiers

*Example with 9 tiers:* The top 3 tiers comprise the top 30–40%. Risk-based pricing notices go to consumers in the bottom 6 tiers.

### Credit Card Issuer Alternative (§1022.72(c))

A credit card issuer may satisfy the requirements by providing a risk-based pricing notice when: (i) the consumer applies under an application program or solicitation, more than one purchase APR may apply, and (ii) the card issuer provides the consumer with a card at a purchase APR higher than the lowest purchase APR available under that program or solicitation. A card issuer providing the lowest available APR for a specific solicitation need not provide a notice even if other solicitations offer lower APRs.

## Content of the Risk-Based Pricing Notice (§1022.73(a))

### For New Credit (§1022.72(a) or (c))

The notice must include:

| Item | Content |
|------|---------|
| i | Statement that a consumer report includes information about the consumer's credit history and the type of information included |
| ii | Statement that the terms offered have been set based on information from a consumer report |
| iii | Statement that the terms may be less favourable than terms offered to consumers with better credit histories |
| iv | Statement that the consumer is encouraged to verify the accuracy of the report and has the right to dispute inaccurate information |
| v | Identity of each CRA that furnished a consumer report used in the credit decision |
| vi | Statement that Federal law gives the consumer the right to obtain a copy of the consumer report from the CRA(s) identified, **without charge**, for 60 days after receipt of the notice |
| vii | How to obtain a consumer report from the identified CRA(s), including a toll-free telephone number where applicable |
| viii | Direction to the Bureau's website for more information about consumer reports |
| ix | **If a credit score was used in setting the material terms:** (A) statement explaining what a credit score is; (B) the credit score used; (C) the range of possible scores under the model; (D) all key factors that adversely affected the score (no more than 4 factors, or 5 if inquiries is one of the key factors); (E) the date the score was created; (F) the name of the CRA or other person that provided the score |

### For Account Review (§1022.72(d))

The account review notice contains the same 9 categories, but framed around: the account review (not an application); the APR increase; and the credit score used in increasing the APR (if applicable).

**Model forms:**
- New credit: Appendix H-1 (without credit score) or H-6 (with credit score)
- Account review: Appendix H-2 (without credit score) or H-7 (with credit score)
- Use of the applicable model form constitutes compliance with the content requirements

## Form and Timing (§1022.73(b)–(c))

**Form:** Clear and conspicuous; oral, written, or electronic.

**Timing for new closed-end credit:** Before consummation of the transaction, but not earlier than the time the decision to approve the application is communicated to the consumer.

**Timing for new open-end credit:** Before the first transaction is made under the plan, but not earlier than the time the decision to approve is communicated.

**Timing for account review:** At the time the decision to increase the APR is communicated, or — if no notice is given before the effective date — no later than **5 days** after the effective date.

**Contemporaneous purchase credit (open-end):** For credit granted at point of sale to finance a contemporaneous purchase, the notice may be provided at the earlier of: (i) the time of the first mailing after the decision to approve; or (ii) within 30 days after the decision to approve. **(§1022.73(c)(3))**

**Auto dealer transactions:** If a bank or finance company arranges for an auto dealer to provide the notice on its behalf (pursuant to §1022.73(c)(2)), the bank/finance company satisfies its obligation if the dealer provides the applicable notice within the required time periods.

## Exceptions — Credit Score Disclosure Alternatives (§1022.74)

These exceptions allow a person to avoid the risk-based pricing notice entirely by providing a different, more informative notice:

### Exception 1 — Specific Terms Applied For and Granted (§1022.74(a))
If the consumer applied for and was granted the specific material terms the person specified (before obtaining the consumer report), no risk-based pricing notice is required. This exception only applies to a specific single term — a range of alternatives (e.g., "APR between 8–12%") does not qualify.

### Exception 2 — Adverse Action Notice (§1022.74(b))
If a person provides an adverse action notice under FCRA §615(a), no risk-based pricing notice is required. Adverse action eliminates the risk-based pricing notice obligation entirely.

### Exception 3 — Prescreened Solicitations (§1022.74(c))
If a person obtains a consumer report as part of a prescreened list under FCRA §604(c)(2) and uses it to make a firm offer of credit, no risk-based pricing notice is required — even if the firm offer is made on materially worse terms than offers made to consumers on other prescreened lists.

### Exception 4 — Residential Real Property Loans (§1022.74(d))
For credit secured by 1–4 units of residential real property, instead of a risk-based pricing notice, the person may provide a **credit score disclosure notice** to each consumer that contains the following (items A–I):
- (A) Description of a consumer report and credit history
- (B) Explanation of what a credit score is
- (C) Statement that the consumer's credit score can affect credit availability and cost
- (D) Information required under FCRA §609(g) (credit score, range, key factors, date, CRA name)
- (E) **Distribution of credit scores** — a bar graph with minimum 6 bars illustrating the distribution of scores under the same scoring model, using the same scale; or an equivalent clear and readily understandable graphical or written statement
- (F) Encouragement to verify accuracy and right to dispute
- (G) Federal right to free annual reports from each nationwide CRA
- (H) Contact information for the centralised source for free annual reports
- (I) Direction to the Bureau's website

**Form requirements:** Clear and conspicuous; provided with or on the FCRA §609(g) notice; segregated from other information; in writing in a form the consumer may keep.

**Timing:** At the time the §609(g) disclosure is provided, at/before consummation (closed-end) or before the first transaction (open-end).

**Model form:** Appendix H-3.

### Exception 5 — Other Consumer Credit Extensions (§1022.74(e))
For consumer credit not secured by residential real property, instead of a risk-based pricing notice, a person may provide a **credit score disclosure notice** containing items A–L:
- Same as the mortgage notice (items A–C above)
- (D) The current credit score or most recent credit score previously calculated for a purpose related to the extension of credit
- (E) The range of possible scores under the model
- (F) Distribution of credit scores (same bar graph/statement requirement — minimum 6 bars)
- (G) The date the credit score was created
- (H) The name of the CRA or other person that provided the credit score
- (I) Encouragement to verify accuracy and right to dispute
- (J) Federal right to free annual reports from each nationwide CRA
- (K) Contact information for the centralised source
- (L) Direction to the Bureau's website

**Form requirements:** Clear and conspicuous; segregated from other information; in writing in a form the consumer may keep.

**Timing:** As soon as reasonably practicable after the credit score is obtained, at/before consummation or before the first transaction.

**Model form:** Appendix H-4.

### Exception 6 — Credit Score Not Available (§1022.74(f))
If a person regularly obtains credit scores and provides score disclosure notices under exception 4 or 5, but a credit score is not available from the regular CRA for a specific consumer, the person may provide a **credit score not available notice** (9 items A–I), provided it does not obtain a score from another CRA. If a score is obtained from another source, exception 6 is unavailable and exception 5 must be used.

**Model form:** Appendix H-5.

## Rules of Construction (§1022.75)

**One notice per credit extension:** A consumer is entitled to no more than one risk-based pricing notice per grant, extension, or provision of credit (or one credit score disclosure notice under the applicable exception). **(§1022.75(a))**

**Multi-party transactions:** The **initial creditor** — the person to whom the credit obligation is initially payable — must provide the risk-based pricing notice (or satisfy the applicable exception). A purchaser or assignee of the credit contract is not required to provide a notice. **(§1022.75(b))**

**Multiple consumers (joint applications):** The person must provide a separate notice to each consumer. For risk-based pricing notices that include a credit score, each notice must contain only the credit score of the consumer to whom that notice is provided. If no credit score is included in the notice, a single notice addressed to both consumers at the same address satisfies the requirement. **(§1022.75(c))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Business credit is entirely excluded.** The risk-based pricing notice obligation does not apply to any extension of credit primarily for a business purpose — only personal/family/household credit. **(§1022.70(a)(2))**
- **The most common compliance path for consumer lenders is the credit score disclosure exception, not the risk-based pricing notice.** Providing a credit score disclosure notice under §1022.74(d) (mortgage) or §1022.74(e) (other consumer credit) eliminates the need to determine which consumers receive worse terms and provide individualised risk-based pricing notices. **(§1022.74(d)–(e))**
- **The credit score disclosure must include a score distribution bar chart.** The chart must use the same scoring model and scale as the score provided to the consumer and contain at least 6 bars. A CRA-provided graph that meets these requirements satisfies this element. **(§1022.74(d)(1)(ii)(E); §1022.74(e)(1)(ii)(F))**
- **When a credit score is used in setting terms, the risk-based pricing notice must include the actual score.** Including the score, range, key adverse factors (up to 4, or 5 if inquiries is among them), creation date, and CRA name is mandatory — not optional — when the score influenced the material terms. **(§1022.73(a)(1)(ix))**
- **Account review triggers the obligation when it results in an APR increase.** A periodic review that does not change terms does not trigger a notice. The notice timing for APR increases triggered by account review is at the time the increase is communicated or, at the latest, 5 days after the effective date. **(§1022.72(d); §1022.73(c)(1)(iii))**
- **The cutoff score for the credit score proxy method must be recalculated at least every 2 years.** Stale cutoff scores that no longer reflect the current distribution of the furnisher's approved consumers may cause the wrong consumers to receive (or not receive) notices. **(§1022.72(b)(1)(iii)(C))**
