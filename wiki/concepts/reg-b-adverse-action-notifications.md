# Regulation B — Adverse Action Notifications (§1002.9)

## What Is Adverse Action?

"Adverse action" has a specific legal definition under Reg B **(§1002.2(c))** that is broader than a simple credit denial:

**Adverse action includes:**
1. A **refusal to grant credit** in substantially the amount or on substantially the terms requested, unless the creditor makes a counteroffer and the applicant expressly accepts or uses the credit offered.
2. A **termination of an account** or an unfavourable change in the terms of an account that does not affect all or substantially all of a class of the creditor's accounts.
3. A **refusal to increase** the amount of credit available to an applicant who has made an application for an increase.

**Adverse action does NOT include:**
- A change in terms expressly agreed to by the applicant
- Any action or forbearance relating to an account taken in connection with inactivity, default, or delinquency
- A refusal or failure to authorise an account transaction at point of sale or loan (except when equivalent to a termination or unfavourable change)
- A refusal to extend credit because applicable law prohibits the creditor from extending the credit requested
- A refusal to extend credit because the creditor does not offer that type of credit

**Counteroffer trap:** If the creditor makes a counteroffer (different amount or terms) and the applicant does not expressly accept or use the credit offered, the original refusal becomes adverse action and triggers notification obligations. The 90-day clock runs from the date the creditor notifies the applicant of the counteroffer. **(§1002.9(a)(1)(iv))**

---

## Timing Requirements

A creditor must notify an applicant of action taken within: **(§1002.9(a)(1))**

| Trigger | Deadline |
|---------|----------|
| Completed application — approval, counteroffer, or adverse action | **30 days** after receiving the completed application |
| Incomplete application — adverse action | **30 days** after taking adverse action |
| Existing account — adverse action | **30 days** after taking adverse action |
| Counteroffer — applicant does not expressly accept or use the credit | **90 days** after notifying the applicant of the counteroffer |

**"Completed application"** means the creditor has received all information it regularly obtains and considers in evaluating applications for the amount and type of credit requested — including credit reports, third-party approvals, and any additional information requested from the applicant. A creditor cannot artificially extend the timeline by delaying a decision on a substantially complete application. **(§1002.2(f))**

---

## Content of Adverse Action Notice

When adverse action is taken, the notification must contain all of the following **(§1002.9(a)(2))**:

1. **Statement of the action taken**
2. **ECOA notice** — a statement substantially similar to the prescribed model language:

> *"The Federal Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits creditors from discriminating against credit applicants on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the capacity to enter into a binding contract); because all or part of the applicant's income derives from any public assistance program; or because the applicant has in good faith exercised any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act. The Federal agency that administers compliance with this law concerning this creditor is [name and address]."*

3. **Either:**
   - **(i) A statement of specific reasons** for the action taken; **or**
   - **(ii) Disclosure of the right** to request a statement of specific reasons within 60 days, if the statement is requested within 60 days of the creditor's notification. This disclosure must include the name, address, and telephone number of the person or office from which reasons can be obtained. If the creditor offers oral delivery of reasons, the applicant must also be informed of the right to have the reasons confirmed in writing within 30 days.

**Which agency to list in the ECOA notice:** Appendix A to Part 1002 lists the specific federal agency to be named for each type of creditor. For banks with total assets over $10 billion and their affiliates: the CFPB. For national banks: the OCC. For non-bank creditors not covered by specific agencies: the FTC.

---

## The Specific Reasons Requirement — The Most Litigated Element

The statement of specific reasons must be **specific** and must **indicate the principal reason(s)** for the adverse action. **(§1002.9(b)(2))**

**Explicitly insufficient reasons (named in the regulation):**
- "The adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies"
- "The applicant failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system"

**What "specific" means in practice:**
- The reasons must identify the actual factors that drove the decision — not a characterisation of the decision process.
- For a credit scoring system: the reason codes must identify the specific factors the scoring model weighted most heavily against the applicant (e.g., "too many recent inquiries," "delinquent past or present credit obligations," "length of credit history too short").
- For a judgmental system: the reasons must reflect the specific aspects of the applicant's application that the creditor found unfavourable.
- Combination systems (score + judgmental override): reasons must reflect both the score factors and any additional judgmental factors applied.

**Number of reasons:** There is no fixed maximum, but the regulation requires the principal reasons. Providing every possible adverse reason is not required — only those that actually drove the decision.

**"Right to reasons" alternative:** If the creditor chooses to disclose the right to request reasons rather than providing them upfront, the applicant must request them within 60 days of the creditor's notification. The creditor must then provide the statement within a reasonable time. If the creditor offers oral reasons, it must also provide written confirmation within 30 days of a written request from the applicant.

## Incomplete Applications

A creditor has two options when it receives an application that is incomplete regarding matters the applicant can complete **(§1002.9(c))**:

**Option 1 — Take action:** Notify the applicant of the action taken (which may itself be adverse action) in accordance with §1002.9(a).

**Option 2 — Notice of incompleteness:** Send a written notice to the applicant specifying the information needed, designating a reasonable time for the applicant to provide the information, and informing the applicant that failure to provide the information will result in no further consideration. The creditor has no further obligation under this option if the applicant fails to respond within the designated time.

**Oral request alternative:** The creditor may inform the applicant orally of the need for additional information. If the application remains incomplete, the creditor must then send a written notice of incompleteness.

**Time clock for incomplete applications:** The 30-day adverse action clock begins running when the creditor decides to deny the application based on incompleteness. The clock does not run from the date the application was received if the creditor is waiting for additional information.

## Business Credit — Modified Rules

**Small business credit (gross revenues ≤$1 million in preceding fiscal year), trade credit, credit incident to a factoring agreement, and similar types of business credit (§1002.9(a)(3)(i)):**

- The statement of the action taken **may be given orally or in writing** when adverse action is taken.
- Disclosure of the applicant's right to a statement of reasons **may be given at the time of application** rather than at the time adverse action is taken, provided the disclosure contains the required information.
- For telephone applications, an oral statement of the action taken and the right to reasons satisfies the notification requirements.

**Large business credit (gross revenues exceeding $1 million in preceding fiscal year) and extensions of trade credit (§1002.9(a)(3)(ii)):**

- The creditor must notify the applicant within a **reasonable time** orally or in writing of the action taken.
- The creditor must provide a **written statement of reasons** for the adverse action only if the applicant makes a **written request** within **60 days** of the creditor's notification.

---

## Small-Volume Creditors

A creditor that did not receive more than **150 applications** during the preceding calendar year may satisfy the notification requirements (including statements of specific reasons) through **oral notifications**. **(§1002.9(d))**

This applies to the entire §1002.9 notification framework — not just the delivery method. A small-volume creditor may orally disclose the ECOA notice, the action taken, and the statement of specific reasons.

---

## Withdrawal of Applications and Multiple Creditors

**Withdrawal by applicant:** When an applicant submits an application and the parties contemplate that the applicant will enquire about its status, if the creditor approves the application and the applicant has not enquired within **30 days** after applying, the creditor may treat the application as withdrawn and is not required to notify the applicant. **(§1002.9(e))**

**Multiple creditors:** When an application is submitted to more than one creditor and credit is offered by one, the other creditors taking adverse action must each comply with §1002.9. A notice given by a third party must disclose the identity of each creditor on whose behalf the notice is given. **(§1002.9(g))**

## Model Forms

Appendix C to Part 1002 provides sample notification forms. Using these model forms, or substantially similar language, constitutes compliance with the content requirements of §1002.9:

- **Form C-1:** Sample notice of action taken and statement of reasons (credit scoring system)
- **Form C-2:** Sample notice of action taken and statement of reasons (credit scoring system — with right to reasons alternative)
- **Form C-3:** Sample notice of action taken and statement of reasons (judgmental system)
- **Form C-4:** Sample notice of action taken and statement of reasons (combined system)
- **Form C-5:** Sample notice of action taken (approved application or credit offered)
- **Form C-6:** Sample notice of incomplete application

Creditors may modify the model forms to reflect their specific products and reasons, provided the substance of the required disclosures is preserved.

## Key Points for Agents

- **The 30-day clock runs from the completed application, not the application date.** If a creditor requests additional information, the clock restarts when the completed application is received — but the creditor cannot artificially stall. **(§1002.2(f); §1002.9(a)(1)(i))**
- **A counteroffer that is not accepted becomes adverse action.** If a creditor offers $8,000 when the applicant applied for $10,000, and the applicant does not accept, the creditor has 90 days from the counteroffer notification to treat the matter as closed. If no acceptance occurs, adverse action obligations apply. **(§1002.9(a)(1)(iv))**
- **Reason codes from credit scoring systems must be model-specific, not generic.** Saying "insufficient credit score" names the result, not the cause. The CFPB and courts have consistently held that the reasons must identify the factors — "high utilisation ratio," "insufficient payment history on revolving accounts" — not the conclusion. **(§1002.9(b)(2))**
- **The ECOA notice must name the correct federal agency.** Using the wrong agency in the notice (e.g., a non-bank fintech listing the OCC rather than the FTC or CFPB) is a technical violation of §1002.9(a)(2). See Appendix A for the authoritative agency list. **(§1002.9(b)(1); Appendix A)**
- **Incomplete application notices must designate a specific reasonable time.** A vague notice saying "please provide the missing information" is not compliant. The notice must specify what is needed and give a deadline. **(§1002.9(c)(2))**
- **Business credit adverse action obligations scale with revenue.** The modified rules for business credit are not a blanket exemption — they are a reduced-formality regime. The obligation to notify, disclose ECOA rights, and provide reasons (on request) still applies. **(§1002.9(a)(3))**
