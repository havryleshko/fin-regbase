# CFPB — Regulatory Framework Overview

## Overview

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) is the primary US federal regulator for consumer financial products and services. Created by Title X of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (2010) and operational from 21 July 2011, it is the closest US equivalent to the FCA's B2C supervisory function — though structurally and jurisdictionally distinct.

The CFPB's statutory purpose is to ensure consumer financial markets are fair, transparent, and competitive, and to protect consumers from unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices. **(Dodd-Frank §1021; 12 USC 5511)**

## Supervisory Jurisdiction

The CFPB's scope is deliberately broader than the banking regulators it was designed to complement. It supervises three categories of entity:

### Non-Depository Covered Persons (§1024)
Any person offering or providing consumer financial products or services not subject to primary prudential supervision. This includes:
- Fintechs (payments, lending, BNPL, digital wallets)
- Mortgage originators and servicers
- Payday lenders and short-term lenders
- Private student loan originators
- Debt collectors and debt buyers
- Consumer reporting agencies (larger participants)
- Money transfer services

The CFPB has authority to define "larger participants" in markets by rule, expanding its supervisory reach over non-bank sectors.

### Large Depository Institutions (§1025)
Federally insured banks, thrifts, and credit unions with total assets exceeding $10 billion, and their affiliates. Smaller depository institutions remain supervised by the prudential regulators (OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, NCUA) for consumer compliance, though the CFPB retains enforcement authority.

### Service Providers (§1026)
Any service provider to a covered person — regardless of the service provider's own regulatory status. Outsourcing does not export compliance obligations: the covered person remains fully responsible for the service provider's compliance with Federal consumer financial law.

## Two-Layer Compliance Structure

All supervised entities face a two-layer compliance obligation:

### Layer 1 — UDAAP (Catch-All Standard)
Section 1036 of Dodd-Frank (12 USC 5536) prohibits any covered person or service provider from engaging in unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices (UDAAPs) in connection with any consumer financial product or service. UDAAP applies to conduct not reached by any enumerated law and is assessed in every CFPB examination regardless of product. See [[cfpb-udaap]] for full standards.

### Layer 2 — Enumerated Consumer Laws
Specific federal statutes administered or enforced by the CFPB, each implemented by a Regulation promulgated under Title 12 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR). Key statutes:

| Statute | Regulation | CFR Part | Subject matter |
|---------|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Truth in Lending Act (TILA) | Reg Z | 12 CFR 1026 | Credit cost disclosure, APR, right of rescission |
| Consumer Leasing Act (CLA) | Reg M | 12 CFR 1013 | Consumer lease disclosures |
| Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) | Reg E | 12 CFR 1005 | EFTs, debit card overdraft opt-in, prepaid accounts, remittance transfers |
| Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) | Reg B | 12 CFR 1002 | Credit discrimination prohibition |
| Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) | Reg V | 12 CFR 1022 | Consumer reports, adverse action notices, risk-based pricing notices |
| Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) | — | — | Debt collection conduct |
| Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) | Reg P | 12 CFR 1016 | Consumer financial privacy, information sharing, opt-out |
| Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) | Reg C | 12 CFR 1003 | Mortgage lending data reporting |
| Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) | Reg X | 12 CFR 1024 | Mortgage settlement services |

Both layers apply simultaneously. A practice can be fully compliant with all enumerated laws and still be a UDAAP violation. Conversely, a UDAAP violation may also constitute a specific enumerated law violation, compounding the finding.

## Supervision Principles

The CFPB operates under three explicitly stated supervision principles:

**1. Consumer risk focus.** Examinations assess risks to consumers, not institutional safety and soundness. The CFPB may collect information about potential risks to consumers even where no specific legal standard applies, including potential UDAAPs.

**2. Data-driven.** Examination scheduling, scope, and depth are driven by consumer complaint data, market intelligence, prior supervisory findings, and the entity's size and market position.

**3. Consistency (bank/non-bank parity).** The same compliance standards apply to a bank and a non-bank offering equivalent products. A fintech offering a buy-now-pay-later product faces the same CFPB requirements as a bank offering a comparable credit product.

## Examination Scheduling

The CFPB does not operate on a fixed periodic examination cycle. Examinations are risk-scheduled based on:
- Consumer complaint volume and nature
- Prior examination findings and MRA status
- Market size, market share, and product risk
- Supervisory intelligence from other agencies

**Full-scope examinations** assess the entity's complete compliance management system and all product lines. **Targeted reviews** address a specific product, practice, or prior finding without a full-scope examination.

## Enforcement Authority

The CFPB has civil enforcement authority only. It cannot bring criminal prosecutions. Civil enforcement actions may seek any or all of the following remedies:
- **Rescission or reformation** of consumer contracts
- **Restitution** to harmed consumers
- **Disgorgement** of ill-gotten gains
- **Civil monetary penalties** (up to $1m per day for knowing violations under 12 USC 5565)
- **Public notification** of violations and enforcement actions
- **Activity limitations** — restrictions on the entity's products, services, or operations

Enforcement referrals to the Department of Justice for criminal matters (e.g., fraud) occur separately via coordination, not CFPB enforcement authority.

## Relationship to Other US Regulators

The CFPB occupies one layer of a fragmented multi-agency regulatory landscape:

| Agency | Primary function relevant to fintechs |
|--------|--------------------------------------|
| CFPB | B2C consumer protection; UDAAP; enumerated consumer laws |
| FinCEN | AML/CFT; Bank Secrecy Act; SAR reporting |
| OCC | National bank charters; preemption |
| FDIC / Federal Reserve | State bank and bank holding company supervision |
| SEC / FINRA | Investment products; broker-dealer regulation |
| FTC | Residual UDAP authority for non-banks not covered by CFPB |
| State regulators | Licensing; state consumer protection laws (concurrent with CFPB) |

Unlike the UK's FCA, no single US agency covers all of B2C, B2B, and investment regulation. A US fintech typically has regulatory relationships with the CFPB, FinCEN, one or more state regulators, and (depending on product) the SEC or OCC.

## Key Points for Agents

- **The CFPB's jurisdiction over fintechs is broad and expanding.** Any person offering consumer financial products or services is a potential "covered person" — no banking charter is required to fall within CFPB supervision. **(Dodd-Frank §1024)**
- **UDAAP + enumerated laws are complementary, not alternative.** Both apply in every examination. Compliance with all enumerated laws does not immunise against UDAAP findings.
- **Service providers are not a regulatory firewall.** The covered person remains fully liable for service provider compliance. The CFPB scrutinises service provider oversight as a standalone examination module. **(Dodd-Frank §1026)**
- **The CFPB has civil enforcement only.** Penalties can be severe (up to $1m/day for knowing violations) but criminal referrals require coordination with DOJ or state attorneys general.
- **US regulatory fragmentation is the baseline.** A fintech operating in the US will face CFPB (consumer protection), FinCEN (AML), state licensing requirements, and potentially SEC or OCC oversight simultaneously. There is no single-regulator equivalent of the FCA's FSMA authorisation gateway.
- **The three supervision principles operationalise the mandate.** Consumer risk focus, data-driven scheduling, and bank/non-bank parity are not aspirational — they are the stated basis on which examination scope, depth, and follow-up are determined.
