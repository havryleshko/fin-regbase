# CFPB — Bureau Structure and Independence

## Overview

Title X, Subtitle A (§§1011–1018) establishes the Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection as an independent bureau within the Federal Reserve System, defines its leadership structure, insulates it from congressional appropriations, and creates a structural safety-valve via Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC) oversight. These provisions are not merely organisational — they are the structural foundation that determines how much latitude the Bureau has to regulate without interference from other political actors.

---

## Establishment Within the Federal Reserve System (§1011)

The Bureau is established within the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. This is a deliberate architectural choice: the Bureau is not a standalone executive agency (like the FTC) and is not an independent commission with multi-member leadership (like the SEC). It is a single-Director bureau nested within the Federal Reserve's legal structure but operationally independent from it.

**What "within" does not mean:** The Federal Reserve has no authority to direct, manage, or control the Bureau's functions, budget, or decisions. The Board of Governors may not:
- Intervene in examination, supervisory, or enforcement proceedings
- Merge, consolidate, or transfer Bureau functions to another component of the Federal Reserve
- Limit or alter the Bureau's authority
- Prevent the Bureau from publishing any information it considers necessary **(§1012(c))**

The Bureau uses Federal Reserve facilities and services, including payroll and human resources, in exchange for reimbursement. This is an administrative convenience, not a supervisory relationship. **(§1012(b))**

## The Director (§§1011–1012)

### Appointment and Term

The Director is appointed by the President by and with the advice and consent of the Senate. The Director serves a single 5-year term. There is no limit on the number of terms. **(§1011(b))**

### Removal Protection

The President may remove the Director from office only for **inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office** — the traditional "for cause" removal standard applied to independent agency heads. The Director cannot be removed at will by a new administration. **(§1011(c))**

**Seila Law context:** The Supreme Court held in *Seila Law LLC v. CFPB* (2020) that the single-Director structure with for-cause removal protection was unconstitutional as applied. Congress subsequently ratified the Bureau's prior actions; the Director now serves subject to the President's removal authority under the Court's ruling, though the statutory text of §1011(c) was not amended. The practical independence of the Director therefore depends on current constitutional interpretation.

### Deputy Director

The Bureau has a Deputy Director, appointed by the Director to serve in an acting capacity if the Director is absent or the directorship is vacant. **(§1011(b)(5))**

### Executive Authority

The Director has executive authority over Bureau personnel, resources, and operations and is the "head" of the Bureau for purposes of federal administrative law. **(§1012(a))**

## Internal Offices (§1013)

Subtitle A requires the Director to establish the following offices within the Bureau:

| Office | Primary function |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Office of Fair Lending and Equal Opportunity** | Oversight and enforcement of ECOA and HMDA; coordination with DOJ and HUD on fair lending referrals |
| **Office of Financial Education** | Programs to improve understanding of financial products and services; partnerships with State and local governments and nonprofit organisations |
| **Office of Service Member Affairs** | Financial protection for active duty military, veterans, and their families; coordination with Department of Defense |
| **Office of Financial Protection for Older Americans** | Elder financial exploitation prevention; consumer education for consumers 62 and over |
| **Office of Financial Research** | This office is established at Treasury (not within the Bureau) but is closely coordinated with Bureau data-sharing activities |
| **Office of the Ombudsman** | Informal resolution of complaints from covered persons about the Bureau's supervisory or examination processes |

The Director may establish additional offices as needed. **(§1013(g))**

---

## Consumer Advisory Board (§1014)

The Director must establish a Consumer Advisory Board (CAB) to advise and consult with the Bureau on consumer financial products and services. Requirements:

- **Minimum size:** 6 members
- **Composition:** Members must have expertise in consumer protection, financial services, community development, fair lending, civil rights, consumer financial products for underserved communities, or representatives of insured depository institutions, non-depository covered persons, or other market participants **(§1014(b))**
- **Meetings:** The Director must meet with the CAB at least **twice per year** **(§1014(c))**
- **Reporting:** The Bureau must publish the minutes of each CAB meeting

The CAB is advisory only — it cannot block or veto Bureau rules.

## Funding Mechanism (§1017)

### Federal Reserve Transfer (Not Appropriations)

The Bureau is funded by transfers from the combined earnings of the Federal Reserve System, not by congressional appropriations. The Bureau Director submits a budget to the Federal Reserve Board, which transfers the requested amount subject to caps:

| Year of operation | Maximum transfer (% of Fed operating expenses) |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Year 1 | 10% |
| Year 2 | 11% |
| Year 3 and thereafter | 12% |

The 12% cap applies to the Federal Reserve's *total operating expenses* for the prior year. This cap has consistently exceeded the Bureau's actual budget requests.

**Why this matters:** The Bureau cannot be defunded through the annual congressional appropriations process. Congress cannot reduce the Bureau's operational capacity by declining to appropriate funds. This insulation from the appropriations process was a deliberate legislative choice following the 2008 financial crisis, premised on the belief that consumer protection regulators funded through appropriations would be vulnerable to political pressure from the industries they regulate.

### Supplemental Appropriations

The Director may request additional appropriations from Congress, subject to congressional approval, if the capped transfer is insufficient. This is a one-way safety-valve: Congress can supplement but cannot restrict below the Fed transfer floor. **(§1017(e))**

### Fund Administration

The Bureau maintains a "Consumer Financial Protection Fund" at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Fund is available to the Bureau without fiscal year limitation (no year-end expiration). Interest and earnings on the Fund are available to the Bureau. **(§1017(a)–(b))**

## FSOC Override Authority (§1023)

The Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC) may stay and set aside Bureau rules under a narrow structural safety-valve:

### Petition Process

Any member agency of FSOC (excluding the Bureau itself) may petition FSOC to stay a Bureau rule within 10 days of its publication in the Federal Register. FSOC may issue the stay by a 2/3 vote of members, including the Chairperson (the Secretary of the Treasury). The stay lasts up to **90 days** while FSOC deliberates. **(§1023(a)–(b))**

### Standard for Setting Aside

FSOC may set aside (permanently rescind) the Bureau rule by a 2/3 vote, including the Chairperson, if FSOC determines that the rule:
1. Would put the safety and soundness of the United States banking system at serious risk; **or**
2. Would put the stability of the financial system of the United States at serious risk

FSOC's written determination must explain the findings and the legal and factual basis for the decision. **(§1023(c))**

### What the Override Cannot Do

FSOC cannot set aside a rule because:
- Member agencies disagree with the Bureau's policy choices
- The rule imposes compliance costs on regulated entities
- The rule reduces industry profitability
- The rule prioritises consumer protection over industry convenience

The override standard is limited to systemic risk to the banking system or financial stability. A rule that is commercially unpopular but does not threaten systemic stability is immune from FSOC override.

### Judicial Review

FSOC decisions to stay or set aside Bureau rules are subject to judicial review in the US Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit. **(§1023(e))**

## Designated Transfer Date and Predecessor Agency Functions (§§1061–1067)

The Secretary of the Treasury designated **July 21, 2011** as the "designated transfer date" — one year after enactment. On that date:

- All consumer financial protection functions of the predecessor agencies transferred to the Bureau
- All rules, regulations, guidance, and orders issued by predecessor agencies under enumerated consumer laws took effect as Bureau rules and remained in force until amended or repealed
- Pending proceedings, investigations, and litigation transferred to the Bureau
- Personnel performing transferred functions received offers of employment from the Bureau

Predecessor agencies that transferred functions: Federal Reserve Board (TILA, ECOA, HMDA, EFTA, RESPA, Truth in Savings, Homeowners Protection Act), OCC (national bank consumer compliance), OTS (thrift consumer compliance — OTS itself was subsequently abolished), FDIC (state-chartered insured bank consumer compliance), FTC (rulemaking authority for FCRA, FDCPA, and other enumerated consumer laws), NCUA (credit union consumer compliance), HUD (RESPA, SAFE Act, ILSFDA). **(§1061)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **The Bureau's independence is structural, not nominal.** The combination of for-cause removal protection (contested post-*Seila Law*), appropriations independence (Federal Reserve transfer), and FSOC's narrow override standard makes the CFPB significantly more insulated from political interference than a typical executive agency. The practical independence of the Director depends on whether the *Seila Law* ruling is applied. **(§§1011–1012, 1017, 1023)**
- **The Federal Reserve hosts but does not control the Bureau.** The Bureau uses Federal Reserve operational infrastructure but the Fed cannot direct, limit, or interfere with Bureau supervision or rulemaking. **(§1012(c))**
- **The FSOC veto requires 2/3 including the Treasury Secretary and applies only to systemic risk — not policy disagreement.** A Bureau rule that is unpopular with the banking industry but does not threaten systemic stability cannot be overridden through FSOC. **(§1023)**
- **The designated transfer date (July 21, 2011) means predecessor agency rules still govern.** Many CFPB-administered regulations (including early versions of Reg E, Reg B, Reg Z, and Reg V) were originally issued by the Federal Reserve Board or FTC and carry-forward as Bureau rules. The issuance history matters for understanding the authority behind any specific regulatory provision. **(§§1061–1066)**
- **The Bureau's internal offices (fair lending, service members, older Americans) have coordination and referral functions that affect fintechs.** The Office of Fair Lending and Equal Opportunity refers ECOA and HMDA violations to DOJ; the Office of Service Member Affairs monitors compliance with SCRA and MLA alongside CFPB-administered consumer laws. **(§1013)**
