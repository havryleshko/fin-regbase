# CFPB Compliance Management System (CMS) and CMR Framework

## Overview

The CFPB evaluates every supervised entity's compliance through a standardised Compliance Management Review (CMR). The CMR assesses the entity's overall Compliance Management System (CMS) — the totality of controls, oversight, and accountability structures the entity uses to ensure it complies with Federal consumer financial law.

A strong CMS does not merely respond to violations after they occur. It anticipates consumer risks, embeds compliance in product design and operations, and ensures the board and senior management have the information and authority needed to drive a compliance culture. A weak CMS — even in the absence of identified violations — is itself an adverse finding in the CC Rating System.

## The CC Rating System

The CFPB uses the FFIEC Uniform Consumer Compliance Rating System (CC Rating System). Ratings run from 1 (strongest) to 5 (most serious). Ratings are determined at the conclusion of an examination and reflect the entity's overall compliance posture.

| CC Rating | Status | Supervisory significance |
|-----------|--------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Satisfactory | Strong performance; any weaknesses are minor and self-correcting |
| 2 | Satisfactory | Adequate compliance; correctable weaknesses that management is addressing |
| 3 | Less than satisfactory | Developing weakness or inadequate systems; management action required |
| 4 | Less than satisfactory | Serious deficiencies requiring immediate corrective action |
| 5 | Less than satisfactory | Critical failures pervasive throughout; most serious supervisory concern |

Ratings are assessed across three categories:
1. **Board and Management Oversight**
2. **Compliance Program**
3. **Violations of Law and Consumer Harm**

A rating of 3 or above triggers enhanced supervisory attention and, for depository institutions, can affect regulatory approval processes. Ratings 4 and 5 typically result in formal enforcement action or at minimum an MRA with a specific remediation timeline.

## The Five CMR Modules

The CMR is structured as five sequential modules. Examiners complete all applicable modules and synthesise findings at Module 5.

---

### Module 1 — Board and Management Oversight

The board of directors (or equivalent governing body for non-banks) bears ultimate responsibility for the CMS. The CFPB assesses whether:
- The board has adopted and maintains written policies that provide for a strong CMS
- The board receives sufficient, accurate, and timely compliance reporting
- Senior management has established appropriate compliance accountability structures
- The compliance function operates independently of business lines (i.e., compliance/underwriting is separated from sales/production)
- Change management processes ensure that new products, systems, or processes are reviewed for compliance risk before launch

For non-bank fintechs without formal board structures, the CFPB looks for equivalent governance mechanisms — senior leadership accountability, compliance committee structures, and documented oversight.

---

### Module 2 — Compliance Program

The compliance program is assessed across four interconnected components:

#### (i) Policies and Procedures
Written policies and procedures must:
- Address all applicable Federal consumer financial laws and regulations
- Be current (updated when law or the entity's practices change)
- Be communicated to relevant staff
- Be accessible and operationally usable

#### (ii) Training
Compliance training must be:
- Provided to all relevant employees (including management and front-line staff)
- Role-appropriate — the depth and content must match the employee's function
- Kept current as laws or products change
- Documented (records of who was trained, when, and on what)

Training deficiency has direct statutory significance for UDAAP and FCRA liability: for certain statutory offences, untrained staff face a specific statutory defence, but the entity faces regulatory breach for failing to train.

#### (iii) Monitoring and Audit
An effective CMS requires two distinct functions:
- **Monitoring** (line-level, ongoing) — day-to-day assessment of whether compliance controls are working; typically performed by the compliance function
- **Audit** (independent, periodic) — independent review of whether the compliance program itself is effective; typically performed by internal audit or third-party auditors

The CFPB assesses whether monitoring and audit findings are escalated appropriately, acted upon, and tracked through to resolution. Monitoring that does not result in corrective action is treated as a system failure, not a mitigant.

#### (iv) Consumer Complaint Response
Consumer complaints are treated as a primary data source for identifying systemic compliance failures. The CFPB assesses whether:
- The entity has written complaint intake and response procedures
- Complaints are tracked and categorised by product, issue type, and root cause
- Complaint data is analysed for trends and escalated to management and the board
- Complaints are resolved accurately and within appropriate timeframes
- Complaint data informs product and process improvements

An entity with a high volume of unresolved complaints, or complaints that reveal a pattern of non-compliance, will receive adverse CMR findings even if no formal violation has been cited.

---

### Module 3 — Service Provider Oversight

Outsourcing compliance functions does not outsource compliance responsibility. The covered person remains fully liable for the service provider's compliance with Federal consumer financial law. The CFPB assesses the entity's service provider oversight programme, including:

- **Due diligence before engagement:** Does the entity assess the service provider's compliance management capabilities before entering the relationship?
- **Written contracts:** Do agreements clearly allocate compliance responsibilities and include audit rights?
- **Ongoing monitoring:** Does the entity monitor service provider performance against compliance standards on an ongoing basis?
- **Remediation:** Does the entity have procedures to address service provider compliance failures, including contract termination where necessary?

The CFPB may cite the covered person for a service provider's compliance failure if the covered person's oversight programme was inadequate. The covered person cannot use a contractual indemnity from the service provider as a defence to CFPB supervisory action.

---

### Module 4 — Violations of Law and Consumer Harm

When violations of Federal consumer financial law are identified, the CFPB assesses each violation using four factors:

| Factor | What examiners assess |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Root Cause** | Was the violation caused by a flawed policy, inadequate training, a systems error, poor management oversight, or deliberate conduct? |
| **Severity** | How significant is the harm to individual consumers? Does it affect financial standing, credit access, or safety? |
| **Duration** | How long has the violating practice been in place? A recent error is treated differently from a years-long systemic failure. |
| **Pervasiveness** | Is the violation isolated to one transaction, one product line, or systemic across the entity? |

These four factors together determine the weight of the finding. A violation that is systemic (high pervasiveness), long-running (high duration), caused by a deliberate policy decision (root cause), and results in significant financial harm (high severity) will receive the most serious treatment — typically an MRA and likely enforcement referral.

**Remediation of consumer harm** is a separate assessment from the violation itself. Even where a violation is found, the entity's proactive identification and remediation of consumer harm is a material mitigant in determining supervisory response.

---

### Module 5 — Examiner Conclusions and Wrap-Up

Module 5 synthesises findings across Modules 1–4 and produces:
- An aggregate CC Rating across the three rating categories
- Matters Requiring Attention (MRAs), where applicable
- Supervisory Recommendations (SRs), where applicable
- A written examination report

**MRAs** are legally significant findings directed to the board. They specify:
- The deficiency identified
- The required corrective action
- A timeframe for remediation

Failure to address MRAs within the agreed timeframe is treated as an escalation trigger and may result in an enforcement referral.

**Supervisory Recommendations** address CMS weaknesses that do not rise to the level of a violation. They are not legally enforceable but are tracked across examination cycles. An SR that persists unaddressed in a subsequent examination will typically be elevated to an MRA.

## CMR-IT — Technology Compliance

The CFPB applies a parallel CMR-IT module to assess technology and information systems compliance. The CMR-IT applies the same five-module structure to the entity's IT governance, data management, technology risk management, and systems controls relevant to consumer compliance. This is particularly relevant for fintechs where the product is delivered through technology and where compliance controls are embedded in (or absent from) the technology stack.

## Key Points for Agents

- **A weak CMS is an adverse finding in its own right.** The CFPB rates the CMS independently of whether specific violations are found. An entity with adequate enumerated law compliance but a structurally deficient CMS will receive a less-than-satisfactory CC Rating. **(CFPB CMR Module 5)**
- **The compliance program has four mandatory components.** Policies and procedures, training, monitoring/audit, and consumer complaint response must all be present and functioning. Weakness in any one component is an adverse Module 2 finding. **(CFPB CMR Module 2)**
- **Consumer complaints are examined as a compliance data source.** The CFPB treats complaint trends as indicators of systemic non-compliance. An entity that does not analyse and act on complaint data will receive adverse findings regardless of other compliance metrics. **(CFPB CMR Module 2(iv))**
- **Service provider oversight is a standalone examination module.** The inability to demonstrate active oversight of service providers — due diligence, monitoring, contractual protections, remediation — results in adverse Module 3 findings. The covered person cannot contract away CFPB liability. **(Dodd-Frank §1026; CFPB CMR Module 3)**
- **Violation assessment uses four factors.** Root Cause, Severity, Duration, and Pervasiveness determine whether a violation results in an MRA, enforcement referral, or a lighter supervisory response. Proactive consumer remediation is a mitigant in this assessment. **(CFPB CMR Module 4)**
- **MRAs are board-directed with enforceable timeframes.** Failure to remediate MRAs within agreed timeframes is an escalation trigger. MRAs are not negotiable once issued; they require a written remediation plan approved by the board. **(CFPB CMR Module 5)**
- **Monitoring and audit are distinct functions.** Monitoring is operational (ongoing, compliance-team led); audit is independent (periodic, internal audit or third party). The CFPB assesses both and distinguishes between entities that monitor but never act on findings and those with genuinely functioning control loops. **(CFPB CMR Module 2(iii))**
