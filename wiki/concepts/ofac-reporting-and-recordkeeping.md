# OFAC — Reporting Obligations and Recordkeeping

## Overview

31 CFR Part 501 Subpart B establishes three distinct reporting obligations for US persons subject to OFAC's jurisdiction: (1) records on demand, (2) reports of blocked property (initial, annual, and unblocking), and (3) reports of rejected transactions. These obligations apply across all OFAC sanctions programmes. Separately, §501.601 imposes a 10-year recordkeeping retention requirement — the longest in the US financial compliance framework.

---

## Recordkeeping — §501.601

Every person subject to OFAC's jurisdiction must retain **all records** relating to any transaction that is subject to OFAC's jurisdiction for **10 years** after the date of the transaction. This applies regardless of whether a violation occurred or was suspected. **(§501.601)**

This 10-year standard is distinct from and longer than the BSA's 5-year retention requirement (31 CFR §1010.430). An institution subject to both frameworks must apply the longer period to transactions within OFAC's jurisdiction.

---

## Records on Demand — §501.602

OFAC may, at any time and without notice, require any person subject to its jurisdiction to **furnish records** relating to any transaction subject to OFAC's jurisdiction. The demand may be issued as a subpoena or written administrative order. **(§501.602)**

Failure to furnish records constitutes an independent civil violation carrying a penalty of:
- Up to **$29,150** irrespective of whether any other violation is alleged
- Up to **$72,876** where OFAC has reason to believe the underlying transaction(s) is valued at greater than $500,000

These penalties may accrue as a **continuing violation** — assessed each month that the failure to furnish persists. OFAC may also seek judicial enforcement of the requirement to furnish information. **(Appendix A to Part 501, §IV.A)**

## Blocked Property Reports — §501.603

### Initial Report

When a US person **blocks** property (freezes funds, assets, or property in which a sanctioned person has an interest), it must file an initial report with OFAC within **10 business days** of the blocking. **(§501.603(b)(1))**

The initial report must include:
- The name of the blocked person
- The type and amount of property blocked
- The date of blocking
- The basis for blocking (the applicable OFAC programme and sanctions authority)
- Contact information for the reporting person

### Annual Report

All blocked property that remains blocked as of **June 30** of each year must be reported in an annual report filed with OFAC by **September 30** of that year. **(§501.603(b)(2))**

Annual reports cover all property currently blocked — not just property blocked since the previous report. This creates an ongoing annual compliance cycle.

### Unblocking Report

When blocked property is **released** (unblocked), the person must file a report with OFAC within **10 business days** of the release. **(§501.603(b)(3))**

### Filing Method

Reports are filed via the OFAC Reporting System (ORS) available on OFAC's website, or as otherwise directed by OFAC. The ORS assigns an identification number to each report; this number is required in any subsequent unblocking or mistaken-identity release request. **(§501.603; §501.806(d)(5))**

### Penalty for Late Filing

Late filing of blocked property reports carries specific penalties:
- Up to **$3,550** if filed within 30 days of the due date
- Up to **$7,104** if filed more than 30 days after the due date
- Additional **$1,422 per 30-day period** that the report remains unfiled, for up to **10 years**

**(Appendix A to Part 501, §IV.B)**

## Rejected Transaction Reports — §501.604

When a US person **rejects** (refuses to process) a transaction because it would violate OFAC regulations, the person must report the rejected transaction to OFAC within **10 business days** of the rejection. **(§501.604)**

### Blocked vs. Rejected — Key Distinction

| Situation | Outcome | Report type |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Transaction involves property in which a sanctioned person has an interest | **Block** — freeze and hold the property | Blocked property report (§501.603) |
| Transaction is prohibited but involves no property to freeze (e.g., prohibited service, wire transfer that cannot be processed) | **Reject** — refuse and return or decline to process | Rejected transaction report (§501.604) |

A common example: a wire transfer arriving at a US bank that must be rejected because the originator or beneficiary is on the SDN List but there is no property to freeze (the funds have not yet entered a US account). The bank rejects the transfer rather than blocks it.

---

## Litigation and Arbitration Reports — §501.605

Any US person who becomes party to litigation or arbitration proceedings concerning blocked property must promptly notify OFAC. **(§501.605)**

This ensures OFAC is aware of any legal proceedings that may affect its ability to enforce blocking orders or that may result in court-ordered disposition of blocked property.

---

## Applicability — §501.606

The reporting and recordkeeping requirements of Subpart B apply to **all OFAC sanctions programmes** administered under Chapter V of Title 31 CFR. Programme-specific parts (e.g., 31 CFR Parts 510, 515, 560, 594) may impose additional reporting requirements on top of these baseline obligations. **(§501.606)**

## Reporting Deadlines Summary

| Event | Obligation | Deadline |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Blocking of property | Initial blocked property report | 10 business days after blocking |
| Blocked property remaining frozen | Annual blocked property report | By September 30 (for property blocked as of June 30) |
| Release of blocked property | Unblocking report | 10 business days after release |
| Rejection of a prohibited transaction | Rejected transaction report | 10 business days after rejection |
| OFAC demand for records | Furnish records | Immediately upon demand |

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **10-year retention applies to all OFAC-covered transactions — not just blocked property.** Any transaction within OFAC's jurisdiction must have its records retained for 10 years from the transaction date, regardless of whether the transaction was blocked, rejected, or permitted. This is twice the BSA's 5-year standard. **(§501.601)**
- **Blocked and rejected transactions trigger different report types with the same 10-business-day deadline.** Blocking freezes property (report under §501.603); rejection refuses a transaction involving no freezable property (report under §501.604). Both reports are due within 10 business days. Confusing blocked with rejected — or failing to report either — is an independent civil violation. **(§§501.603–501.604)**
- **Annual blocked property reports are required by September 30 every year, covering all property blocked as of June 30.** This is not a one-time obligation — it recurs annually for as long as property remains blocked. Failure to file carries escalating penalties of $1,422 per 30-day period, up to 10 years. **(§501.603(b)(2); Appendix A §IV.B)**
- **Records on demand must be furnished to OFAC immediately and without notice.** OFAC's authority to compel production is broad — it covers any record relating to any transaction within OFAC's jurisdiction. Failure to comply is a continuing violation: penalties up to $29,150 per violation (up to $72,876 if underlying transaction exceeds $500K) accrue monthly. **(§501.602; Appendix A §IV.A)**
