# BSA — Suspicious Activity Reports for Banks

## Overview

§1020.320 establishes the bank-specific SAR requirements. Every bank must file a SAR for any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law or regulation that meets the monetary threshold. Banks may also voluntarily file SARs for transactions below the threshold or for transactions whose reporting is not required by §1020.320 but which the bank believes are relevant to a possible violation of any law or regulation. **(§1020.320(a)(1))**

---

## Filing Threshold — §1020.320(a)(2)

A transaction requires SAR reporting if it is **conducted or attempted, by, at, or through the bank**, involves or aggregates **at least $5,000 in funds or other assets**, and the bank **knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect** that:

- **(i)** The transaction **involves funds derived from illegal activities** or is intended or conducted in order to **hide or disguise funds or assets derived from illegal activities** (including, without limitation, the ownership, nature, source, location, or control of such funds or assets) as part of a plan to **violate or evade any Federal law or regulation** or to avoid any transaction reporting requirement under Federal law or regulation
- **(ii)** The transaction is **designed to evade any requirements of the BSA** or of any other regulations promulgated under the BSA
- **(iii)** The transaction **has no business or apparent lawful purpose** or is not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage, and the bank knows of **no reasonable explanation** for the transaction after examining the available facts, including the background and possible purpose of the transaction

---

## Filing Procedures — §1020.320(b)

### What to File

A suspicious transaction is reported by completing a **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR)** and collecting and maintaining supporting documentation. **(§1020.320(b)(1))**

### Where to File

SARs are filed with **FinCEN**, in a central location to be determined by FinCEN, as indicated in the instructions to the SAR form. **(§1020.320(b)(2))**

### When to File — §1020.320(b)(3)

| Circumstance | Deadline |
|-------------|---------|
| Suspect identified on date of detection | **30 calendar days** from the date of initial detection |
| No suspect identified on date of detection | **60 calendar days** from the date of initial detection to identify a suspect (still file within 60 days even if suspect is never identified) |
| Absolute outer limit | **No case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days** from the date of initial detection of a reportable transaction |

**Ongoing schemes — immediate telephone notification:** In situations involving violations requiring immediate attention — such as, for example, **ongoing money laundering schemes** — the bank shall **immediately notify, by telephone, an appropriate law enforcement authority** in addition to filing a timely SAR. **(§1020.320(b)(3))**

---

## Exceptions — §1020.320(c)

A bank is **not required** to file a SAR for:
- A **robbery or burglary** committed or attempted that is reported to appropriate law enforcement authorities
- **Lost, missing, counterfeit, or stolen securities** with respect to which the bank files a report pursuant to 17 CFR 240.17f-1 (the SEC's lost and stolen securities reporting rule)

**(§1020.320(c))**

---

## Retention of Records — §1020.320(d)

A bank must retain:
- A **copy of any SAR filed**
- The **original or business record equivalent of any supporting documentation** for a period of **5 years from the date of filing the SAR**

Supporting documentation must be:
- Identified and maintained by the bank as such
- Deemed to have been filed with the SAR
- Made available to FinCEN, any Federal, State, or local law enforcement agency, or any Federal regulatory authority that examines the bank for BSA compliance, or any State regulatory authority administering a State law requiring BSA compliance — **upon request** **(§1020.320(d))**

---

## Confidentiality of SARs — §1020.320(e)

A SAR, and **any information that would reveal the existence of a SAR**, are confidential and shall not be disclosed except as specifically authorised.

### Prohibition on Disclosure by Banks — §1020.320(e)(1)

**General rule:** No bank, and no director, officer, employee, or agent of any bank, shall disclose a SAR or any information that would reveal the existence of a SAR.

**Subpoena refusal obligation:** Any bank, director, officer, employee, or agent of any bank that is **subpoenaed or otherwise requested** to disclose a SAR or information that would reveal the existence of a SAR **shall decline to produce** the SAR or such information, citing §1020.320(e)(1)(i) and 31 USC 5318(g)(2)(A)(i). The bank **must notify FinCEN** of any such request and the response thereto. **(§1020.320(e)(1)(i))**

**"Rules of construction"** — provided that no person involved in any reported suspicious transaction is notified that the transaction has been reported, §1020.320(e)(1) shall **not** be construed as prohibiting:

- Disclosure of a SAR (or information that would reveal its existence) to **FinCEN** or any Federal, State, or local law enforcement agency, Federal regulatory authority, or State regulatory authority administering a BSA-compliance law **(§1020.320(e)(1)(ii)(A)(1))**
- Disclosure of the **underlying facts, transactions, and documents** upon which a SAR is based to another financial institution, or its directors, officers, employees, or agents, for the **preparation of a joint SAR** **(§1020.320(e)(1)(ii)(A)(2)(i))**
- Disclosure of the underlying facts to **certain employment references or termination notices** to the full extent authorised by 31 USC 5318(g)(2)(B) **(§1020.320(e)(1)(ii)(A)(2)(ii))**
- **Sharing of a SAR** (or information that would reveal its existence) within the bank's **corporate organisational structure** for purposes consistent with Title II of the BSA, as determined by regulation or guidance **(§1020.320(e)(1)(ii)(B))**

### Prohibition on Disclosure by Government Authorities — §1020.320(e)(2)

Federal, State, local, territorial, or Tribal government authorities, and their directors, officers, employees, or agents, shall not disclose a SAR or information that would reveal the existence of a SAR, **except as necessary to fulfil official duties consistent with Title II of the BSA**. "Official duties" does not include disclosure of a SAR in a **private legal proceeding**, including a request for disclosure pursuant to 31 CFR 1.11 (Treasury's FOIA rules). **(§1020.320(e)(2))**

---

## Safe Harbour — §1020.320(f)

A bank, and any director, officer, employee, or agent of any bank, that **makes a voluntary disclosure** of any possible violation of law or regulation to a government agency, or makes a disclosure pursuant to §1020.320 or any other authority, including a disclosure made jointly with another institution, is **protected from liability** to any person for any such disclosure, or for failure to provide notice of such disclosure to any person identified in the disclosure — to the **full extent provided by 31 USC 5318(g)(3)**. **(§1020.320(f))**

---

## Examination and Compliance — §1020.320(g)

Banks shall be examined by FinCEN or its delegatees for compliance with §1020.320. Failure to satisfy the requirements of this section may be a violation of the BSA and of Part 1020 of Title 31 CFR. Such failure may also constitute a violation of provisions of Title 12 of the Code of Federal Regulations. **(§1020.320(g))**

## Bank SAR vs General SAR Provision

| Feature | Bank SAR (§1020.320) | General provision (§1010.320) |
|---------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| Threshold | ≥**$5,000** | Directs to sector-specific part |
| Deadline | 30 days (60 if no suspect at detection) | Same framework |
| Ongoing scheme obligation | Immediate telephone notification to law enforcement | Not specified in §1010.320 |
| Exceptions | Robbery/burglary reported to police; lost/stolen securities per 17 CFR 240.17f-1 | Not specified in §1010.320 |
| SAR confidentiality | Full absolute rule with subpoena refusal obligation | Stated at §1010.320 level |
| Safe harbour | Explicit — full extent of 31 USC 5318(g)(3) | Referenced at §1010.320 level |
| Examination | By FinCEN or delegatees; failure = BSA violation + potential 12 CFR violation | By FinCEN and sector-specific regulators |

## Key Points for Agents

- **The $5,000 threshold applies to the aggregate of a transaction or attempted transaction, not individual components.** If a transaction involves or aggregates at least $5,000 and meets any of the three grounds, SAR filing is required. Attempted transactions are included. **(§1020.320(a)(2))**
- **The 30-day standard applies when a suspect is identified on the date of detection; the 60-day extension is only for the period to identify a suspect — not a general extension.** In no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days from initial detection. A bank that identifies a suspect on day 45 must still file within 60 days of initial detection (not 30 days from day 45). **(§1020.320(b)(3))**
- **Ongoing schemes require immediate telephone notification to law enforcement in addition to SAR filing — the two obligations are cumulative, not alternatives.** A bank that identifies an ongoing money laundering scheme cannot wait for the SAR deadline; it must call law enforcement immediately and also file the SAR on time. **(§1020.320(b)(3))**
- **The subpoena refusal obligation is affirmative.** A bank that receives a subpoena for a SAR or information that would reveal its existence must not merely decline — it must actively cite §1020.320(e)(1)(i) and 31 USC 5318(g)(2)(A)(i) in declining and must notify FinCEN of the request and the bank's response. **(§1020.320(e)(1)(i))**
- **Sharing underlying facts (not the SAR itself) with another FI for joint SAR preparation is permitted — but sharing the SAR or its existence is not.** The distinction matters for multi-institution suspicious activity investigations. The bank may discuss the underlying transaction facts with a co-filing institution; it may not disclose the SAR document or the fact that a SAR was filed. **(§1020.320(e)(1)(ii)(A)(2)(i))**
