# OFAC — Enforcement Process and Civil Penalties

## Overview

When OFAC identifies an apparent violation of US economic sanctions, it follows a structured enforcement process governed by 31 CFR Part 501 Subpart D and Appendix A (the Economic Sanctions Enforcement Guidelines). The process runs from initial investigation through Pre-Penalty Notice, civil monetary penalty (or lesser response), ALJ hearing (for TWEA-based penalties), Secretary's designee review, and judicial review. The penalty amount is determined by a four-quadrant base penalty matrix adjusted by 11 General Factors.

---

## Types of Enforcement Response

OFAC has a tiered menu of responses to an apparent violation. Each is a distinct formal action:

| Response | Nature | Final agency determination? |
|----------|--------|---------------------------|
| No Action | No violation established; OFAC may send a letter noting the closure | No |
| Request for Additional Information | Administrative subpoena under §501.602 | No |
| Cautionary Letter | Conduct occurred but insufficient evidence or severity; OFAC expresses concern | No |
| Finding of Violation | Violation documented; opportunity to respond before becoming final | Yes (once final) |
| Civil Monetary Penalty | Penalty Notice issued after Pre-Penalty Notice process | Yes |
| Criminal Referral | Referred to law enforcement; may run concurrently with civil action | N/A |
| Licence Denial / Suspension / Revocation | Administrative action on any general or specific licence | Yes |
| Cease and Desist Order | Order to stop conduct OFAC believes is ongoing or likely to recur | Yes |

**(Appendix A to Part 501, §II)**

## Civil Penalty Process

### Step 1 — Pre-Penalty Notice

OFAC issues a **Pre-Penalty Notice** when it has reason to believe a sanctions violation occurred and believes a civil monetary penalty is appropriate. The Pre-Penalty Notice sets out:
- The alleged violations and their value
- The base category used to calculate the proposed penalty
- The General Factors most relevant to the proposed penalty amount
- The statutory maximum applicable to each alleged violation
- The proposed penalty amount **(Appendix A §V.A.1)**

The Subject Person may submit a written response addressing the violations, applicable General Factors, and any other relevant information. **(Appendix A §V.A.2)**

If OFAC determines a penalty amount more than 10% higher than the Pre-Penalty Notice amount is appropriate, or intends to allege additional violations, it must issue a revised Pre-Penalty Notice before proceeding. **(Appendix A §V.A.3)**

### Step 2 — Penalty Notice

If OFAC concludes a civil monetary penalty is warranted after reviewing any response, it issues a **Penalty Notice**. The Penalty Notice:
- Constitutes **final agency action** that a violation has occurred
- Sets the final penalty amount (adjusted from the Pre-Penalty Notice based on the response and any further OFAC review)
- Will not exceed the Pre-Penalty Notice amount by more than 10% absent a revised Pre-Penalty Notice **(Appendix A §V.A.3)**

### Step 3 — ALJ Hearing (TWEA programmes only)

Under TWEA, a respondent may request a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge within the time period specified. ALJ hearings are governed by §§501.700–501.747, which establish:
- A preponderance of evidence standard (§501.732)
- Discovery procedures (depositions, interrogatories limited to 20 questions, document production — all complete 20 days before hearing) (§501.723)
- Separation of investigative and adjudicative functions (§501.720)
- Public hearings as the default (§501.721)
- ALJ authority to impose civil penalties consistent with the Penalty Guidelines (§501.740)

### Step 4 — Secretary's Designee Review

The Secretary's designee may, in their discretion, review an ALJ decision — either on petition of a party (filed within 10 days of the ALJ's decision) or on the Secretary's designee's own motion. Review is **not a right** but is a **prerequisite to judicial review** under the Administrative Procedure Act (5 USC 704). **(§501.741)**

### Step 5 — Judicial Review

Federal district court review is available after administrative remedies are exhausted (i.e., after the Secretary's designee has acted or declined to act). **(§501.741(g))**

### Step 6 — DOJ Collection

If a civil monetary penalty imposed under a Penalty Notice is not paid within **30 calendar days** of the written notice of imposition, OFAC may refer the matter to the US Department of Justice for collection in a civil action in federal district court. **(§501.746)**

## Settlements

A settlement does not constitute a final agency determination that a violation has occurred. Settlements may be initiated by OFAC or the Subject Person at any time and are negotiated under the General Factors principles. OFAC may condition settlement discussions on execution of a tolling agreement with respect to the statute of limitations. **(Appendix A §V.C)**

Settlement prior to issuance of a Pre-Penalty Notice: the Subject Person may request that OFAC withhold the Pre-Penalty Notice pending settlement discussions, and OFAC will generally agree as long as discussions are in good faith and the statute of limitations is not at risk. **(Appendix A §V.C.2)**

## The 11 General Factors — §III of Appendix A

OFAC considers all of the following factors in determining both the administrative response and the penalty amount:

### A — Willful or Reckless Violation of Law
Did the Subject Person know the conduct was a violation? Did they demonstrate reckless disregard? Was there concealment of the conduct? Is there a pattern of similar conduct? Did senior management know or should have known?

### B — Awareness of Conduct at Issue
The greater the Subject Person's actual knowledge or reason to know about the conduct constituting the apparent violation, the stronger the enforcement response. For entities, awareness focuses on supervisory and managerial staff and senior officers.

### C — Harm to Sanctions Programme Objectives
- Economic or other benefit conferred on sanctioned individuals, entities, or countries
- Implications for US policy and the integrity of the sanctions programme
- Whether the conduct would have been licensable under existing OFAC policy
- Whether the conduct supported a humanitarian activity

### D — Individual Characteristics
- Commercial sophistication of the Subject Person
- Size of operations and financial condition (small business status is considered)
- Volume of transactions (violations as a proportion of total transaction volume)
- **Sanctions history: prior penalties, Findings of Violation, cautionary or evaluative letters in the 5 years preceding the apparent violation**

### E — Compliance Programme
The existence, nature, and adequacy of the Subject Person's risk-based OFAC compliance programme at the time of the apparent violation. Where OFAC has a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the Subject Person's regulator, OFAC follows the MOU procedures to assess the compliance programme. A robust, adequately resourced SCP that meets the Framework's five components is directly mitigating under this factor.

### F — Remedial Response
Actions taken by the Subject Person upon learning of the apparent violation:
- Did the Subject Person immediately stop the conduct?
- Were the causes of the violation fully and expeditiously identified?
- Was senior management informed?
- Were new or enhanced internal controls implemented?
- Was a thorough review undertaken to identify other possible violations?

### G — Cooperation with OFAC
- Voluntary self-disclosure (VSD) of the apparent violation
- Full provision of all relevant information
- Research and disclosure of other violations arising from the same course of conduct
- Promptness of response to all requests
- Entry into a tolling agreement if requested by OFAC

**Note:** Refusal to enter into a tolling agreement will **not** be considered an aggravating factor. OFAC will publicly note substantial cooperation. **(Appendix A §III.G)**

### H — Timing
Whether the apparent violation occurred immediately after the imposition of sanctions or the addition of a new name to the SDN List — giving limited time to update compliance processes.

### I — Other Enforcement Action
Parallel enforcement action by federal, state, or local agencies for the same or similar apparent violations.

### J — Future Compliance / Deterrence Effect
The impact that administrative action may have on promoting future compliance by the Subject Person and similarly situated entities in the same industry sector.

### K — Other Relevant Factors
Any other factor OFAC deems relevant on a case-by-case basis to ensure the enforcement response is proportionate to the nature of the violation.

## Base Penalty Matrix

The base penalty for a civil monetary penalty is determined by two binary variables: whether the case is **egregious** and whether the Subject Person made a **voluntary self-disclosure (VSD)**.

|  | **Non-Egregious** | **Egregious** |
|--|-------------------|--------------|
| **VSD = Yes** | ½ of transaction value, capped at **lesser of $188,850 or ½ of applicable statutory max** | **½ of applicable statutory maximum** |
| **VSD = No** | Applicable schedule amount, capped at **lesser of $377,700 or applicable statutory max** | **Applicable statutory maximum** |

**Egregiousness** is determined based primarily on General Factors A (willfulness/recklessness), B (awareness), C (harm to programme objectives), and D (individual characteristics), with emphasis on A and B. A determination that a case is egregious is made by the OFAC Director or Deputy Director. **(Appendix A §V.B.1)**

### Statutory Maximums (Inflation-Adjusted)

| Statute | Statutory maximum per violation |
|---------|--------------------------------|
| IEEPA | Greater of $377,700 or 2× the underlying transaction value |
| TWEA | $111,308 |
| FNKDA | $1,876,699 |
| AEDPA | Greater of $99,703 or 2× the amount a financial institution was required to retain |
| CDTA | $17,062 |

**(Appendix A §V.B.2.a.v)**

### Adjustments to Base Penalty

| Circumstance | Adjustment |
|-------------|-----------|
| Substantial cooperation without VSD (e.g., third-party report, full provision of additional information) | 25–40% reduction from base |
| First violation (no penalty notice or Finding of Violation in preceding 5 years for substantially similar conduct) | Up to 25% reduction |

In all cases, the proposed penalty will not exceed the applicable statutory maximum. **(Appendix A §V.B.2.b)**

### Applicable Schedule Amount

The "applicable schedule amount" used in the non-egregious/no-VSD quadrant is:

| Transaction value | Schedule amount |
|-----------------|----------------|
| < $1,000 | $1,000 |
| $1,000 – $9,999 | $10,000 |
| $10,000 – $24,999 | $25,000 |
| $25,000 – $49,999 | $50,000 |
| $50,000 – $99,999 | $100,000 |
| $100,000 – $199,999 | $200,000 |
| ≥ $200,000 | Statutory maximum |

**(Appendix A §I.B)**

## Public Disclosure of Penalty Information — §501.805(d)

After conclusion of a civil penalties proceeding or informal settlement, OFAC publishes — at least monthly — the following for proceedings against **entities**:
- Name and address
- Sanctions programme involved
- Brief description of the violation or alleged violation
- Whether the proceeding resulted in an informal settlement or penalty
- Whether the entity **voluntarily self-disclosed**
- Amount of penalty or agreed settlement

For proceedings against **individuals**, OFAC releases only aggregate statistics — individual names are not disclosed publicly. **(§501.805(d))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Voluntary self-disclosure is a mathematically significant mitigating factor — it formally halves the base penalty in every quadrant of the matrix.** In a non-egregious case with VSD, the base is ½ transaction value capped at $188,850. Without VSD, the base is the applicable schedule amount capped at $377,700. In egregious cases, VSD halves the applicable statutory maximum (e.g., IEEPA: $188,850 vs. $377,700). **(Appendix A §V.B.2)**
- **A Penalty Notice is final agency action; it cannot exceed the Pre-Penalty Notice amount by more than 10% without a new Pre-Penalty Notice.** This creates a procedural floor for respondents. If OFAC wants to increase the proposed penalty by more than 10% or add new violations, it must issue a revised Pre-Penalty Notice and restart the response period. **(Appendix A §V.A.3)**
- **TWEA-based penalties are the only penalties with an ALJ hearing right.** IEEPA, FNKDA, and other statutory programme penalties do not have the same formal ALJ process under Part 501. For TWEA proceedings, the ALJ hearing is governed by §§501.700–501.747, and Secretary's designee review is a prerequisite to any judicial challenge. **(§§501.740–501.741)**
- **The 5-year sanctions history lookback determines "first violation" mitigation.** A Subject Person with no penalty notice or Finding of Violation within the 5 years preceding the apparent violation's transaction date qualifies for up to 25% reduction as a first violation. However, cautionary or evaluative letters — which are not final agency determinations — may still be considered in the General Factors analysis. **(Appendix A §V.B.2.b.ii)**
- **Unpaid penalties after 30 calendar days are referred to DOJ.** OFAC advises the Financial Management Division upon imposition; if the penalty is not paid within 30 days of written notice of imposition, the matter is referred to the US Department of Justice for civil collection in federal district court. **(§501.746)**
