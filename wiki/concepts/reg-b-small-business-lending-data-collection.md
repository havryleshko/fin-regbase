# Regulation B — Small Business Lending Data Collection (12 CFR Part 1002, Subpart B)

## Overview

Subpart B (§§1002.101–1002.114) implements section 704B of ECOA (15 USC 1691c-2), added by section 1071 of the Dodd-Frank Act. It requires covered financial institutions to collect, maintain, and annually report to the CFPB detailed data on applications for credit from small businesses — including information about whether applicants are minority-owned, women-owned, or LGBTQI+-owned businesses.

The dual purpose of Section 1071 is: **(§1002.101(b))**
1. To facilitate enforcement of fair lending laws; and
2. To enable communities, governmental entities, and creditors to identify business and community development needs and opportunities of women-owned, minority-owned, and small businesses.

**Effective date:** August 29, 2023. Compliance is phased based on origination volume (see below).

---

## Who Is Covered — "Covered Financial Institution"

A **covered financial institution** is any financial institution that originated at least **100 covered credit transactions for small businesses** in **each of the two preceding calendar years**. **(§1002.105(b))**

A **financial institution** is any partnership, company, corporation, association (incorporated or unincorporated), trust, estate, cooperative organisation, or other entity that engages in any financial activity. This is broad — it includes non-bank lenders, fintech lenders, and CDFIs, not just banks. **(§1002.105(a))**

**Subsidiaries:** A covered financial institution that is a subsidiary of another covered financial institution must complete a separate small business lending application register. **(§1002.109(a)(2))**

---

## What Is a Small Business?

**Small business** means a business with **gross annual revenue of $5 million or less** for its preceding fiscal year. **(§1002.106(b)(1))**

This threshold is based on the SBA's "small business concern" definition (15 USC 632(a), 13 CFR 121.101–121.107) but uses a fixed $5 million gross annual revenue ceiling regardless of industry-specific SBA size standards.

**CPI adjustment:** Every 5 years after January 1, 2025, the $5 million threshold adjusts based on the CPI-U (all urban consumers, not seasonally adjusted), rounded to the nearest $500,000. **(§1002.106(b)(2))**

A **business** has the same meaning as "business concern or concern" in 13 CFR 121.105 — broadly, any entity organised for profit or non-profit that is independently owned and operated. **(§1002.106(a))**

---

## What Is a Covered Application?

A **covered application** is an oral or written request for a covered credit transaction that is made in accordance with procedures used by a financial institution for the type of credit requested. **(§1002.103(a))**

**Not a covered application:**
- Reevaluation, extension, or renewal requests on an existing business credit account, unless the request seeks additional credit amounts
- Inquiries and prequalification requests **(§1002.103(b))**

---

## What Is a Covered Credit Transaction?

A **covered credit transaction** is an extension of business credit that is not an excluded transaction. **(§1002.104(a))**

**Excluded transactions — Subpart B does NOT apply to:** **(§1002.104(b))**
1. **Trade credit** — financing arrangements where a business acquires goods or services without immediate payment
2. **HMDA-reportable transactions** — covered loans or applications under Regulation C (12 CFR Part 1003)
3. **Insurance premium financing**
4. **Public utilities credit** (as defined in §1002.3(a))
5. **Securities credit** (as defined in §1002.3(b))
6. **Incidental credit** (as defined in §1002.3(c))

---

## The 20 Data Fields to Collect (§1002.107(a))

A covered financial institution must compile and maintain the following 20 data points for each covered application:

| # | Field | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Unique identifier | Alphanumeric identifier linking to the specific application file |
| 2 | Application date | Date application received or shown on paper/electronic form |
| 3 | Application method | How the applicant submitted the application |
| 4 | Application recipient | Direct to institution, affiliate, or via third party |
| 5 | Credit type | Credit product; type of guarantees; loan term in months |
| 6 | Credit purpose | Purpose(s) of the credit applied for or originated |
| 7 | Amount applied for | Initial credit amount or credit limit requested |
| 8 | Amount approved/originated | Amount approved (if not accepted) or originated |
| 9 | Action taken | Originated / approved-not-accepted / denied / withdrawn / incomplete |
| 10 | Action taken date | Date of action |
| 11 | Denial reasons | Principal reason(s) for denial (for denied applications) |
| 12 | Pricing information | Interest rate (fixed or adjustable with components); total origination charges; broker fees; initial annual charges; additional cost for merchant cash advances/sales-based financing; prepayment penalty existence and amount |
| 13 | Census tract | Location where proceeds will be principally applied |
| 14 | Gross annual revenue | Applicant's gross annual revenue for preceding fiscal year |
| 15 | NAICS code | 3-digit North American Industry Classification System code |
| 16 | Number of workers | Number of non-owners working for the applicant |
| 17 | Time in business | How long the applicant has been in business |
| 18 | Minority/women/LGBTQI+-owned business status | Whether applicant is a minority-owned, women-owned, and/or LGBTQI+-owned business |
| 19 | Ethnicity, race, and sex of principal owners | For each principal owner (25%+ direct equity) |
| 20 | Number of principal owners | Number of applicant's principal owners |

**(§1002.107(a)(1)–(20))**

**Definitions for ownership categories:**
- **Minority-owned business:** More than 50% of ownership/control AND more than 50% of net profits/losses accrue to American Indian or Alaska Native, Asian, Black or African American, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander, or Hispanic or Latino individual(s). **(§1002.102(m))**
- **Women-owned business:** More than 50% of ownership/control AND more than 50% of net profits/losses accrue to women. **(§1002.102(s))**
- **LGBTQI+-owned business:** More than 50% of ownership/control AND more than 50% of net profits/losses accrue to individual(s) who identify as lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, or intersex. **(§1002.102(l))**
- **Principal owner:** Individual who directly owns 25% or more of the equity interests of a business. **(§1002.102(o))**

## Collection Procedures — Anti-Discouragement Obligation

A covered financial institution must not discourage an applicant from responding to requests for applicant-provided data. It must maintain procedures to collect data in a manner reasonably designed to obtain a response. **(§1002.107(c)(1))**

For data collected directly from the applicant, procedures must ensure:
- The initial request for applicant-provided data occurs **prior to notifying the applicant of final action** on the application
- The request is prominently displayed or presented
- The collection does not have the effect of discouraging a response
- Applicants can easily respond **(§1002.107(c)(2))**

A **low response rate** for applicant-provided data may indicate discouragement or other failure to maintain compliant collection procedures. **(§1002.107(c)(4))**

**Previously collected data:** A covered financial institution may reuse previously collected data (collected within 36 months for most fields, or within the same calendar year for gross annual revenue) if the institution has no reason to believe the data are inaccurate. **(§1002.107(d))**

## The Firewall Requirement (§1002.108)

This is the most operationally complex element of Subpart B.

**Prohibition:** Unless an exception applies, an employee or officer of a covered financial institution (or its affiliate) **shall not have access** to an applicant's responses to the institution's inquiries about:
- Whether the applicant is a minority-owned, women-owned, and/or LGBTQI+-owned business (§1002.107(a)(18))
- The ethnicity, race, and sex of the applicant's principal owners (§1002.107(a)(19))

...if that employee or officer is **involved in making any determination** concerning the evaluation of the covered application or the creditworthiness of the applicant. **(§1002.108(b))**

**"Involved in making any determination"** means participating in a decision regarding the evaluation of a covered application from a small business or the creditworthiness of a small business applicant for a covered credit transaction. **(§1002.108(a)(1))**

**"Should have access"** means an employee or officer may need to collect, see, consider, refer to, or otherwise use the information to perform their assigned job duties. **(§1002.108(a)(2))**

**Exception:** The prohibition does not apply to an employee or officer if the institution determines that it is **not feasible** to limit access and the institution **provides the applicant with a notice** (the "firewall notice") informing them that one or more employees or officers involved in making credit determinations may have access to the §1002.107(a)(18) and (19) responses. **(§1002.108(c))**

The firewall notice must be provided at the same time as the §1002.107(a)(18) and (19) inquiries are made. **(§1002.108(d))**

**Operational implication:** Institutions must either: (A) build technical access controls that prevent underwriters and credit decision makers from accessing the demographic and ownership fields; or (B) implement the notice process and document why access controls are not feasible.

## Reporting to the CFPB (§1002.109)

**Annual reporting:** A covered financial institution must submit its **small business lending application register** to the CFPB **on or before June 1** following the calendar year for which data are compiled. **(§1002.109(a)(1)(i))**

An authorised representative with knowledge of the data must certify its accuracy and completeness. **(§1002.109(a)(1)(ii))**

**What to include in the submission — institution-level identifying information (§1002.109(b)):**
- Institution name and headquarters address
- Contact information for the regulator/Bureau
- Federal prudential regulator (if applicable)
- Federal Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)
- Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)
- RSSD ID number (if applicable)
- Parent entity information (name, LEI, RSSD ID of immediate parent and top-holding parent)
- Institution type
- Whether the institution is voluntarily reporting applications from small businesses that are not otherwise covered

**Multi-institution transactions:** Where more than one financial institution must make a credit decision to approve a single covered credit transaction, only the **last covered financial institution** with authority to set the material terms of the transaction is required to report. **(§1002.109(a)(3))**

---

## Publication and Disclosure Rules (§1002.110)

**CFPB publication:** The Bureau must make data from the small business lending application register publicly available on an annual basis, subject to deletions or modifications that would advance a privacy interest. **(§1002.110(a))**

**Institution's public statement obligation:** A covered financial institution must make available on its public website (or otherwise upon request) a statement that its small business lending application register **is or will be available from the Bureau**. This statement must be available from the date the institution submits its register until its obligation to retain the register expires. **(§1002.110(c)–(d))**

**Prohibition on further disclosure of demographic data:** **(§1002.110(e))**
- A financial institution shall not disclose or provide to a third party the §1002.107(a)(18) and (19) data (minority/women/LGBTQI+ ownership status; ethnicity/race/sex of principal owners) except to further compliance with the Act or as required by law.
- A third party that obtains this data for compliance purposes is similarly prohibited from further disclosure.

---

## Recordkeeping (§1002.111)

**Retention period:** A covered financial institution must retain its small business lending application register for **at least 3 years** after it is required to be submitted to the CFPB. **(§1002.111(a))**

**Separation of demographic data:** The institution must maintain the applicant's responses to §1002.107(a)(18) (ownership status) and §1002.107(a)(19) (ethnicity/race/sex) data **separately from the rest of the application file**. **(§1002.111(b))**

**No PII in the register:** When compiling and maintaining the register, the institution must not include any name, specific address, telephone number, email address, or other PII concerning any individual who is or is connected with an applicant, other than as required by §1002.107 or §1002.111(b). **(§1002.111(c))**

---

## Enforcement, Bona Fide Errors, and Safe Harbors (§1002.112)

**Enforcement:** A violation of Subpart B is subject to administrative sanctions and civil liability under ECOA sections 704 and 706. **(§1002.112(a))**

**Bona fide error:** A bona fide error in compiling, maintaining, or reporting data is not a violation if it was unintentional and occurred despite the maintenance of procedures reasonably adapted to avoid such errors. A financial institution is **presumed** to maintain adequate procedures if the number of errors found in a random sample of its submission does not equal or exceed a threshold specified by the Bureau in Appendix F. **(§1002.112(b))**

**Safe harbors (§1002.112(c)):**
1. **Application date:** Reporting an application date within 3 business days of the actual date is not a violation.
2. **Census tract:** An incorrect census tract entry is not a violation if the institution obtained the census tract by correctly using a geocoding tool provided by the FFIEC or the Bureau.
3. **NAICS code:** An incorrect 3-digit NAICS code is not a violation if the institution obtained the code by: (i) relying on the applicant's representations or an appropriate third-party source; or (ii) identifying the NAICS code itself, provided the institution maintained procedures reasonably adapted to do so correctly.
4. **Incorrect determination of small business status:** Not a violation if the institution had a reasonable basis to believe the application was a covered application for a covered credit transaction from a small business — even if it later concludes it should not have collected the data.

## Compliance Dates — Phased Implementation (§1002.114(b))

| Origination volume (covered credit transactions for small businesses in **each** of 2022 and 2023) | Compliance date |
|---|---|
| ≥2,500 transactions | **July 1, 2026** |
| ≥500 transactions (not in tier above) | **January 1, 2027** |
| ≥100 transactions (not in tiers above) | **October 1, 2027** |
| <100 transactions in 2022 and/or 2023 but subsequently reaches ≥100 in two consecutive years | In accordance with §1002.105(b), but no earlier than October 1, 2027 |

**Alternative time periods:** An institution that cannot determine its 2022/2023 originations may use 2023/2024 or 2024/2025 originations to determine its compliance tier. **(§1002.114(c)(3))**

**Early voluntary collection:** Institutions in all tiers may voluntarily begin collecting §1002.107(a)(18) and (19) data up to 12 months before their applicable compliance date, provided they comply with the firewall, collection procedure, and notice requirements when doing so. **(§1002.114(c)(1))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **The $5 million gross annual revenue threshold is fixed for Subpart B — it does not vary by industry.** Unlike SBA size standards (which use industry-specific employee counts or revenue caps), Subpart B uses a single $5 million gross revenue ceiling for all industries. A manufacturing firm with 500 employees but $6 million revenue is not a "small business" under Subpart B. **(§1002.106(b))**
- **HMDA-reportable transactions are excluded.** A covered loan under Regulation C is not a covered credit transaction under Subpart B. Financial institutions subject to both HMDA and Subpart B must identify which transactions fall under each regime — the regimes do not overlap. **(§1002.104(b)(2))**
- **The firewall is an operational control, not just a policy.** The regulation prohibits access, not just use. An underwriter who can see but is instructed not to use demographic data is not in compliance. Institutions need technical access controls or a documented infeasibility determination plus applicant notice. **(§1002.108)**
- **Annual reporting is due June 1 — for data collected the prior calendar year.** For a July 2026 compliant institution, the first submission covers data collected from July 1 to December 31, 2026, and is due June 1, 2027. **(§1002.109(a)(1))**
- **The 3-year recordkeeping period runs from the submission date, not the collection date.** If a register is submitted June 1, 2027, it must be retained until at least June 1, 2030. **(§1002.111(a))**
- **Demographic data must be physically separated from the application file.** This is not satisfied by role-based access controls alone — the data must be maintained separately in a distinct record. **(§1002.111(b))**
- **Bona fide error presumption is data-rate based.** The Bureau publishes in Appendix F the error rate threshold below which an institution is presumed to have maintained adequate procedures. Institutions should track their own error rates against this threshold. **(§1002.112(b))**
