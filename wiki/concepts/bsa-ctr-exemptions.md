# BSA — CTR Exemptions for Banks

## Overview

§1020.315 allows banks to exempt certain customers from Currency Transaction Report (CTR) filing requirements under §1010.311. A bank is not required to file a CTR for a currency transaction between an exempt person and that bank, or (in limited circumstances) between an exempt person and affiliated banks. The exemption reduces unnecessary reporting burden for transactions with known, low-risk customers — but does not eliminate SAR obligations or monitoring requirements. **(§1020.315(a))**

---

## The Seven Categories of Exempt Persons — §1020.315(b)

| Category | §1020.315 | Who qualifies | Designation required? |
|----------|-----------|--------------|----------------------|
| 1 | (b)(1) | **Another bank**, to the extent of the bank's domestic operations | No |
| 2 | (b)(2) | **Department or agency of the US, any State, or any political subdivision** of any State | No |
| 3 | (b)(3) | Any **entity established under US, State, or political subdivision law** that exercises governmental authority on behalf of the US, a State, or a political subdivision | No |
| 4 | (b)(4) | Any entity (other than a bank) whose **common stock or analogous equity is listed on NYSE, AMEX, or NASDAQ** (National Market Securities only; not "NASDAQ Capital Markets Companies"), to the extent of its domestic operations | Yes |
| 5 | (b)(5) | Any **subsidiary (other than a bank)** of a listed entity in (b)(4) that is organised under US or State law and at least **51% owned** by the listed entity, to the extent of its domestic operations | Yes |
| 6 | (b)(6) | Any **non-listed business** — a commercial enterprise (other than an ineligible business) that: (i) maintains a transaction account at the bank for at least 2 months; (ii) frequently engages in currency transactions with the bank in excess of $10,000; and (iii) is incorporated/organised or registered under US or State law | Yes |
| 7 | (b)(7) | Any **payroll customer** — a person that: (i) maintains a transaction account at the bank for at least 2 months; (ii) operates a firm that frequently withdraws >$10,000 in currency to pay US employees; and (iii) is incorporated/organised or registered under US or State law | Yes |

## Designation Procedure — §1020.315(c)

For categories (b)(4)–(b)(7), a bank must **designate** the customer as an exempt person by **filing FinCEN Form 110** within the **30-calendar-day period beginning on the day of the first reportable CTR-triggering transaction** with that customer sought to be exempted. The designation must be made separately by each bank treating that customer as exempt (except as provided in the affiliated-bank rule). **(§1020.315(c)(1))**

**No FinCEN Form 110 required** for: transfers to or from any of the 12 Federal Reserve Banks; or customers in categories (b)(1)–(b)(3). **(§1020.315(c)(2)(i))**

**Early designation:** A bank may designate a non-listed business or payroll customer **before the first CTR-triggering transaction** if the bank:
- Conducts and **documents a risk-based assessment** of the customer, and
- Forms a **reasonable belief** that the customer has a **legitimate business purpose** for conducting frequent currency transactions

This early designation is available even if the customer has not yet maintained an account for 2 months. **(§1020.315(c)(2)(ii))**

**Affiliated banks:** The designation required by this section may be made by a **parent bank holding company** or one of its bank subsidiaries on behalf of all bank subsidiaries of the holding company, provided the designation lists each bank subsidiary to which it shall apply. **(§1020.315(e)(6))**

---

## Annual Review — §1020.315(d)

A bank must review the eligibility of exempt persons in categories (b)(4)–(b)(7) **at least once each year** to determine whether each person remains eligible for the exemption. As part of the annual review, the bank must review the application of its monitoring system (required under §1020.315(h)(2)) to each existing account of an exempt person in categories (b)(6) and (b)(7). **(§1020.315(d))**

---

## Ineligible Businesses — §1020.315(e)(8)

The following businesses **may not be treated as non-listed businesses** under (b)(6):

- Serving as financial institutions or agents of financial institutions of any type
- Purchase or sale to customers of **motor vehicles of any kind, vessels, aircraft, farm equipment, or mobile homes**
- Practice of **law, accountancy, or medicine**
- **Auctioning** of goods
- **Chartering or operation of ships, buses, or aircraft**
- **Gaming** of any kind (other than licensed parimutuel betting at race tracks)
- **Investment advisory services or investment banking services**
- **Real estate brokerage**
- **Pawn brokerage**
- **Title insurance and real estate closing**
- **Trade union activities**
- Any other activities that may be specified by FinCEN

A business engaged in **multiple business activities** may be treated as a non-listed business so long as **no more than 50%** of its gross revenues are derived from ineligible activities. **(§1020.315(e)(8))**

## Operating Rules — §1020.315(e)

**Governmental departments and agencies (b)(2)/(b)(3)):** A bank may treat a person as a government entity if the name of the person reasonably indicates it is a government entity, or if the person is known generally in the community to be a State, District of Columbia, tribal government, territory, insular possession, or political subdivision or wholly-owned agency or instrumentality. Examples of entities exercising governmental authority: New Jersey Turnpike Authority; Port Authority of New York and New Jersey. **(§1020.315(e)(2))**

**Stock exchange listings for (b)(4):** A bank may rely on a NYSE, AMEX, or NASDAQ listing published in a newspaper of general circulation, a commonly accepted stock symbol guide, SEC EDGAR, or any Internet site maintained by NYSE, AMEX, or NASDAQ. **(§1020.315(e)(3))**

**Listed company subsidiaries for (b)(5):** A bank may rely on: a reasonably authenticated corporate officer's certificate; a reasonably authenticated photocopy of IRS Form 851 (Affiliation Schedule); or an Annual Report or Form 10-K filed with the SEC. **(§1020.315(e)(4))**

**Aggregated accounts:** A bank may treat all exemptible accounts of a non-listed business or payroll customer as a **single account** for purposes of determining qualification. If the bank elects to treat accounts as a single account, it must continue to do so consistently. **(§1020.315(e)(5))**

**Sole proprietorships:** May be treated as a non-listed business or payroll customer if they otherwise meet the applicable requirements. **(§1020.315(e)(7))**

---

## Limitation on Exemption — Agent Transactions — §1020.315(f)

A transaction carried out by an exempt person **as an agent for another person** who is the beneficial owner of the funds is **not exempt**. The agent's exempt status does not shield the transaction from CTR reporting where the true beneficial owner of the funds is a different, non-exempt person. **(§1020.315(f))**

---

## Limitation on Liability — §1020.315(g)

**No bank shall be subject to penalty** for failure to file a CTR under §1010.311 with respect to a transaction by an exempt person for which the exemption requirements are satisfied, **unless** the bank:
- **(i)** Knowingly files **false or incomplete information** with respect to the transaction or the customer; or
- **(ii)** Has **reason to believe** that the customer does not meet the exempt person criteria, or that the transaction is not a transaction of the exempt person

A bank that treats a customer as exempt shall remain subject to the CTR filing rules for that customer until the completion of the next required annual review for categories (b)(4)–(b)(7), absent specific knowledge of disqualifying information. **(§1020.315(g))**

**Automatic revocation:** The status of a listed company as an exempt person under (b)(4) ceases once the company ceases to be listed. The status of a listed company subsidiary as exempt under (b)(5) ceases once the subsidiary ceases to be at least 51% owned by a listed entity. **(§1020.315(i))**

---

## SAR Obligation NOT Eliminated — §1020.315(h)

The CTR exemption does **not** eliminate or reduce in any way a bank's obligation to file a SAR under §1020.320 with respect to any transaction — including any transaction in currency — that the bank knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect is a suspicious transaction as described in §1020.320(a)(2)(i), (ii), or (iii). **(§1020.315(h)(1))**

A bank must establish and maintain a **monitoring system** reasonably designed to detect, for each account of a non-listed business or payroll customer, those transactions in currency that would require a bank to file a SAR. **(§1020.315(h)(2))**

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **Categories (b)(1)–(b)(3) (banks, governments) are automatically exempt — no FinCEN Form 110 required.** The bank does not need to take any affirmative action to exempt transactions with other banks or government entities. Categories (b)(4)–(b)(7) require formal designation. **(§1020.315(b)–(c))**
- **Designation must occur within 30 days of the first CTR-triggering transaction — or earlier with a documented risk assessment.** Early designation (before the first transaction or before the 2-month account tenure) is available only if the bank documents a risk-based assessment forming a reasonable belief in legitimate business purpose. **(§1020.315(c))**
- **The ineligible business list (§1020.315(e)(8)) is a hard exclusion — not a risk-based determination.** Financial institution agents, car/aircraft/vessel dealers, gaming establishments, real estate brokers, pawn brokers, investment advisers, and investment bankers cannot be non-listed businesses regardless of their perceived risk level. **(§1020.315(e)(8))**
- **CTR exemption and SAR obligation are entirely independent.** Granting a CTR exemption does not reduce or eliminate the SAR obligation. A bank must maintain a monitoring system for non-listed business and payroll accounts specifically to identify SAR-triggering transactions that happen to be in currency. **(§1020.315(h))**
- **Annual review is mandatory for (b)(4)–(b)(7) and must include a review of the monitoring system.** Failure to conduct the annual review can expose the bank to liability even for transactions that would otherwise be exempt. **(§1020.315(d))**
