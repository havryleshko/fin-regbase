# Regulation P — Privacy Notice Requirements

## Consumer vs. Customer — the Foundational Distinction

The entire Regulation P notice framework pivots on whether an individual is a mere **consumer** or a **customer**.

| Term | Definition | Notice trigger |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Consumer** (§1016.3(e)) | Individual who obtains or has obtained a financial product or service for personal, family, or household purposes — includes applicants | Must receive initial notice **before** NPI is disclosed to any nonaffiliated third party (unless an exception under §§1016.14/1016.15 covers the disclosure) **(§1016.4(a)(2))** |
| **Customer** (§1016.3(i)) | A consumer who has a **continuing relationship** with the institution | Must receive initial notice at or before the relationship is established; must receive annual notice throughout the relationship **(§§1016.4(a)(1), 1016.5(a))** |

### What Constitutes a Continuing Relationship (§1016.3(j))

A continuing relationship exists when a consumer:
- Has a deposit, investment, or credit account with the institution
- Holds a loan that the institution owns or services
- Purchases insurance from the institution
- Holds an investment product through the institution (e.g., custodial IRA)
- Enters an agreement for the institution to arrange or broker a home mortgage loan
- Leases personal property from the institution on a non-operating basis
- Obtains financial, investment, or economic advisory services for a fee

**No continuing relationship** (therefore consumer only, not customer):
- ATM cash withdrawal at another institution's ATM
- Purchase of a cashier's check or money order
- Purchase of airline tickets, travel insurance, or traveler's checks in isolated transactions
- Institution sells the loan and does not retain servicing rights **(§1016.3(j)(2))**

## Initial Privacy Notice (§1016.4)

### When Required

| Recipient | Timing |
|-----------|--------|
| New **customer** (general rule) | At or before the time the customer relationship is established |
| New customer (relationship not at customer's election) | Within a reasonable time after establishing the relationship — e.g., institution acquires servicing rights from another institution; the consumer has no choice **(§1016.4(e)(1)(i))** |
| New customer (providing notice would substantially delay the transaction) | At or before the time of the transaction; consumer must agree to receive notice later **(§1016.4(e)(1)(ii))** |
| **Consumer** (non-customer) | Before the institution discloses NPI to any nonaffiliated third party **(§1016.4(a)(2))** |

**Existing customers obtaining a new product or service:** An institution satisfies the initial notice obligation by:
1. Providing a revised notice (§1016.8) that covers the new product/service; or
2. Providing no new notice at all if the existing notice remains accurate with respect to the new product/service **(§1016.4(d))**

### Initial Notice Not Required (§1016.4(b))

An institution need not provide an initial notice to a consumer (non-customer) if:
- The institution does not disclose NPI about the consumer to any nonaffiliated third party other than under §§1016.14 and 1016.15; **AND**
- The institution does not have a customer relationship with the consumer **(§1016.4(b))**

## Annual Privacy Notice (§1016.5)

### General Rule

An institution must provide a **clear and conspicuous** annual notice to each customer **at least once in any 12-consecutive-month period** during the continuation of the customer relationship. The institution may define the 12-month period on a consistent basis (e.g., calendar year). **(§1016.5(a))**

**Example:** If a customer opens an account on any day of Year 1, the institution must send the annual notice by December 31 of Year 2 (if defining the period as calendar year). **(§1016.5(a)(2))**

### Annual Notice Exception (§1016.5(e)) — Key Safe Harbour

An institution is **not required to deliver an annual privacy notice** if both conditions are met:

**Condition 1:** The institution provides NPI to nonaffiliated third parties only under one or more of the following exceptions:
- §1016.13 (service providers and joint marketing — with written contract)
- §1016.14 (processing and servicing transactions)
- §1016.15 (other exceptions — consent, fraud prevention, legal requirements, etc.)

**Condition 2:** The institution has not changed its policies and practices with respect to disclosing NPI from what was disclosed in the most recently provided privacy notice. **(§1016.5(e)(1))**

**Losing the exception:** If the institution changes practices so it no longer meets both conditions, it must deliver an annual notice. If a revised notice (§1016.8) is also required, the annual notice must be delivered in accordance with normal timing. If a revised notice is **not** required (the change does not trigger §1016.8), the annual notice must be delivered **within 100 days of the change**. **(§1016.5(e)(2))**

**Former customers:** An institution need not provide an annual notice to a former customer. **(§1016.5(b)(1))**

## Required Contents of Privacy Notices (§1016.6)

Every initial, annual, and revised notice must include each applicable item:

| Item | Content |
|------|---------|
| 1 | Categories of NPI collected |
| 2 | Categories of NPI disclosed |
| 3 | Categories of affiliates and nonaffiliated third parties to whom NPI is disclosed (excluding §§1016.14/1016.15 parties) |
| 4 | Categories of NPI disclosed about **former** customers and parties to whom disclosed |
| 5 | If sharing under §1016.13 (service provider/joint marketing): separate statement of NPI categories and contracted parties |
| 6 | Explanation of consumer's opt-out right and opt-out methods (if opt-out applies) |
| 7 | Any FCRA §603(d)(2)(A)(iii) affiliate marketing opt-out disclosures |
| 8 | Policies and practices for protecting confidentiality and security of NPI |
| 9 | Any other disclosure the institution wishes to include **(§1016.6(a))** |

**Disclosure about parties under exceptions (§1016.6(b)):** When disclosing NPI under §§1016.14 or 1016.15, the institution is not required to list those parties in items 3 or 4. It may simply state: "For everyday business purposes, such as to process transactions, maintain account(s), respond to court orders and legal investigations, or to report to credit bureaus" and "As permitted by law."

**Simplified notice:** If the institution does not disclose (and does not reserve the right to disclose) NPI to affiliates or nonaffiliated third parties other than under §§1016.14/1016.15, it may simply state that fact and need only provide items 1, 8, and 9. **(§1016.6(c)(5))**

## Model Privacy Form (§1016.2; Appendix to Part 1016)

The Appendix contains a standardised two-page **model privacy form** (available in four versions: no opt-out; telephone/online opt-out; mail-in opt-out form; optional mail-in opt-out form). Use of the model form **consistent with the Instructions** in the Appendix constitutes compliance with notice content requirements in §§1016.6 and 1016.7 — institutions gain regulatory safe harbour. Use is not mandatory. **(§1016.2(a))**

**Key model form elements:**
- **Page 1:** "FACTS" header; Why/What/How key frame; disclosure table ("Reasons we can share your personal information") with Yes/No and opt-out columns; optional "To limit our sharing" section; Questions box
- **Page 2:** Who we are; What we do (protection, collection methods, limits on sharing); Definitions (affiliates, nonaffiliates, joint marketing)

The model form must use **minimum 10-point type**, portrait orientation, white or light paper, and may include a corporate logo. **(Appendix B.3)**

## Opt-Out Notice (§1016.7)

The opt-out notice must state: (i) that NPI is or will be disclosed to nonaffiliated third parties; (ii) that the consumer has the right to opt out; and (iii) a reasonable means to opt out. **(§1016.7(a)(1))**

### Reasonable vs. Unreasonable Opt-Out Means

| Reasonable | Not Reasonable |
|-----------|----------------|
| Designated checkboxes in a prominent position on relevant forms | Only means is for the consumer to write their own letter |
| Reply form with mailing address | Only means after the initial notice is a check-off box provided with the initial notice but not included with a subsequent notice |
| Electronic means (email, website form) if consumer agrees to electronic delivery |  |
| Toll-free telephone number |  |

**(§1016.7(a)(2)(ii)–(iii))**

### Timing of Opt-Out Implementation

- Opt-out must be implemented **as soon as reasonably practicable** after receipt **(§1016.7(g))**
- A consumer may exercise the opt-out right **at any time** **(§1016.7(h))**
- The institution must allow **at least 30 days** from mailing for consumers to opt out (or at least 30 days from electronic acknowledgment) before beginning the disclosure — this constitutes a "reasonable opportunity" **(§1016.10(a)(3))**

### Duration

An opt-out direction applies permanently until the consumer revokes it in writing or electronically (if agreed). When a customer relationship ends, the opt-out direction continues to apply to NPI collected during or related to that relationship. A **new customer relationship** with the same institution does **not** automatically reset a prior opt-out direction. **(§1016.7(i))**

## Delivering Notices (§1016.9)

Notices must be delivered so consumers can reasonably be expected to receive **actual notice**, in writing or (if the consumer agrees) electronically.

**Reasonable expectation of actual notice:**
- Hand-delivered printed copy
- Mailed printed copy to last known address
- For electronic transactions: post on the electronic site and require acknowledgment as a necessary step to obtaining the product/service
- For isolated transactions (e.g., ATM): post notice on the ATM screen and require acknowledgment **(§1016.9(b)(1))**

**Not sufficient for actual notice:**
- Posting a sign in a branch or office
- Publishing general advertisements
- Sending email to a consumer who does not electronically obtain products or services from the institution **(§1016.9(b)(2))**

**Annual notices only — relaxed standard:** An institution may reasonably expect a customer to receive the annual notice if: (1) the customer uses the website for financial products/services and has agreed to receive notices electronically, and the current notice is continuously and conspicuously posted on the website; or (2) the customer has requested that the institution refrain from sending relationship information, and the notice remains available upon request. **(§1016.9(c))**

**Oral notice is never sufficient** — oral description of the notice content does not satisfy any notice requirement. **(§1016.9(d))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Customers require both initial and annual notices; one-time consumers require a notice only if NPI will be disclosed outside §§1016.14/1016.15.** The customer/consumer distinction is the primary gate for notice obligations. **(§§1016.3(e), (i); 1016.4–1016.5)**
- **The annual notice exception is practically significant.** Institutions sharing only under service-provider, transaction-processing, and legal exceptions — and that have not changed their stated policies — have no annual notice obligation. Many banks and fintechs that do not sell customer data to marketers qualify. **(§1016.5(e))**
- **Requiring a consumer to write their own letter to opt out is explicitly unreasonable.** This is named as an unreasonable method in the regulation itself. Reasonable methods include checkboxes, reply forms, electronic submission, and toll-free numbers. **(§1016.7(a)(2)(ii)–(iii))**
- **The model form provides regulatory safe harbour but is not mandatory.** Institutions may use their own notices that meet the content requirements of §1016.6. **(§1016.2(a))**
- **Opt-out directions are durable — they survive relationship termination and are not automatically reset by a new relationship.** A consumer who opts out remains opted out for all NPI collected from the prior relationship, even after becoming a customer again. **(§1016.7(i))**
