# BSA — Correspondent Accounts, Private Banking, Shell Bank Prohibition, and Special Measures

## Overview

31 CFR Part 1010 Subpart F establishes three overlapping due diligence regimes and an absolute prohibition for covered financial institutions operating correspondent banking and private banking relationships with foreign financial institutions and non-US persons:

1. **Correspondent account due diligence** (§1010.610) — risk-based DD for all foreign FI correspondent accounts; mandatory enhanced DD for high-risk categories
2. **Private banking account due diligence** (§1010.620) — mandatory KYC and PEP screening for accounts ≥$1M held by non-US persons
3. **Shell bank prohibition** (§1010.630) — absolute ban on correspondent accounts for foreign shell banks; 3-year recertification safe harbour
4. **Special measures** (§§1010.651–1010.664) — entity-specific and country-level account prohibitions under Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act

---

## Key Definitions (§1010.605)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Correspondent account** | Account established for a foreign financial institution (FFI) to receive deposits from, make payments on behalf of, or handle other financial transactions of the FFI |
| **Foreign shell bank** | A foreign bank without a physical presence in any country |
| **Physical presence** | A place of business: (a) maintained by a foreign bank; (b) at a fixed address in a country where the bank is authorised to conduct banking; (c) where the bank employs one or more individuals on a full-time basis; and (d) maintains operating records related to its banking activities; and (e) is subject to inspection by the licensing banking authority |
| **Private banking account** | Account (or combination of accounts) at a covered FI: (a) minimum aggregate deposit ≥$1,000,000; (b) established on behalf of one or more **non-US persons** as direct or beneficial owners; and (c) assigned to or administered by a relationship manager |
| **Senior foreign political figure (PEP)** | A current or former: senior official in the executive, legislative, administrative, military, or judicial branch of a foreign government; senior official of a major foreign political party; senior executive of a foreign government-owned commercial enterprise; plus immediate family members (spouses, parents, siblings, children, spouse's parents and siblings); plus close associates who are widely publicly known to be associated with the PEP |
| **Beneficial owner (Subpart F)** | An individual with a level of control over, or entitlement to, the funds or assets in an account that, as a practical matter, enables the individual to control, manage, or direct the account |
| **Regulated affiliate** | A foreign shell bank that: (a) is an affiliate of a depository institution, credit union, or foreign bank that maintains physical presence in the US or a foreign country; and (b) is subject to supervision by the banking authority in the country of the affiliated institution |
| **Offshore banking licence** | A licence that prohibits the licensed entity from conducting banking activities with citizens of, or in the local currency of, the jurisdiction that issued the licence |

## Correspondent Account Due Diligence (§1010.610)

### Who Is Covered

Covered financial institutions for §1010.610:
- Banks with AML compliance programme requirements under 31 USC 5318(h)
- Broker-dealers registered with the SEC
- Futures commission merchants and introducing brokers registered with CFTC
- Mutual funds **(§1010.605(e)(1))**

### General Due Diligence Programme

A covered FI must establish a **due diligence programme** that includes:
1. Policies, procedures, and controls reasonably designed to detect and report, on an ongoing basis, any known or suspected money laundering activity conducted through or involving any correspondent account with a foreign financial institution
2. Determination of whether any correspondent account is subject to enhanced DD (paragraph (b))
3. Assessment of the ML risk of each correspondent account based on all relevant factors:
   - Nature of the FFI's business and the markets it serves
   - Type, purpose, and anticipated activity of the account
   - Nature and duration of the relationship with the FFI
   - Anti-money laundering and supervisory regime of the FFI's jurisdiction
   - Information about the FFI's AML record **(§1010.610(a))**

### Enhanced Due Diligence — When Mandatory (§1010.610(b)–(c))

Enhanced DD **must** be applied to correspondent accounts maintained for a foreign bank that operates under:
1. An **offshore banking licence**
2. A banking licence issued by a country **designated by FATF as non-cooperative** with international AML principles/procedures, and with which the US concurs in that designation
3. A banking licence in a jurisdiction **designated by the Secretary** as warranting special measures due to money laundering concerns

Enhanced DD procedures must include, as appropriate:
- **Enhanced scrutiny** of the correspondent account to guard against ML
- **Transaction monitoring** reasonably designed to detect ML and suspicious activity
- For **payable-through accounts:** Obtaining identity of persons authorised to direct transactions, plus sources and beneficial owners of funds flowing through **(§1010.610(b)(1)(iii)(A))**
- Determining whether the foreign bank **maintains sub-correspondent accounts** for other foreign banks that use the US correspondent account, and if so, taking reasonable steps to assess and mitigate those nested correspondent bank risks **(§1010.610(b)(2))**
- For **non-publicly-traded foreign banks:** Obtaining identity of each **owner** (defined as >10% ownership of any class of voting securities or interests) and the nature and extent of each owner's interest **(§1010.610(b)(3))**

### When Enhanced Due Diligence Cannot Be Performed (§1010.610(d))

The due diligence programme must include procedures for circumstances where appropriate DD or enhanced DD cannot be performed, including when the covered FI should:
- Refuse to open the account
- Suspend transaction activity
- File a suspicious activity report
- Close the account

### Exemptions (§1010.610(g))

A financial institution defined in 31 USC 5312(a)(2) or (c)(1) or §1010.100(t) is exempt from the correspondent account DD requirements of 31 USC 5318(i)(1) pertaining to correspondent accounts.

## Private Banking Account Due Diligence (§1010.620)

### Who Is Covered

Covered financial institutions for §1010.620: banks, commercial banks, trust companies, private bankers, branches of foreign banks in the US, credit unions, savings associations, §25A corporations, and broker-dealers. **(§1010.605(e)(2))**

### Minimum Due Diligence Requirements

For every private banking account (≥$1M, non-US persons), the due diligence programme must:

1. **Ascertain the identity of all nominal and beneficial owners** of the account
2. **Determine whether any owner is a senior foreign political figure (PEP)**
3. **Ascertain the source(s) of funds** deposited into the account and the purpose and expected use of the account
4. **Review account activity** to ensure it is consistent with the information obtained about the client's source of funds, and with the stated purpose and expected use, as needed to guard against ML and report suspicious activity **(§1010.620(b))**

### Enhanced Scrutiny for PEP Accounts (§1010.620(c))

If any nominal or beneficial owner of a private banking account is a **senior foreign political figure**, enhanced scrutiny must be applied. The enhanced scrutiny must be **reasonably designed to detect and report transactions that may involve the proceeds of foreign corruption**.

**Proceeds of foreign corruption** means any asset or property acquired by, through, or on behalf of a senior foreign political figure through:
- Misappropriation, theft, or embezzlement of public funds
- Unlawful conversion of a property of a foreign government
- Acts of bribery or extortion
- Any asset into which such assets have been transformed or converted **(§1010.620(c)(2))**

### When Due Diligence Cannot Be Performed (§1010.620(d))

The programme must include procedures for circumstances where appropriate DD cannot be performed (refuse to open, suspend activity, file SAR, or close account).

## Shell Bank Prohibition (§1010.630)

### The Absolute Rule

A covered financial institution **must not** establish, maintain, administer, or manage a correspondent account in the United States for, or on behalf of, a **foreign shell bank**. **(§1010.630(a)(1)(i))**

A covered FI must also take **reasonable steps** to ensure that any correspondent account it maintains for a foreign bank is **not being used by that foreign bank** to indirectly provide banking services to a foreign shell bank. **(§1010.630(a)(1)(ii))**

**Exception:** The prohibition does not prevent a covered FI from providing correspondent account services to a **regulated affiliate** (a shell bank that is affiliated with a physically-present depository institution and supervised by a banking authority). **(§1010.630(a)(1)(iii))**

### Records of Owners and Agents (§1010.630(a)(2))

A covered FI that maintains a correspondent account for a **non-publicly-traded** foreign bank must maintain records in the United States identifying:
- The **owners** of the foreign bank (any person owning, controlling, or having the power to vote ≥25% of any class of voting securities, or controlling the election of a majority of directors, directly or indirectly)
- The **name and street address of a person in the United States** who is authorised and has agreed to act as agent for service of legal process with respect to the correspondent account

**Exception:** No records required for any foreign bank required to have on file with the Federal Reserve Board Form FR Y-7 identifying its current owners. **(§1010.630(a)(2)(ii))**

### Three-Year Recertification Safe Harbour (§1010.630(b))

A covered FI is deemed in compliance with the prohibition if it **obtains a certification or recertification from the foreign bank at least once every three years**. The certification must confirm that the foreign bank does not operate as a shell bank and confirms compliance with the prohibition.

**Safe harbour termination triggers:** If the covered FI knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that any information in the certification is no longer correct, the FI must request verification or correction and, if the foreign bank fails to verify or correct, take appropriate measures or obtain correct information. **(§1010.630(c))**

### Account Closure Requirements (§1010.630(d))

If the certification is not obtained within **30 calendar days** of account establishment, and renewed at least every 3 years, the covered FI must close all correspondent accounts with that foreign bank within a **commercially reasonable time**, not permitting the foreign bank to establish new positions or execute any new transactions (other than closure transactions).

A covered FI **may not reestablish** a closed account and may not establish a new correspondent account until a valid certification is obtained. **(§1010.630(d)(4))**

**Limitation on liability:** A covered FI is not liable for terminating a correspondent account in compliance with these requirements. **(§1010.630(d)(5))**

### Recordkeeping (§1010.630(e))

Retain the original of any document provided by a foreign bank (including all certifications) for **at least 5 years** after the covered FI no longer maintains any correspondent account for that foreign bank.

### Foreign Bank Records Subpoena (§1010.670)

The Secretary of the Treasury or the Attorney General may issue a summons or subpoena to any foreign bank with a US correspondent account for records related to that account, including records maintained outside the US. If the foreign bank fails to comply or to initiate US court proceedings contesting the summons:

The covered FI must **terminate the correspondent relationship within 10 business days** of written notice from the Secretary or AG (after consultation with the other). **(§1010.670(d))**

- **Limitation on liability:** No liability for such termination **(§1010.670(e))**
- **Failure to terminate:** Civil penalty up to **$10,000 per day** until terminated **(§1010.670(f))**

## Special Measures — Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act (§§1010.651–1010.664)

FinCEN has designated specific foreign banks and financial institutions as primary money laundering concerns and imposed special measures prohibiting US financial institutions from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for them, and requiring enhanced due diligence to prevent their indirect use. The standard pattern for each designation is:

**Prohibition on accounts:** No covered FI may open or maintain a correspondent account in the United States for, or on behalf of, the designated institution.

**Prohibition on transactions involving designated institution:** Covered FIs must take reasonable steps to not process transactions for the correspondent account of a foreign bank that involve the designated institution.

**Special due diligence of correspondent accounts:** Covered FIs must apply special DD to their foreign correspondent accounts reasonably designed to guard against use to process transactions involving the designated institution, at minimum including:
- **(A)** Notifying correspondent account holders that the covered FI knows or has reason to believe provides services to the designated institution that such correspondents may not provide that institution with access to the US correspondent account
- **(B)** Taking reasonable steps to identify any use of foreign correspondent accounts by the designated institution

**Termination:** A covered FI that knows or has reason to believe a foreign bank's correspondent account has been or is being used to process transactions involving the designated institution must take all appropriate steps to further investigate and prevent such access, including notification and, where necessary, termination of the correspondent account.

| Designation | Section | Notes |
|------------|---------|-------|
| **Burma** (Burmese banking institutions) | §1010.651 | Terminates existing accounts; exception for OFAC-licensed transactions |
| **Commercial Bank of Syria** (including Syrian Lebanese Commercial Bank) | §1010.653 | Prohibition on direct and indirect use |
| **FBME Bank, Ltd.** (all branches, offices, subsidiaries, any jurisdiction) | §1010.658 | [81 FR 18493, March 31, 2016] |
| **North Korea** (North Korean banking institutions and financial institutions) | §1010.659 | No accounts; no transactions involving North Korean FIs; [81 FR 78721, November 9, 2016] |
| **Bank of Dandong** (all subsidiaries, branches, offices) | §1010.660 | [82 FR 51765, November 8, 2017] |

| **Al-Huda Bank** (all subsidiaries, branches, offices) | §1010.663 | [89 FR 55058, July 3, 2024] |
| **Huione Group** (includes Haowang Guarantee, Huione Pay PLC, Huione Crypto) | §1010.664 | [90 FR 48311, October 16, 2025] |

### Iran-Specific Note (§1010.661(b)(1))

For Iranian financial institutions, the prohibition specifically notes: covered FIs should **block and report to OFAC** any accounts that are blocked pursuant to any OFAC sanctions authority. Such blocked accounts must be maintained in accordance with the OFAC Reporting Procedures and Penalties Regulations, 31 CFR Part 501. This interplay with OFAC is unique to the Iran special measure.

## Key Points for Agents

- **The shell bank prohibition is absolute; there is no risk-based exception.** No US FI may ever maintain a correspondent account for a foreign shell bank regardless of perceived risk level. The only permissible exception is for regulated affiliates (shell bank affiliated with and supervised through a physically-present institution). The 3-year recertification safe harbour is a compliance mechanism — not an exception to the prohibition. **(§§1010.630(a), (b))**
- **Correspondent account DD is risk-based; enhanced DD for three categories is mandatory and non-discretionary.** The trigger categories (offshore banking licence, FATF non-cooperative, Secretary-designated for special measures) are bright lines — FIs cannot substitute a general risk assessment for the mandatory enhanced DD programme. **(§1010.610(b)–(c))**
- **Private banking accounts require PEP screening as a regulatory minimum, not a best practice.** Any private banking account (≥$1M, non-US persons, with a relationship manager) must include screening for senior foreign political figures, with enhanced scrutiny for corruption proceeds if any owner is a PEP. **(§1010.620(b)–(c))**
- **Active Section 311 special measures are institution-specific prohibitions, not OFAC sanctions.** They prohibit correspondent accounts for specific named institutions and require pass-through DD even where the named institution is accessing US correspondent banking through third-party chains. Violation can result in civil penalties up to $1,776,364 per violation. **(§§1010.651–1010.664, 1010.821)**
- **A foreign bank's failure to comply with a Treasury/AG subpoena triggers a mandatory 10-business-day termination obligation on the US FI.** Failure to terminate incurs a $10,000-per-day civil penalty. The termination is treated as no-liability for the US FI. **(§1010.670)**
