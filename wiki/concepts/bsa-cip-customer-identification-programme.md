# BSA — Customer Identification Programme (CIP)

## Overview

§1020.220 requires every bank with an AML compliance programme under 31 USC 5318(h) to implement a written Customer Identification Programme (CIP). The CIP must be appropriate for the bank's size and type of business and must be part of the bank's AML compliance programme. The CIP governs what identifying information must be collected, how identity must be verified, when government lists must be checked, what records must be kept, and how customers must be notified. **(§1020.220(a)(1))**

---

## Who Is a "Customer" for CIP Purposes

For §1020.220, **customer** means a person that opens a new account. It also includes an individual who opens an account for a minor or a non-legal entity (such as a civic club). **(§1020.100(b)(1))**

**Customer does NOT include:** **(§1020.100(b)(2))**
- A financial institution regulated by a Federal functional regulator or a state bank regulator
- A person described in the CTR exempt persons categories (§1020.315(b)(2) through (b)(4))
- A person with an **existing account** at the bank, provided the bank has a reasonable belief it knows the true identity of the person

**Account** for CIP purposes excludes: check-cashing, wire transfer, or sale of a check or money order (no formal banking relationship); accounts acquired through mergers/acquisitions; ERISA employee benefit plan accounts. **(§1020.100(a)(2))**

---

## Required Identifying Information — §1020.220(a)(2)(i)

Prior to opening an account, the CIP must specify the identifying information that will be obtained from each customer. At minimum, the bank must obtain:

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| **Name** | Full legal name |
| **Date of birth** | For individuals only |
| **Address** | For individuals: residential or business street address; if no street address, an APO/FPO box number, or the residential/business address of next of kin or another contact individual. For non-individuals (corporations, partnerships, trusts): a principal place of business, local office, or other physical location |
| **Identification number** | For US persons: **taxpayer identification number (TIN)**. For non-US persons: TIN; or **passport number and country of issuance**; or **alien identification card number**; or **number and country of issuance of any other government-issued document** evidencing nationality or residence and bearing a photograph or similar safeguard |

**(§1020.220(a)(2)(i)(A))**

**Special cases:**
- **TIN applied for but not received:** The CIP may include procedures to open an account before receiving the TIN, provided the CIP requires the bank to confirm the application was filed before account opening and to obtain the TIN within a reasonable period thereafter. **(§1020.220(a)(2)(i)(B))**
- **Credit card accounts:** The bank may obtain required information from a third-party source prior to extending credit. **(§1020.220(a)(2)(i)(C))**
- **Foreign business or enterprise with no identification number:** The bank must request alternative government-issued documentation certifying the existence of the business or enterprise. **(§1020.220(a)(2)(i)(A)(4)(ii), Note)**

---

## Identity Verification — §1020.220(a)(2)(ii)

The CIP must include risk-based procedures for **verifying the identity of each customer to the extent reasonable and practicable**. The procedures must enable the bank to form a **reasonable belief** that it knows the true identity of each customer. **(§1020.220(a)(2))**

### Documentary Verification — §1020.220(a)(2)(ii)(A)

For banks relying on documents:

| Customer type | Acceptable documents |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Individuals | Unexpired government-issued identification evidencing **nationality or residence** and bearing a **photograph or similar safeguard** — e.g., driver's licence or passport |
| Non-individuals (corporations, partnerships, trusts) | Documents showing the **existence of the entity** — e.g., certified articles of incorporation, government-issued business licence, partnership agreement, trust instrument |

### Non-Documentary Verification — §1020.220(a)(2)(ii)(B)

Non-documentary methods include: contacting the customer; independently verifying identity by comparing customer-provided information against information from a **consumer reporting agency**, **public database**, or other source; checking references with other financial institutions; obtaining a financial statement.

The CIP must include non-documentary procedures that address circumstances where:
- An individual is **unable to present an unexpired government-issued photo ID**
- The bank is **not familiar with the documents presented**
- The account is opened **without the customer appearing in person**
- The bank is otherwise presented with circumstances increasing the risk it cannot verify identity through documents

### Additional Verification for Non-Individual Customers — §1020.220(a)(2)(ii)(C)

Where a new account is opened by a non-individual (corporation, partnership, trust) and the bank cannot verify the entity's identity through documentary or non-documentary methods, the bank will obtain information about the **individuals with authority or control over the account** (including signatories) in order to verify the entity's identity.

---

## Failure to Verify — §1020.220(a)(2)(iii)

The CIP must include procedures for circumstances where the bank **cannot form a reasonable belief** that it knows the true identity of a customer. These procedures must address:

- **(A)** When the bank should **not open** an account
- **(B)** The terms under which a customer may **use an account while the bank attempts to verify** identity
- **(C)** When the bank should **close an account** after attempts to verify identity have failed
- **(D)** When the bank should **file a Suspicious Activity Report** in accordance with applicable law and regulation

---

## Recordkeeping — §1020.220(a)(3)

The CIP must include procedures for making and maintaining a record of all information obtained. Required records include:

- **(A)** All identifying information about the customer obtained under §1020.220(a)(2)(i) (name, DOB, address, ID number)
- **(B)** A description of any document relied on for documentary verification — type of document, any ID number contained in it, the place of issuance, and (if any) the date of issuance and expiration date
- **(C)** A description of the methods and results of any non-documentary verification measures taken
- **(D)** A description of the resolution of any substantive discrepancy discovered when verifying identifying information

**Retention:**
- Information in (A): **5 years** after the date the account is **closed** (for credit card accounts: 5 years after the account is closed or becomes dormant)
- Information in (B), (C), and (D): **5 years** after the record is made **(§1020.220(a)(3)(ii))**

---

## Government List Comparison — §1020.220(a)(4)

The CIP must include procedures for determining whether a customer appears on **any list of known or suspected terrorists or terrorist organisations issued by any Federal government agency** and designated as such by Treasury in consultation with Federal functional regulators.

The procedures must require the bank to make this determination **within a reasonable period of time** after the account is opened, or **earlier** if required by the applicable list or another Federal law or regulation. The bank must follow all Federal directives issued in connection with such lists. **(§1020.220(a)(4))**

---

## Customer Notice — §1020.220(a)(5)

The CIP must include procedures for providing bank customers with **adequate notice** that the bank is requesting information to verify their identities.

**Adequate notice** means the bank generally describes the identification requirements of §1020.220 and provides the notice in a manner reasonably designed to ensure the customer is able to view it before or when opening an account — for example, by posting a notice in the lobby, on the bank's website, including it on account applications, or providing written or oral notice.

**Sample notice (provided in the regulation):**
> *IMPORTANT INFORMATION ABOUT PROCEDURES FOR OPENING A NEW ACCOUNT*
> To help the government fight the funding of terrorism and money laundering activities, Federal law requires all financial institutions to obtain, verify, and record information that identifies each person who opens an account.
> What this means for you: When you open an account, we will ask for your name, address, date of birth, and other information that will allow us to identify you. We may also ask to see your driver's licence or other identifying documents.

**(§1020.220(a)(5))**

---

## Reliance on Another Financial Institution — §1020.220(a)(6)

The CIP may include procedures specifying when a bank will rely on the performance by another financial institution (including an affiliate) of any CIP procedures, for any customer of the bank that is opening, or has opened, an account or a similar formal banking or business relationship with the other institution, provided:

- **(i)** Such reliance is **reasonable under the circumstances**
- **(ii)** The other FI is **subject to a rule implementing 31 USC 5318(h)** and is **regulated by a Federal functional regulator**
- **(iii)** The other FI enters into a **contract requiring it to certify annually** that it has implemented its AML programme and that it will perform (or its agent will perform) the specified CIP requirements

**(§1020.220(a)(6))**

---

## Exemptions — §1020.220(b)

The appropriate Federal functional regulator, with the concurrence of the Secretary of the Treasury, may exempt any bank or type of account from CIP requirements. The regulator and Secretary shall consider whether the exemption is consistent with the purposes of the BSA and with safe and sound banking. **(§1020.220(b))**

## CIP vs CDD Rule — Key Distinctions

| Feature | CIP (§1020.220) | CDD Rule (§1010.230) |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| When triggered | Every **new account** | Every new account opened by a **legal entity customer** |
| Who is covered | All customers (individuals and entities) | Legal entity customers only |
| Core obligation | Collect and verify 4 identifying elements | Collect and verify BO information (25% ownership + 1 control prong) |
| Verification standard | Reasonable belief of true identity | Documents, non-documentary, or certification |
| Retention | 5 years from account closure | 5 years from account closure |
| Government list check | Required | Not separately required (but OFAC obligations apply) |

Both requirements apply when a legal entity customer opens a new account. The CIP collects entity-level identifying information; the CDD rule collects individual beneficial owner information.

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **CIP applies to every new account opening by every customer — including legal entity customers.** The CDD rule (§1010.230) then layers on top for legal entity customers to collect BO information. Both are required simultaneously; neither satisfies the other. **(§§1020.220, 1010.230)**
- **Non-US persons have four acceptable identification number alternatives.** TIN; passport number + country of issuance; alien identification card number; or any government-issued document with photo evidencing nationality or residence. A foreign business with no identification number at all requires alternative government-issued documentation certifying its existence. **(§1020.220(a)(2)(i)(A)(4))**
- **Non-documentary verification is required (not optional) in four specific circumstances.** When the customer cannot present an unexpired government-issued photo ID; when documents are unfamiliar; when the account is opened without the customer appearing in person; or when other circumstances increase the risk that documents alone are insufficient. Non-documentary methods must be described in the written CIP. **(§1020.220(a)(2)(ii)(B))**
- **The 5-year retention clock runs from account closure, not from the date of collection.** This means CIP records for long-standing accounts may need to be retained for many years beyond collection. For credit card accounts, the clock starts from closure or dormancy (whichever is later). **(§1020.220(a)(3)(ii))**
- **Reliance on another FI for CIP requires an annual certification contract — not just a one-time agreement.** The other institution must certify annually that it has implemented its AML programme and will perform the specified CIP requirements. The relying bank must ensure reliance is reasonable and the other FI is regulated by a Federal functional regulator. **(§1020.220(a)(6))**
