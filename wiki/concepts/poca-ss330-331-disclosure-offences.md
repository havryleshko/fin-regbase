# POCA 2002 ss.330–331 — Statutory Disclosure Offences

## Overview

Sections 330 and 331 of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 create the two criminal offences that underpin the UK's suspicious activity reporting (SAR) regime for the regulated sector. They operate as a two-stage pipeline:

- **s.330** — the offence committed by an **employee or person in the regulated sector** who fails to disclose a suspicion of money laundering to their nominated officer (or directly to the NCA).
- **s.331** — the offence committed by the **nominated officer (MLRO)** who receives an internal disclosure under s.330 and fails to pass it to the NCA.

Both sections require four conditions to be satisfied before an offence is committed. Both specify what the required disclosure must contain. Both provide defences. The conditions and defences differ in important ways.

The existing wiki page [[sar-consent-tipping-off]] covers the operational framework — suspicion thresholds, the DAML consent procedure, and tipping off — sourced from JMLSG guidance. This page provides the statutory foundation that JMLSG guidance implements.

## Section 330 — The Staff Offence

### Four Conditions (s.330(1)–(4))

A person commits an offence only if **all four conditions** are satisfied:

**First condition (s.330(2)):** The person—
- (a) knows or suspects; or
- (b) has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting;

that another person is engaged in money laundering.

**Second condition (s.330(3)):** The information or other matter on which the knowledge/suspicion is based, or which gives reasonable grounds for it, came to the person **in the course of a business in the regulated sector**.

**Third condition (s.330(3A)):** Either—
- (a) the person **can identify** the other person engaged in money laundering, or the whereabouts of any of the laundered property; or
- (b) the person **believes, or it is reasonable to expect them to believe**, that the information will or may assist in identifying that other person or the whereabouts of the laundered property.

**Fourth condition (s.330(4)):** The person **does not make the required disclosure** to—
- (a) a nominated officer; or
- (b) a person authorised by the Director General of the National Crime Agency;

**as soon as is practicable** after the information comes to them.

### The Third Condition Is a Gate

The third condition (s.330(3A)) limits the s.330 offence to situations where the person can actually assist identification. A person who has a suspicion but genuinely cannot identify the suspect and cannot see how their information would assist identification does not commit the offence by failing to report. In practice, this threshold is low — the "believes, or it is reasonable to expect them to believe, that the information will or may assist" limb catches most situations where a suspicion is formed in the regulated sector.

### What the Required Disclosure Must Contain (s.330(5))

The required disclosure is a disclosure of:
- **(a)** the **identity of the other person** mentioned in s.330(2), **if the person knows it**;
- **(b)** the **whereabouts of the laundered property**, so far as the person knows it; and
- **(c)** the **information or other matter** mentioned in s.330(3) — i.e., the underlying information on which the knowledge or suspicion is based.

**S.330(5A):** The laundered property is the property forming the subject-matter of the money laundering that the person knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, the other person to be engaged in.

The disclosure obligation is bounded by what the person knows. Elements (a) and (b) are qualified by "if he knows it" / "so far as he knows it". Element (c) is not qualified — the underlying information must always be disclosed.

### Defences (s.330(6)–(7C))

A person does not commit an offence under s.330 in four situations:

#### (i) Reasonable Excuse (s.330(6)(a))

The person has a **reasonable excuse** for not making the required disclosure. What constitutes reasonable excuse is not defined in POCA and has not been extensively litigated. It is a narrow, fact-specific defence.

#### (ii) Legal Professional Privilege (s.330(6)(b); (10)–(11))

A **professional legal adviser** or **relevant professional adviser** (accountant, auditor, or tax adviser who is a member of a qualifying professional body — see s.330(14)) does not commit an offence where:
- (i) if they know the identity of the suspect or whereabouts of the laundered property, they know it because of information that came to them **in privileged circumstances**; or
- (ii) the underlying information itself came to them in privileged circumstances.

Information comes in **privileged circumstances** (s.330(10)) when communicated:
- (a) by a client (or their representative) in connection with the giving of legal advice to that client;
- (b) by a person seeking legal advice from the adviser; or
- (c) by a person in connection with legal proceedings or contemplated legal proceedings.

**Privilege does not apply** where the information was communicated with the **intention of furthering a criminal purpose** (s.330(11)). A client instructing their lawyer to help structure a transaction to conceal proceeds of crime cannot claim privilege for that communication.

**s.330(9A):** A legal or relevant professional adviser who discloses information to their nominated officer solely for the purpose of obtaining advice about making a disclosure — and does not intend it to be a disclosure under s.330 — is not treated as having made a disclosure to a nominated officer. This protects internal privilege-advice-seeking from becoming an inadvertent SAR.

#### (iii) Untrained Staff (s.330(7))

A person does not commit an offence if:
- (a) they **do not know or suspect** that another person is engaged in money laundering; **and**
- (b) their employer **has not provided them with the training** specified by the Secretary of State for the purposes of this section.

Both conditions must be satisfied. This defence does not apply to a person who subjectively suspects money laundering regardless of whether they were trained. It is directed at genuinely unaware staff in firms that have failed to deliver required AML training.

**Operational significance:** The untrained-staff defence is a direct statutory incentive for firms to deliver compliant AML training. A firm that fails to train staff cannot rely on this defence for its employees — but the employees themselves may. Conversely, trained staff who form no suspicion commit no offence even without a report.

**s.330(7B):** An equivalent defence applies to a person employed by or in partnership with a legal or relevant professional adviser to provide assistance or support, where the information came to them in connection with that support and came to the adviser in privileged circumstances.

#### (iv) Overseas Conduct Exclusion (s.330(7C))

A person does not commit an offence if:
- (a) they **know, or believe on reasonable grounds**, that the money laundering is occurring in a particular country or territory outside the UK; **and**
- (b) the money laundering—
  - (i) **is not unlawful** under the criminal law applying in that country or territory; **and**
  - (ii) **is not of a description prescribed** by the Secretary of State.

Both limbs of (b) must be satisfied. A firm cannot rely on this exclusion merely because the conduct is lawful abroad if it has been specifically prescribed by the Secretary of State. The exclusion requires positive knowledge or reasonable belief about the foreign jurisdiction's law — it does not apply by default.

### What Is a Disclosure to a Nominated Officer (s.330(9))

A disclosure to a nominated officer is a disclosure:
- (a) made to a person **nominated by the alleged offender's employer** to receive disclosures under s.330; and
- (b) made **in the course of the alleged offender's employment**.

## Section 331 — The MLRO Offence

### Four Conditions (s.331(1)–(4))

A person **nominated to receive disclosures under s.330** (i.e., the MLRO / nominated officer) commits an offence if all four conditions are satisfied:

**First condition (s.331(2)):** The nominated officer—
- (a) knows or suspects; or
- (b) has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting;

that another person is engaged in money laundering.

**Second condition (s.331(3)):** The information on which the knowledge/suspicion is based came to them **in consequence of a disclosure made under s.330** — i.e., from an internal report by a member of staff.

**Third condition (s.331(3A)):** One of the following:
- (a) the nominated officer **knows the identity** of the suspect or the whereabouts of any laundered property, **in consequence of the s.330 disclosure**;
- (b) that other person, or the whereabouts of any laundered property, **can be identified from the information or other matter** received; or
- (c) the nominated officer **believes, or it is reasonable to expect them to believe**, that the information will or may assist in identifying that person or the whereabouts of the laundered property.

**Fourth condition (s.331(4)):** The nominated officer **does not make the required disclosure** to a person authorised by the Director General of the NCA **as soon as is practicable** after the information comes to them.

### Key Differences from Section 330

| Point | s.330 (staff) | s.331 (nominated officer) |
|-------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Second condition | Information came in course of **business in the regulated sector** | Information came **in consequence of a s.330 disclosure** |
| Third condition (3A)(a) | Person **can identify** suspect/whereabouts | Nominated officer **knows** identity/whereabouts **from the disclosure** |
| Disclosure recipient | Nominated officer **or** NCA | NCA **only** |
| LPP defence | Yes — s.330(6)(b) | No |
| Untrained staff defence | Yes — s.330(7) | No |

The MLRO's third condition in limb (a) requires actual knowledge derived from the internal disclosure — not merely the ability to identify. Limbs (b) and (c) are forward-looking: can the information enable identification, or is it reasonable to expect the MLRO believes it may assist?

### What the Required Disclosure Must Contain (s.331(5))

The nominated officer's required disclosure to the NCA must contain:
- **(a)** the **identity of the suspect**, if that identity was disclosed to the nominated officer under s.330;
- **(b)** the **whereabouts of the laundered property**, so far as disclosed under s.330; and
- **(c)** the **information or other matter** mentioned in s.331(3) — the underlying information forming the basis of suspicion.

The scope of elements (a) and (b) is bounded by what was disclosed internally under s.330. The MLRO is not obliged to disclose identity or location information that was not included in the internal report. Element (c) is unqualified.

### Defences (s.331(6)–(6A))

#### (i) Reasonable Excuse (s.331(6))

The nominated officer has a **reasonable excuse** for not making the required disclosure.

#### (ii) Overseas Conduct Exclusion (s.331(6A))

Identical to s.330(7C): no offence where the nominated officer knows or believes on reasonable grounds that the money laundering is occurring in a jurisdiction where it is not unlawful under local criminal law, and not prescribed by the Secretary of State.

The MLRO has no LPP defence and no untrained-staff defence. Those are staff-level protections.

### JMLSG Guidance as a Statutory Court Consideration (ss.330(8); 331(7))

In deciding whether a person committed an offence under either section, the court **must consider** whether they followed any relevant guidance which was:
- (a) **issued by a supervisory authority or any other appropriate body**;
- (b) **approved by the Treasury**; and
- (c) **published in a manner approved as appropriate** to bring it to the attention of affected persons.

This is the statutory basis for JMLSG guidance having legal weight in s.330 and s.331 prosecutions. JMLSG Guidance Part I is the principal Treasury-approved guidance for the regulated financial sector. Following it is not a complete defence — the court "must consider" it, not automatically exonerate compliance with it — but non-compliance with JMLSG guidance significantly increases prosecution risk.

## Key Points for Agents

- **The identification gate (ss.330(3A); 331(3A)) is a precondition for both offences.** Neither the staff member nor the MLRO commits an offence by failing to report unless they can identify (or the information may assist identification of) the suspect or the laundered property. In practice this gate is low — the "will or may assist identification" limb is broad — but it means a genuine inability to identify anyone from the information is a complete answer to a charge under either section.
- **The required disclosure has three fixed statutory elements (ss.330(5); 331(5)).** Identity (if known), whereabouts of laundered property (so far as known), and the underlying information. These are the minimum statutory contents. A SAR that omits the underlying information is deficient on its face regardless of JMLSG guidance compliance. Identity and whereabouts are qualified by knowledge — the person is not required to disclose what they do not know.
- **LPP is a statutory defence to s.330 — but only for legal and qualifying professional advisers, and not where information was provided to further a criminal purpose (s.330(6)(b); (10)–(11)).** Accountants, auditors, and tax advisers who are members of qualifying professional bodies are "relevant professional advisers" and may rely on the LPP defence in the same circumstances as solicitors. The crime-furtherance exception overrides privilege entirely.
- **The untrained-staff defence (s.330(7)) is a direct incentive for mandatory AML training.** A staff member who has not been given required training and does not subjectively suspect money laundering commits no s.330 offence by failing to report. But a trained staff member who forms no suspicion also commits no offence. The defence protects untrained staff who genuinely see nothing — it does not protect trained staff who fail to report genuine suspicions, nor does it protect the firm from regulatory breach for failing to train.
- **The overseas conduct exclusion (ss.330(7C); 331(6A)) applies where the ML is lawful in the jurisdiction where it occurs — but only if not prescribed by the Secretary of State.** This matters for cross-border correspondent banking, trade finance, and cryptoasset transactions where the underlying activity may be lawful in the originating jurisdiction. The exclusion requires positive knowledge or reasonable belief about foreign law — it is not available by default, and the two limbs of s.330(7C)(b) are conjunctive.
- **JMLSG guidance has statutory weight in prosecutions (ss.330(8); 331(7)).** The court must consider whether the defendant followed Treasury-approved guidance. This is not automatic exoneration — the court must consider it, not apply it as a safe harbour. But it creates a strong incentive to document compliance with JMLSG processes, particularly the nominated officer's evaluation and decision-making records.
