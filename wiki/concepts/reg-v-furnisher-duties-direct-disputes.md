# Regulation V — Furnisher Duties and Direct Disputes (12 CFR Part 1022, Subpart E)

## Overview

Subpart E (§§1022.40–1022.43) implements FCRA §623 (15 USC 1681s-2), which establishes duties for persons who furnish information to consumer reporting agencies (CRAs). The subpart requires furnishers to maintain written policies and procedures for the accuracy and integrity of furnished information, and to investigate "direct disputes" — disputes submitted directly by consumers to the furnisher rather than to a CRA.

This subpart applies to any lender, creditor, servicer, debt collector, or other entity that provides account information to CRAs. It does not apply to persons who provide information solely to obtain a consumer report, persons acting as a CRA, the consumer themselves, or character reference sources.

---

## Who Is a Furnisher? (§1022.41(c))

A **furnisher** is an entity that furnishes information relating to consumers to one or more CRAs for inclusion in a consumer report. An entity is **not** a furnisher if it:
1. Provides information to a CRA solely to obtain a consumer report (e.g., an employer pulling a background check)
2. Is acting as a CRA itself (as defined in FCRA §603(f))
3. Is the consumer to whom the information pertains
4. Is a neighbour, friend, associate, or other character reference responding to a CRA's specific request

---

## Core Definitions (§1022.41)

**Accuracy (§1022.41(a)):** Information is accurate if it:
1. Reflects the terms of and liability for the account or other relationship;
2. Reflects the consumer's performance and other conduct with respect to the account or other relationship; and
3. Identifies the appropriate consumer.

All three conditions must be satisfied. Accuracy is not merely avoiding false statements — it requires the information to correctly identify the consumer *and* correctly represent both the account terms and the consumer's conduct.

**Integrity (§1022.41(d)):** Information has integrity if it:
1. Is substantiated by the furnisher's records at the time it is furnished;
2. Is furnished in a form and manner designed to minimise the likelihood that it may be incorrectly reflected in a consumer report; and
3. Includes all information in the furnisher's possession about the account or relationship that the Bureau has determined (in Appendix E) would be materially misleading to omit.

The integrity requirement is violated not just by inaccurate information but also by **omitting** information whose absence would mislead — for example, omitting that a debt is disputed.

**Direct dispute (§1022.41(b)):** A dispute submitted directly to a furnisher (including a debt collector) by a consumer concerning the accuracy of any information contained in a consumer report that pertains to an account or relationship the furnisher has or had with the consumer.

---

## Reasonable Policies and Procedures for Accuracy and Integrity (§1022.42)

Each furnisher must:
1. **Establish and implement** reasonable written policies and procedures regarding the accuracy and integrity of information it furnishes to CRAs;
2. **Consider the guidelines** in Appendix E in developing those policies and procedures, and incorporate those guidelines that are appropriate to the nature, size, complexity, and scope of the furnisher's activities; and
3. **Review and update** the policies and procedures periodically to ensure continued effectiveness.

**Appendix E guidelines** address: identifying the consumer and account; the types of information furnished; methods of furnishing; procedures for verifying information before furnishing; processes for updating and correcting information; and oversight of third-party vendors who assist in furnishing.

There is no prescribed format or length for furnisher policies and procedures. A small fintech furnishing information on a single product line has different requirements than a large multi-product bank. The standard is one of reasonableness scaled to the furnisher's operations.

## Direct Disputes — Obligation to Investigate (§1022.43)

### Scope: What Disputes Must Be Investigated

A furnisher must conduct a reasonable investigation of a direct dispute if it relates to: **(§1022.43(a))**

1. The consumer's **liability for a credit account or other debt** — e.g., disputes that the debt belongs to the consumer at all, that identity theft or fraud occurred, or that the consumer is an authorised user only
2. The **terms of a credit account or other debt** — e.g., disputes about the principal balance, credit limit, scheduled payment amount, or account type
3. The consumer's **performance or conduct** regarding the account — e.g., disputes about the current payment status, high balance, date a payment was made, amount of a payment, or date an account was opened or closed
4. **Any other information** contained in a consumer report regarding the account that bears on the consumer's creditworthiness, credit standing, credit capacity, character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living

### Exceptions — Disputes a Furnisher Need Not Investigate (§1022.43(b))

A furnisher is **not** required to investigate a direct dispute if:
1. The dispute relates to:
   - (i) The consumer's identifying information **other than** a dispute relating to liability for a credit account (name, DOB, SSN, telephone number, address)
   - (ii) The identity of past or present employers
   - (iii) Inquiries or requests for a consumer report
   - (iv) Information derived from public records — judgments, bankruptcies, liens — **unless** the furnisher provided that information with an account or relationship with the consumer
   - (v) Information related to fraud alerts or active duty alerts
   - (vi) Information provided to a CRA by another furnisher
2. The furnisher has a reasonable belief that the dispute is submitted by, prepared on behalf of, or submitted on a form supplied by a credit repair organisation (as defined by 15 USC 1679a(3) or §403(3)(B)(i) of the Credit Repair Organizations Act)

### Where the Consumer Must Submit the Dispute (§1022.43(c))

A furnisher must investigate a direct dispute **only** if the consumer submits the dispute notice to one of the following addresses:
1. The address of the furnisher provided in a consumer report relating to the consumer
2. An address clearly and conspicuously specified by the furnisher for submitting direct disputes, provided to the consumer in writing or electronically (if the consumer agreed to electronic delivery)
3. **Any business address** of the furnisher, if the furnisher has not provided and specified an address under (1) or (2)

This means a furnisher that fails to designate a dispute address has no right to reject disputes sent to any business address.

### Required Content of the Consumer's Dispute Notice (§1022.43(d))

For a dispute notice to trigger the investigation obligation, it must include:
1. Sufficient information to identify the account or relationship in dispute (account number, consumer name, address, telephone number)
2. The specific information the consumer is disputing and an explanation of the basis for the dispute
3. All supporting documentation or information reasonably required to substantiate the dispute (e.g., copy of relevant portion of consumer report, police report, fraud/identity theft affidavit, court order, account statements)

### Furnisher's Duties Upon Receiving a Valid Direct Dispute (§1022.43(e))

After receiving a dispute notice that meets the address and content requirements:

1. **Conduct a reasonable investigation** with respect to the disputed information
2. **Review all relevant information** provided by the consumer with the dispute notice
3. **Complete the investigation** before the expiration of the period under FCRA §611(a)(1) (15 USC 1681i(a)(1)) within which a CRA would be required to complete its action if the consumer had disputed the information through the CRA — generally **30 days** (or 45 days if the consumer provides additional information)
4. If the investigation finds the reported information was inaccurate: **promptly notify each CRA** to which the furnisher provided inaccurate information, and **provide any correction** necessary to make the information accurate

### Frivolous or Irrelevant Disputes (§1022.43(f))

A furnisher is not required to investigate if it has reasonably determined the dispute is frivolous or irrelevant. A dispute qualifies as frivolous or irrelevant if:
1. The consumer did not provide sufficient information to investigate the disputed information as required by §1022.43(d)
2. The dispute is substantially the same as a dispute previously submitted — either directly or through a CRA — with respect to which the furnisher has already satisfied applicable requirements, **unless** the new dispute includes information listed in §1022.43(d) that was not previously provided
3. One or more of the exceptions in §1022.43(b) applies

**Notice of frivolous determination:** The furnisher must notify the consumer of the determination not later than **5 business days** after making it, by mail or (if authorised by the consumer) by other available means.

**Contents of frivolous notice:** Must include the reasons for the determination and identify any information required to investigate the disputed information. A standardised form describing the general nature of such information may be used.

## Practical Implications for Fintech Furnishers

**Dispute address designation:** Fintechs that furnish credit data should designate a specific, clearly communicated dispute address. Failure to do so means the consumer may send disputes to any business address and trigger investigation obligations.

**30-day investigation window:** The FCRA §611(a)(1) period is the operative deadline. For most disputes this is 30 days; it extends to 45 days if the consumer provides additional information during the investigation.

**CRA notification on correction:** If an investigation reveals an error, the furnisher must notify every CRA to which it provided the inaccurate information — not just the one the consumer mentioned. If a furnisher reports to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, corrections must go to all three.

**Accuracy ≠ integrity:** A furnisher can be technically accurate but still violate the integrity requirement by omitting material information — for example, reporting a debt balance accurately but omitting that the account is currently subject to a dispute, or omitting a settlement agreement that extinguished part of the balance.

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **Furnisher duties apply whenever an entity sends consumer account information to a CRA** — regardless of whether the entity is a bank, fintech lender, servicer, or debt collector. The exception is narrow: providing information solely to obtain a report (not to report on a consumer) does not make one a furnisher. **(§1022.41(c))**
- **Direct disputes go to the furnisher, not the CRA, and trigger independent investigation obligations.** The furnisher cannot redirect a direct dispute to the CRA — it must investigate itself, within the FCRA §611(a)(1) timeframe (typically 30 days). **(§1022.43(a), (e))**
- **If a furnisher has not specified a dispute address, it must accept disputes at any business address.** Fintechs should designate a dedicated dispute address on their consumer report trade lines and in their consumer-facing disclosures to control where disputes arrive. **(§1022.43(c)(3))**
- **A frivolous determination requires a 5-business-day notice to the consumer.** Simply deciding not to investigate without notifying the consumer is itself a violation. The notice must explain why and identify what information is needed. **(§1022.43(f)(2))**
- **Omission of material information is an integrity failure.** A furnisher who omits information whose absence would be materially misleading (e.g., failing to report that a debt was discharged in bankruptcy, or that a dispute is pending) violates the integrity standard even if the reported information is not technically false. **(§1022.41(d)(3))**
