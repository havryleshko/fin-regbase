# Dodd-Frank Act Title X — Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection

## Overview

Title X of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (Public Law 111-203, enacted July 21, 2010) established the Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection (CFPB) and transferred federal consumer financial rulemaking and supervisory authority from seven predecessor agencies to the new Bureau. Title X contains 8 subtitles (A–H) and spans §§1001–1100H of the Act (124 Stat. 1955–2113).

The Bureau became operational on the "designated transfer date" — July 21, 2011, one year after enactment — when statutory authority, rules, proceedings, and personnel transferred from the Federal Reserve Board, OCC, OTS, FDIC, FTC, NCUA, and HUD. **(§1062)**

---

## Subtitle A — Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection (§§1011–1018)

### Establishment and Structure (§1011)

The Bureau is established within the Federal Reserve System as an independent bureau. The Director is appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate to a 5-year term and is removable by the President only for inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office — the traditional "for cause" standard. **(§1011(c))**

The Federal Reserve may not:
- Direct, manage, or control the Bureau's activities or budget
- Consolidate, merge, or transfer Bureau functions
- Intervene in examinations, supervisory, or enforcement proceedings
- Limit or alter the Bureau's powers **(§1012(c))**

### Consumer Advisory Board (§1014)

The Director must establish a Consumer Advisory Board with a minimum of six members having expertise in consumer protection, financial services, community development, or representatives of depository institutions or non-depository covered persons. The Director must meet with the CAB at least twice per year.

### Funding (§1017)

The Bureau is funded by transfers from the Federal Reserve's earnings, deliberately insulating the Bureau from the annual congressional appropriations process. Transfer caps:
- Year 1: ≤10% of Federal Reserve total operating expenses
- Year 2: ≤11%
- Year 3 and thereafter: ≤12%

The Bureau may request additional congressional appropriations if the capped transfer is insufficient for the Bureau's statutory duties. **(§1017(e))**

### Effective Date (§1018)

Title X took effect one day after enactment (July 22, 2010) except as otherwise provided.

## Subtitle B — General Powers of the Bureau (§§1021–1029A)

### Purpose (§1021)

The Bureau's statutory purpose is to implement and enforce federal consumer financial law consistently so that all consumers have access to markets for consumer financial products and services, and markets for those products and services are fair, transparent, and competitive. **(§1021(a))**

### Covered Persons and Definitions (§1002)

The operative definitions govern the Bureau's entire jurisdiction:

**"Covered person"** — any person that engages in offering or providing a consumer financial product or service, and any affiliate of such a person if the affiliate acts as a service provider to the person. **(§1002(6))**

**"Consumer financial product or service"** — any financial product or service offered or provided for use by consumers primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, in connection with: (i) extending credit; (ii) extending or brokering leases; (iii) providing real estate settlement or title insurance services; (iv) engaging in deposit-taking or transmitting or exchanging funds; (v) storing value; (vi) providing check-cashing, check-guarantee, or check-collection services; (vii) providing payments or similar services; (viii) providing financial advisory services to consumers on individual financial matters. **(§1002(5))**

**"Federal consumer financial law"** — Title X itself, the enumerated consumer laws (18 statutes, A–R), any rules or orders prescribed under Title X or the enumerated laws, and authorities transferred to the Bureau. **(§1002(14))**

**Enumerated consumer laws (§1002(12))** — the 18 statutes transferred to Bureau administration:

| Letter | Statute |

| A | Alternative Mortgage Transaction Parity Act of 1982 |
| B | Consumer Leasing Act of 1976 |
| C | Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) |

| F | Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) |
| G | Home Owners Protection Act of 1998 |
| H | Home Mortgage Disclosure Act of 1975 (HMDA) |

| J | S.A.F.E. Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008 |

| R | Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989 (FIRREA) appraisal independence provisions |

### Rulemaking Authority (§1022)

The Bureau may prescribe rules, issue orders, and publish guidance as may be necessary or appropriate to carry out the purposes and objectives of federal consumer financial law. Before issuing a proposed rule, the Bureau must consult with appropriate federal banking agencies for potential prudential, monetary policy, or payment system implications. **(§1022(b)(2))**

The Bureau may grant conditional or unconditional exemptions from any provision of federal consumer financial law if the exemption is in the public interest and consistent with the purposes of federal consumer financial law. **(§1022(b)(3))**

### FSOC Override of Bureau Rules (§1023)

The Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC) may stay a Bureau rule for up to 90 days and subsequently set aside a Bureau rule (not merely modify it) by a vote of 2/3 of FSOC members, including the Chairperson (the Secretary of the Treasury), if:
1. The rule or provision would put the safety and soundness of the United States banking system or the stability of the financial system of the United States at serious risk; **and**
2. Setting aside the rule is necessary to prevent an imminent threat to the safety and soundness of the banking system or financial stability.

This is a narrow structural safety-valve, not a general political override. FSOC may not use this authority to disagree with the Bureau's policy choices. **(§1023)**

### Supervision of Non-Depository Covered Persons (§1024)

The Bureau supervises non-depository covered persons in markets for:
- Residential mortgage origination and servicing
- Private education lending
- Payday lending

For other non-bank markets, the Bureau may designate "larger participants" by rule and supervise those entities. The Bureau may also supervise any non-bank covered person it has reasonable cause to believe is engaging in conduct that poses risks to consumers. **(§1024(a))**

### Supervision of Large Insured Depository Institutions (§1025)

The Bureau has primary supervisory authority (examination, enforcement) over insured depository institutions and credit unions with total assets exceeding $10 billion, and their affiliates. Smaller depositories remain subject to primary supervision by their prudential regulators (OCC, FDIC, Fed, NCUA) for consumer compliance, though the CFPB retains enforcement authority. **(§1025)**

### Supervision of Service Providers (§1026)

Any service provider to a covered person is subject to Bureau supervision and examination to the same extent as if the service provider were itself the covered person. The covered person cannot discharge its compliance obligations by outsourcing. **(§1026)**

### Exclusions (§1027)

The following are **not** "covered persons" for Title X purposes:
- **Merchants and retailers** not otherwise engaged in offering consumer financial products or services **(§1027(a))**
- **Attorneys** with respect to practice of law **(§1027(e))**
- **Auto dealers** — persons predominantly engaged in the sale and servicing of motor vehicles, leasing (not providing financing to finance the purchase), and their affiliates **(§1029)**
- **Real estate brokers and agents** with respect to real estate brokerage activities **(§1027(b))**
- **Persons regulated by state insurance authorities** with respect to insurance activities **(§1027(f))**
- **Tax preparers** with respect to their services **(§1027(c))**
- **Accountants** with respect to accounting services **(§1027(d))**
- Employees of covered persons are not separately covered **(§1027(j))**

These exclusions mean the FTC retains UDAP enforcement authority for excluded entities.

### Arbitration (§1028)

The Bureau is authorised to restrict or prohibit mandatory pre-dispute arbitration agreements in connection with any consumer financial product or service by regulation, after conducting a study and finding that such restrictions are in the public interest and for the protection of consumers. **(§1028)**

## Subtitle C — Specific Bureau Authorities (§§1031–1037)

### UDAAP Prohibition (§§1031, 1036)

Section 1031 authorises the Bureau to prescribe rules to prevent unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices in connection with any transaction with a consumer for a consumer financial product or service.

Section 1036 contains the operative prohibition: it is unlawful for any covered person or service provider to engage in any unfair, deceptive, or abusive act or practice in connection with any transaction with a consumer for a consumer financial product or service. The three standards are independently defined. See [[cfpb-udaap]] for full analysis.

### Disclosure Authority (§1032)

The Bureau may prescribe rules requiring a covered person to disclose the costs, benefits, and risks of consumer financial products or services. Disclosures must be in plain language, presented in a clear, concise, and understandable form, and shall not contain any additional mandatory language unless the Bureau determines such language is necessary. **(§1032)**

### Consumer Access to Financial Records (§1033)

Subject to certain limitations, a covered person shall make available to a consumer information in the control or possession of the covered person concerning the consumer's financial product or service. The Bureau may prescribe rules governing this access. **(§1033)**

### Consumer Complaint Response (§1034)

The Bureau shall establish a unit to receive, review, and refer to the relevant agency complaints submitted by consumers against covered persons. The Bureau shall establish a toll-free number for consumer complaints. **(§1034)**

## Subtitle D — Preservation of State Law (§§1041–1048)

### General Preservation of State Law (§1041)

Federal consumer financial law does not preempt any state statute, regulation, order, or interpretation relating to consumer financial services that affords greater protection to consumers than federal law. A state law is inconsistent with federal consumer financial law — and therefore preempted — only to the extent the state law *prevents or significantly interferes with* the exercise of authority under federal consumer financial law. States may therefore impose requirements stricter than federal minimums. **(§1041)**

### State Attorney General Enforcement (§1042)

State AGs and state regulatory authorities may bring civil actions on behalf of state residents to enforce Title X and any enumerated consumer law. Before filing, the AG must notify the Bureau (or immediately after filing if exigent circumstances prevented prior notice). The Bureau may intervene and be heard in the action. If the Bureau has already instituted its own enforcement action against the defendant, state supervisory agencies may not bring concurrent action for the same violation against the same defendant. **(§1042)**

### National Bank Preemption (§§1044–1048)

Title X did not expand or alter the preemption framework applicable to national banks. National banks continue to be governed by the National Bank Act preemption framework administered by the OCC. A state consumer financial law preempts a national bank only where the OCC makes a formal case-by-case determination supported by substantial evidence that the state law prevents or significantly interferes with the exercise of national bank powers — applying the *Barnett Bank* standard. OCC preemption determinations are subject to de novo judicial review. **(§§1044, 1047)**

Federal savings associations are subject to identical preemption standards via §1045.

Visitorial powers over national banks remain with the OCC. The Bureau exercises its examination authority over national banks through coordinated protocols with the OCC rather than independent visitation. **(§1046)**

## Subtitle E — Enforcement Powers (§§1051–1057)

### Civil Investigative Demands (§1052)

The Bureau may issue civil investigative demands (CIDs) to any person that has possession, custody, or control of documentary evidence, tangible things, written reports, answers to interrogatories, or oral testimony relevant to a CFPB investigation. The CID must describe the nature of the conduct under investigation. Recipients may petition the Director to modify or set aside the CID before the compliance date. Non-compliance is enforceable in federal district court.

### Administrative Cease and Desist (§§1053–1054)

The Bureau may initiate administrative proceedings against a covered person or service provider and issue cease-and-desist orders (including temporary orders effective immediately, lasting up to 60 days, where necessary to prevent continuing harm). If the respondent consents, orders become final immediately. Contested orders go through ALJ hearings within the Bureau. Final orders may require: cessation of the conduct; affirmative corrective action; restitution; and civil monetary penalties.

### Civil Monetary Penalty Tiers (§1055)

| Tier | Daily maximum | Conduct |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Tier 1** | $5,000 per day | Any violation of federal consumer financial law |
| **Tier 2** | $25,000 per day | Reckless engagement in a violation |
| **Tier 3** | $1,000,000 per day | Knowing violation |

Penalty amounts apply per day for each day the violation continues. All amounts are subject to inflation adjustment. The Bureau may bring penalty actions in federal district court or administratively. The Bureau may also seek rescission/reformation of contracts, restitution, and disgorgement in conjunction with penalties. **(§1055)**

### Whistleblower Protection (§1057)

Employees of covered persons are protected against retaliation for: providing information to the Bureau; participating in Bureau proceedings; filing a complaint; initiating proceedings about a potential CFPB violation; or providing information to the employer's internal compliance function regarding potential federal consumer financial law violations. Protected employees who suffer retaliation may seek reinstatement, back pay with interest, and reasonable attorneys' fees. The employee must file a complaint with the Secretary of Labor within 180 days of the retaliatory act.

## Subtitle F — Transfer of Functions (§§1061–1067)

On the designated transfer date (July 21, 2011), the Bureau assumed all consumer financial protection functions, rules, orders, interpretations, and pending proceedings previously held by:
- Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System
- Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)
- Office of Thrift Supervision (OTS) — subsequently abolished
- Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)
- Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — rulemaking authority for consumer financial laws; FTC retained enforcement for excluded entities
- National Credit Union Administration (NCUA)
- Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)

All staff performing transferred functions transferred to the Bureau on the designated transfer date. **(§§1061–1067)**

Predecessor agency rules and orders remained effective until the Bureau amended, repealed, or replaced them. **(§1066)**

## Subtitle G — Regulatory Improvements (§§1071–1079A)

### Section 1071 — Small Business Lending Data Collection (ECOA §704B)

Amended ECOA to add §704B, directing financial institutions that originate covered credit transactions for small businesses to collect and report to the Bureau 20 data fields including: application/action date and type; amount applied for and approved; loan purpose; census tract; NAICS code; minority-owned status; women-owned status; LGBTQI+-owned status; gross annual revenue; ethnicity, race, and sex of principal owners holding 25% or more equity. A mandatory firewall prohibits underwriters from accessing demographic fields unless access controls are not feasible. Data must be submitted to the Bureau annually by June 1. See [[reg-b-small-business-lending-data-collection]] for detailed implementation. **(§1071)**

### Section 1073 — Remittance Transfer Disclosures (EFTA §919)

Added §919 to the EFTA establishing a comprehensive pre-transfer and receipt disclosure framework for remittance transfers. Key requirements:
- **Pre-transfer disclosure:** Transfer amount, exchange rate (to 4 decimal places), fees charged, total to be received by designated recipient, and date funds will be available
- **Receipt:** Same information plus error rights and cancellation rights disclosures
- **Cancellation right:** Consumer may cancel within 30 minutes of payment (before transfer is processed) for full refund within 3 business days
- **Error resolution:** 180-day window to report errors; Bureau has 90 days to investigate and resolve
- **Agent liability:** Remittance transfer provider is liable for violations by its agents
- **Safe harbour:** Providers completing 500 or fewer remittance transfers per year in the prior and current calendar year are exempt

See [[reg-e-remittance-transfers]] for full implementation detail. **(§1073)**

### Section 1075 — Debit Card Interchange Fees (Durbin Amendment; EFTA §920)

Added §920 to the EFTA, directing the Federal Reserve Board to:
1. **Cap interchange fees** at amounts "reasonable and proportional" to the cost incurred by the issuer with respect to the transaction — the Fed implemented this as $0.21 + 5 basis points per transaction (plus $0.01 fraud prevention adjustment) **(§1075(a))**
2. **Prohibit network exclusivity arrangements** — an issuer or payment card network may not restrict the number of payment card networks on which an electronic debit transaction may be processed to fewer than 2 unaffiliated networks **(§1075(b))**
3. **Prohibit routing restrictions** — an issuer or payment card network may not inhibit the ability of any merchant to direct the routing of an electronic debit transaction over any network that may process such transaction **(§1075(b))**

**Small issuer exemption:** The interchange fee cap and related restrictions do not apply to issuers with total consolidated assets below $10 billion (including affiliates). **(§1075(a)(7))**

The merchant discount provisions permit merchants to offer discounts for cash, check, or other payment forms, and to impose minimum transaction amounts of up to $10 for credit card purchases (but not debit). **(§1075(b)(4)–(5))**

### Additional Subtitle G Studies and Requirements

- **§1076:** Bureau must conduct a study on reverse mortgages and report to Congress within 1 year of the designated transfer date
- **§1078:** Bureau must conduct a study on the differences between credit scores sold to consumers and those sold to creditors by CRAs, and report to Congress within 1 year of enactment
- **§1079A:** Extended the Securities Exchange Act civil statute of limitations to the later of: 2 years from discovery or 5 years from violation; criminal statute of limitations set at 6 years for certain securities fraud; directed USSC to promulgate sentencing guidelines for securities and financial fraud offenses

## Subtitle H — Conforming Amendments (§§1081–1100H)

Subtitle H swept conforming amendments through 25+ federal statutes, transferring rulemaking references from predecessor agencies to "Bureau." The substantive scope of each statute was not changed — only the identity of the rulemaking authority and, where applicable, enforcement allocation. Key amendments:

| Section | Statute | Key Change |
|---------|---------|------------|
| §1085 | FDCPA | Bureau replaces FTC as rulemaking authority; FTC retains enforcement |
| §1086 | FCRA/FACTA | Bureau rulemaking; furnisher accuracy guidelines transferred; adverse action model disclosure updated |
| §1094 | HMDA | Bureau replaces Federal Reserve Board; new §304(b) data fields added (credit score, points and fees, APR-benchmark spread, introductory rate period, prepayment penalty terms, loan channel, parcel number, universal loan ID) |
| §1098 | RESPA | Bureau replaces HUD Secretary; Bureau directed to publish integrated TILA-RESPA mortgage disclosure |
| §1100 | S.A.F.E. Act | Bureau develops and maintains NMLS registration system for depository institution loan originators; Bureau has backup authority to establish loan originator licensing system |
| §1100A | TILA | Bureau replaces Federal Reserve Board; integrated disclosure mandate (combined RESPA-TILA); TILA dollar caps raised from $25,000 to $50,000; Bureau adjusts annually for CPI after Dec 31, 2011 |
| §1100C | Telemarketing Act | FTC retains rulemaking but must consult Bureau for rules touching consumer financial products; TCFAPA violations by covered persons = UDAAP violations |
| §1100F | FCRA §615 | Adverse action notices must include credit score used; risk-based pricing notices must include credit score information |
| §1100G | Small Business Fairness | Bureau added as "covered agency" under 5 USC §609(d); regulatory flexibility analyses must assess credit cost impacts on small entities |
| §1100H | Effective date | All Subtitle H amendments (except §§1081–1082) effective on designated transfer date |

## Key Points for Agents

- **The CFPB was funded specifically to avoid congressional appropriations dependence.** The 12% Federal Reserve transfer cap removes a traditional congressional check on the agency's operations — a structural feature that has been the subject of litigation. Any regulatory change that alters the funding mechanism would have immediate operational consequences. **(§1017)**
- **"Covered person" turns on activity, not charter.** A fintech that extends credit, transmits funds, or provides payment services to consumers is a covered person regardless of whether it holds a banking charter. The relevant question is what the entity *does*, not how it is licensed. **(§1002(6))**
- **The FSOC veto is structural, not political.** FSOC can only override a Bureau rule that poses serious risk to banking safety and soundness — it cannot override a rule simply because member agencies disagree with the policy. The Bureau's independence is therefore real, not nominal. **(§1023)**
- **State law can be stricter than federal floors.** Title X explicitly preserves state consumer financial laws that afford greater protection. A state usury cap, additional disclosure requirement, or broader prohibited practice standard is not preempted by federal consumer financial law. **(§1041)**
- **Civil penalty exposure is per day, not per incident.** Tier 3 penalties of $1,000,000 per day for knowing violations can accumulate rapidly in a multi-week or multi-month enforcement period. The total penalty in a major enforcement action routinely reaches tens or hundreds of millions of dollars. **(§1055)**
- **The designated transfer date (July 21, 2011) is the operative effective date for most CFPB authority.** Rules issued before that date by predecessor agencies remain in effect until the Bureau amends or replaces them. The Bureau did not start with a clean slate. **(§1066)**
