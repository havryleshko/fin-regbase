# SUP 10C — FCA Senior Managers Regime: Functions and Approval

## What SUP 10C Does

SUP 10C has two purposes **(SUP 10C.2.1G)**:

1. To specify, under **section 59 of FSMA**, descriptions of the FCA-designated senior management functions (SMFs) for SMCR firms — these are listed in the table at **SUP 10C.4.3R**.
2. To specify the manner in which a firm must apply for FCA approval under section 59 and other procedural obligations for FCA-approved SMF managers.

SUP 10C applies to every SMCR firm **(SUP 10C.1.1R)**. It does not apply to appointed representatives of SMCR firms (those are dealt with by SUP 10A) **(SUP 10C.1.7R)**.

---

## Definition: What Makes a Function a Controlled Function

A function specified in SUP 10C is an **FCA controlled function only to the extent that it is performed under an arrangement** entered into by:
- (1) a firm; or
- (2) a contractor of the firm;

in relation to the carrying on by the firm of a regulated activity **(SUP 10C.3.6R)**.

This is the **arrangement requirement**. A function does not become an FCA controlled function in the abstract — it only becomes one when actually performed under an arrangement connected to a regulated activity. For group structures, a person employed by a holding company will only perform an FCA controlled function if there is an arrangement between the firm and the group member **(SUP 10C.3.9G)**.

A function is covered by the table at SUP 10C.4.3R only if it satisfies **both** the arrangement requirement **(SUP 10C.3.6R)** and the definition of an FCA-designated senior management function **(SUP 10C.3.10R)**. These are conjunctive conditions **(SUP 10C.3.5G)**.

### What Is a Senior Management Function

A function is a **senior management function** (within section 59ZA(2) of FSMA) if:
- (1) it will require the person performing it to be responsible for managing one or more aspects of the firm's affairs, so far as relating to the activity; **and**
- (2) those aspects involve, or might involve, a risk of serious consequences — (a) for the firm; or (b) for business or other interests in the United Kingdom **(SUP 10C.3.11G)**.

'Managing' includes taking decisions, or participating in the taking of decisions, about how one or more aspects of the firm's affairs should be carried on **(SUP 10C.3.12G)**.

## SMF 3 — Executive Director Function

For a **UK SMCR firm**, the executive director function is the function of acting in the capacity of a director (other than a NED) of the firm **(SUP 10C.5.1R(1))**. For an **overseas SMCR firm**, it is the function of acting as such a director in relation to its UK branch where the person has responsibility for managing one or more aspects of the firm's affairs relating to the branch's activities **(SUP 10C.5.1R(2))**. The function does not apply to UK SMCR firms that are partnerships or LLPs **(SUP 10C.5.1R(4))**.

---

## SMF 7 — Group Entity Senior Manager: Strategic-Only Exclusion

A group-level individual whose responsibilities for a subsidiary firm are **limited to developing the group's overall strategy** is unlikely to be performing the group entity senior manager function **(SUP 10C.5B.5G)**. The function applies to those who are **directly responsible for implementing** the group's strategy in the firm. The FCA would not routinely expect subsidiaries to have SMF7 holders where local SMF managers have sufficient control. The strategic-only exclusion is guidance, not a Rule — firms must assess the actual scope of the individual's responsibilities.

## The 12-Week Rule (SUP 10C.3A)

### Purpose

The 12-week rule allows a firm to cover an absent or departing SMF manager without needing to obtain FCA approval for the cover person first. It is not a general exemption — it is a time-limited safety valve for situations the firm did not anticipate **(SUP 10C.3A.1G–3A.4G)**.

### Main Conditions (SUP 10C.3A.6R)

A firm may appoint an individual as temporary cover without FCA approval where all of the following conditions are met:

1. The individual is a member of the **conduct rules staff** of the firm;
2. The appointment is to provide cover for an SMF manager **whose absence is** — (a) temporary; **or** (b) reasonably unforeseen; **and**
3. The appointment is **for less than 12 weeks in a consecutive 12-month period**.

### Extension Where Absence Turns Out to Be Longer (SUP 10C.3A.8R)

Where the absence was reasonably unforeseen and the firm makes a valid and complete application for approval of a permanent replacement **within the first 12 weeks following the absence**, the 12-week period is extended until the FCA finally determines the application. 'Finally determined' means the application is granted, withdrawn, refused, or the Tribunal has disposed of any appeal **(SUP 10C.3A.8R(3))**.

### Fit and Proper

The firm must ensure the temporary replacement is **fit and proper** to perform the role, even though FCA pre-approval is not required **(SUP 10C.3A.13R)**. The assessment takes into account that the person will only be in post temporarily, but must include the skills, knowledge, and expertise needed for any FCA-prescribed responsibilities or SYSC 26 responsibilities reallocated from the absent manager **(SUP 10C.3A.14G)**.

### Prescribed Responsibilities During Absence

Where a firm has allocated **FCA-prescribed senior management responsibilities** to an SMF manager who later becomes absent, those responsibilities must be reallocated to **another SMF manager** — not to the person providing cover unless that cover person is also an SMF manager of the firm **(SUP 10C.3A.20G)**. This is a hard constraint: unapproved temporary replacements cannot inherit prescribed responsibilities.

### Reasonable Use

A firm must use the rule **(SUP 10C.3A.15G)**:
- Only when it actually needs to (not merely because it is entitled to);
- As **infrequently as reasonably possible**; and
- For **as short a time as reasonably possible**.

The FCA expects firms to have succession plans and effective use of notice periods to minimise reliance on the rule. Planned retirements or fixed-term contract endings are not eligible — the absence must be genuinely unforeseen **(SUP 10C.3A.12G)**.

## PRA–FCA Absorption Rule (SUP 10C.9.8R)

For **PRA-authorised firms**, where a candidate will perform both a PRA-designated SMF and what would otherwise be an FCA governing function, and the **PRA notified the FCA of this in its approval application**, the FCA governing function is **absorbed into the PRA approval** — no separate FCA application is required **(SUP 10C.9.8R)**.

Both conditions must be satisfied:
1. The person's role spans a PRA SMF and an FCA governing function; and
2. The PRA included the FCA governing function scope in the notification to the FCA.

This is the primary mechanism for avoiding dual approval in banking and insurance firms. Where the conditions are not met, separate FCA approval remains required.

---

## Application Process (SUP 10C.10)

### Who Applies and When

The application must be **made by the firm**, not by the candidate **(SUP 10C.10)**. **Prior FCA approval is required before** the individual begins to perform the SMF — there is no grace period and no equivalence to the 12-week temporary cover rule for planned appointments **(SUP 10C.10.3G–10.4G)**.

### Individual Liability Under Section 63A FSMA

If an individual **performs an FCA-designated SMF without approval** and knew, or reasonably ought to have known, that they lacked approval, the FCA may impose a **financial penalty on that individual directly** under **section 63A of FSMA** **(SUP 10C.10)**. This is personal liability that runs alongside any enforcement action against the firm. The s.63A power applies specifically to the act of performing an unapproved controlled function — it is not limited to downstream misconduct in the role.

## Key Points for Agents

- **The arrangement requirement is a precondition (SUP 10C.3.6R).** A function listed in the SMF table is an FCA controlled function only to the extent it is actually performed under an arrangement entered into by the firm or its contractor in relation to a regulated activity. Functions performed by group members without a proper arrangement are not FCA controlled functions — but a missing arrangement is itself a compliance failure if the function is in substance being performed.
- **The full SMF table (SUP 10C.4.3R) has 20+ distinct functions across five categories.** Governing functions (SMF1, 3, 7, 9–15, 19, 27), required functions (SMF16, 17), systems and controls functions (SMF2, 4, 5, 24), other functions (SMF18, 21, 22, 23b, 29). Not all functions apply to all firm types — SUP 10C Annex 1 governs which apply to which tier/structure.
- **The 12-week rule requires unforeseen absence — planned departures are ineligible (SUP 10C.3A.6R; 10C.3A.12G).** A firm that knows an SMF manager is retiring or has a fixed-term ending cannot rely on SUP 10C.3A. The rule is for genuinely unexpected vacancies. The temporary replacement must be fit and proper even without FCA pre-approval. Prescribed responsibilities cannot be inherited by the cover person unless they are themselves an approved SMF manager.
- **No one may begin performing an SMF without prior FCA approval — and performing without it is individually punishable (SUP 10C.10; FSMA s.63A).** The application is the firm's obligation, not the candidate's. Individual financial penalties under s.63A apply where the person knew or should have known they lacked approval. This is distinct from enforcement against the firm.
- **The PRA absorption rule (SUP 10C.9.8R) removes the need for a separate FCA application in dual-function cases in PRA-authorised firms**, but only where the PRA notified the FCA of the dual scope in its application. If that notification is absent, both approvals are required. Firms should not assume PRA approval covers FCA functions without verifying the notification was made.
- **SMF7 (group entity senior manager) does not catch pure strategy-setters (SUP 10C.5B.5G).** A group-level individual whose only involvement with a subsidiary is setting overall group strategy is unlikely to need SMF7 approval at the subsidiary. The function applies to those implementing strategy within the firm, not to those determining it at group level. This is a judgment-based assessment — firms should document the scope of the individual's actual responsibilities.
