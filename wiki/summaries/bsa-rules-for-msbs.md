# BSA — Rules for Money Services Businesses (31 CFR Part 1022)

## Overview

31 CFR Part 1022 contains the FinCEN rules applicable specifically to money services businesses (MSBs). It layers MSB-specific obligations on top of the general BSA provisions in 31 CFR Part 1010. MSBs are defined in §1010.100(ff) (Part 1010) and include currency dealers/exchangers, check cashers, issuers/sellers/redeemers of traveler's checks/money orders, providers/sellers of prepaid access, money transmitters, and the US Postal Service. Subpart A of Part 1022 is [Reserved] for definitions (MSBs rely on Part 1010 definitions). **(31 CFR Part 1022)**

---

## Subpart B — Programs

### AML Programme — §1022.210

Every MSB must develop, implement, and maintain an effective written anti-money laundering programme reasonably designed to prevent the MSB from being used to facilitate money laundering and the financing of terrorist activities. **(§1022.210(a))**

**Four Required Elements:**

1. **Policies, procedures, and internal controls** reasonably designed to assure ongoing compliance with applicable BSA requirements
2. **Designated compliance officer** — an individual responsible for assuring day-to-day compliance, training personnel, and ensuring BSA reports are filed and records maintained
3. **Training** for appropriate personnel
4. **Independent review** to monitor and maintain an adequate programme

**(§1022.210(b)(1)–(b)(4))**

**Risk-Commensurate Scope:** The programme must be commensurate with the risks posed by the location and size of, and the nature and volume of financial services provided by, the MSB. **(§1022.210(c))**

**No Board Approval Required:** Unlike banks (§1020.210), there is no requirement that the AML programme be approved by a board of directors. **(§1022.210)**

**Agent Allocation of Responsibility:** An MSB may contractually allocate compliance responsibilities among its agents. However, the MSB (principal) remains **solely responsible** for BSA compliance regardless of any such allocation. Any agreement allocating compliance responsibilities to an agent does not reduce or eliminate the principal's obligations under Part 1022. **(§1022.210(d))**

**Prepaid Access Customer Identification (embedded in AML programme):**

When a provider of prepaid access provides prepaid access to funds that have been loaded onto a device or vehicle, or to a prepaid programme, the provider's AML programme must include procedures for:

- **Collecting required information from customers:** name, date of birth, address, and identification number
- **Verifying the identity** of customers who obtain prepaid access to funds in excess of **$10,000 in a day** (seller obligation); providers must collect/retain information from sellers who obtain prepaid access to funds >$10,000/day
- **Retaining the information** for **5 years** after the last use of the prepaid access vehicle or device

**(§1022.210(b)(5) — embedded as fifth element of programme)**

## Subpart C — Reports Required To Be Made

### SAR for MSBs — §1022.320

Every MSB must file a SAR for any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law or regulation. See full concept page: [[bsa-msb-sar]].

**Key distinctions from bank SAR (§1020.320):**
- **Lower threshold:** ≥**$2,000** (vs. $5,000 for banks); **≥$5,000** for issuers/sellers/redeemers of money orders or traveler's checks who have access to clearance records
- **Fourth filing ground:** adds "the transaction involves use of the MSB to facilitate criminal activity" — absent from the bank SAR provision
- **Voluntary terrorist activity reporting** via FinCEN Hotline: **1-866-556-3974**

**(§1022.320(a)(2))**

**Filing Procedures:** Same structure as bank SAR — SAR to FinCEN; 30-day deadline; 60-day if no suspect identified at detection; immediate law enforcement telephone notification for ongoing schemes. Confidentiality, subpoena refusal, and safe harbour provisions are the same as §1020.320. **(§1022.320(b)–(f))**

## Subpart D — Records Required To Be Maintained

### Dealer in Foreign Exchange Records — §1022.410

A dealer in foreign exchange (currency dealer or exchanger, as defined in §1010.100(ff)(1)) must maintain:

- **Customer TIN** — obtained within 30 days of first transaction
- Records of all **foreign exchange transactions >$1,000** — name, address, and government ID of customer; date and amount of transaction; currencies involved
- Records of items **purchased/sold for amounts >$10,000** outside the United States
- Records of **receipts >$10,000 from foreign sources**

**(§1022.410)**

**Exception:** The dealer in foreign exchange recordkeeping requirements do **not apply** to financial institutions that offer foreign exchange as an ancillary service (such as banks or broker-dealers). **(§1022.410)**

### Prepaid Access Records — §1022.420

**Providers** of prepaid access must maintain, for a period of **5 years**, access to transactional records generated in the ordinary course of business that would be needed to reconstruct a prepaid access device activation, loads, reloads, purchases, withdrawals, and transfers. **(§1022.420(a))**

**Sellers** of prepaid access must maintain, for **5 years**, records of each sale of prepaid access, including the information collected pursuant to the customer identification requirements in the AML programme (§1022.210(b)(5)). **(§1022.420(b))**

See full concept page: [[bsa-prepaid-access]].

## Subpart F — Special Standards of Diligence; Prohibitions; Special Measures

§1022.600 is [Reserved]. All Subpart F obligations for MSBs are governed by 31 CFR Part 1010 Subpart F (correspondent account DD, private banking, shell bank prohibition, special measures). **(§1022.600)**

## MSB Registration — §1022.380

Every MSB (except agents acting solely for one principal and the US Postal Service) must register with FinCEN within 180 days of establishment and re-register every 2 years. Re-registration is also required upon: ownership change; >10% equity transfer; or >50% increase in agents. See full concept page: [[bsa-msb-registration]].

**Civil penalty:** $5,000/day per violation. **Criminal penalty:** 18 USC 1960. **(§1022.380)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Part 1022 imposes a 4-element AML programme; banks (Part 1020) impose a 5-element programme.** MSBs have no board-approval requirement; banks must have board approval. Agent allocation of responsibility is permitted but the principal MSB remains solely liable. **(§1022.210)**
- **MSB SAR threshold is $2,000 — lower than the bank $5,000 threshold.** Money order/traveler's check issuers with clearance records use $5,000. MSBs have a fourth SAR ground: "involves use of the MSB to facilitate criminal activity." Voluntary terrorist activity reports go to the FinCEN Hotline 1-866-556-3974. **(§1022.320)**
- **Every MSB (except pure agents and USPS) must register with FinCEN within 180 days and re-register every 2 years.** Failure exposes the MSB to $5,000/day civil penalties and criminal liability under 18 USC 1960. **(§1022.380)**
- **Prepaid access customer ID (name/DOB/address/ID number) is embedded in the AML programme, not a standalone requirement.** Sellers must verify identity of customers purchasing >$10,000/day. Five-year retention applies to both customer ID information and all prepaid transactional records. **(§§1022.210(b)(5), 1022.420)**
- **Dealer in foreign exchange records apply only to standalone dealers — not to banks or broker-dealers offering FX as an ancillary service.** TIN required within 30 days; all FX transactions >$1,000 must be recorded with customer identity; items >$10,000 outside the US and foreign receipts >$10,000 require separate records. **(§1022.410)**
