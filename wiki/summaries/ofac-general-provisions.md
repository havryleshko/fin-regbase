# OFAC — Reporting, Procedures, Penalties, and Compliance Framework

## Overview

The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) administers and enforces economic and trade sanctions programmes based on US foreign policy and national security goals. OFAC's legal authority derives from multiple statutes, chiefly:

- **Trading with the Enemy Act (TWEA)**, 50 USC 5(b) — wartime economic authority
- **International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA)**, 50 USC 1701 et seq. — peacetime national emergency authority
- **Foreign Narcotics Kingpin Designation Act (FNKDA)**, 21 USC 1901 et seq.
- **Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA)**, 18 USC 2332d
- **Clean Diamond Trade Act (CDTA)**
- Various Executive Orders issued under these statutes

**31 CFR Part 501** is the cross-cutting procedural regulation applying to all OFAC sanctions programmes. It governs recordkeeping, reporting obligations, the licensing and delisting procedures, and the full TWEA civil penalty process — from Pre-Penalty Notice through ALJ hearing to judicial review.

The **OFAC Framework for Compliance Commitments (May 2019)** is OFAC's published guidance on what constitutes an effective sanctions compliance programme (SCP). It identifies the five essential components of an SCP and the ten root causes of SCP breakdowns drawn from OFAC enforcement actions. The Framework is directly relevant to General Factors E (compliance programme) and F (remedial response) in Part 501 Appendix A, which govern whether an adequate SCP mitigates civil penalties.

## Recordkeeping — §501.601

Every person subject to OFAC's jurisdiction must **retain all records** relating to any transaction within OFAC's jurisdiction for **10 years** after the date of the transaction. **(§501.601)**

This is the longest retention requirement in the US financial compliance framework — compared with 5-year retention under the BSA.

---

## Reporting Obligations — §§501.602–501.606

### Records on Demand (§501.602)

OFAC may require any person to furnish records related to any transaction subject to OFAC's jurisdiction, on demand and without notice. Failure to comply is a civil violation independent of any underlying sanctions violation.

### Blocked Property Reports (§501.603)

When a US person blocks property pursuant to OFAC regulations:

- **Initial report:** Within **10 business days** of the blocking
- **Annual report:** By **September 30** of each year, for property blocked as of June 30 of that year
- **Unblocking report:** Within **10 business days** of releasing blocked property

Reports are filed with OFAC via the OFAC Reporting System (ORS) or as otherwise directed. **(§501.603)**

### Rejected Transaction Reports (§501.604)

When a US person rejects (rather than blocks) a transaction because it would violate OFAC regulations, the person must report the rejected transaction to OFAC within **10 business days** of the rejection. **(§501.604)**

**Distinction:** A blocked transaction involves property that must be frozen and held; a rejected transaction is one that cannot lawfully proceed and is simply refused (e.g., a prohibited wire transfer that cannot be processed but involves no property to freeze).

### Litigation / Arbitration Reports (§501.605)

Any US person who becomes engaged in litigation or arbitration involving property blocked under OFAC regulations must notify OFAC of that litigation promptly. **(§501.605)**

### Applicability (§501.606)

The reporting and recordkeeping requirements of Subpart B apply to all OFAC sanctions programmes administered under Chapter V of Title 31 CFR. **(§501.606)**

## Licensing — §501.801

**General licences** authorise categories of transactions under specified conditions without requiring individual applications. They are self-executing — persons who meet the conditions of a general licence may proceed without contacting OFAC. General licences are published in the regulations for each sanctions programme (the relevant Part of Chapter V) or on OFAC's website. **(§501.801(a))**

**Specific licences** are issued to named persons for specific transactions. Applications must be submitted to OFAC's Licensing Division. OFAC's decision constitutes final agency action. Denial of a specific licence does not preclude reapplication at any time on the basis of new facts or changed circumstances. **(§501.801(b))**

Information submitted to OFAC in licence applications is protected from Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) disclosure where OFAC reasonably foresees that disclosure would harm a FOIA-exempt interest. **(§501.801(b)(6))**

## SDN Delisting and Mistaken-Identity Unblocking — §§501.806–501.807

### Mistaken-Identity Unblocking (§501.806)

Where property was blocked due to mistaken identity or typographical/similar error, the person may request release by submitting a request to **OFACReport@treasury.gov** with the phrase **"31 CFR 501.806 — Request for a Compliance Release"** in the subject line. The request must include identifying information, the ORS identification number from the initial blocked property report, and a narrative explaining why the blocking was in error. **(§501.806)**

### SDN List Delisting (§501.807)

A sanctioned person (or owner of blocked property) may petition OFAC for removal from the SDN List or any other OFAC-maintained list by submitting arguments and evidence to **OFAC.Reconsideration@treasury.gov**. OFAC will review the petition, may request additional information and may agree to a meeting, and will provide a written decision. **(§501.807)**

## Civil Penalty Process — Subpart D (§§501.700–501.747)

The TWEA authorises OFAC to impose civil monetary penalties through an administrative process:

### Process Flow

1. **Pre-Penalty Notice** — OFAC issues a written notice setting out the alleged violations, the proposed penalty, the base category, the General Factors most relevant to the penalty amount, and the statutory maximum. **(Appendix A §V.A.1)**

2. **Response to Pre-Penalty Notice** — The Subject Person may submit a written response addressing the alleged violations and any applicable General Factors. The response should include all available documentary or other evidence. **(Appendix A §V.A.2)**

3. **Penalty Notice** — If OFAC determines a civil monetary penalty is warranted, it issues a Penalty Notice. The Penalty Notice **constitutes final agency action** that a violation has occurred. The penalty in the Penalty Notice will not exceed the Pre-Penalty Notice amount by more than 10%, unless a revised Pre-Penalty Notice is first issued. **(Appendix A §V.A.3)**

4. **ALJ Hearing (TWEA only)** — Under TWEA, a respondent may request a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge within the time period specified in the Penalty Notice. Hearings are governed by §§501.700–501.747. **(§§501.700–501.747)**

5. **Secretary's Designee Review** — The Secretary's designee may, in discretion, review an ALJ decision. Such review is a **prerequisite to judicial review** under the Administrative Procedure Act. **(§501.741)**

6. **Judicial Review** — Available in federal district court after administrative remedies are exhausted. **(§501.741(g))**

7. **DOJ Referral** — If a penalty imposed under a Penalty Notice is not paid within **30 calendar days** of the written notice of imposition, the matter may be referred to the US Department of Justice for collection in federal district court. **(§501.746)**

### Types of Administrative Responses

OFAC may respond to an apparent violation with:
- **No Action** — insufficient evidence of a violation
- **Request for Additional Information** — via administrative subpoena (§501.602)
- **Cautionary Letter** — violation conduct occurred, insufficient evidence or insufficient severity to warrant penalty; not a final agency determination
- **Finding of Violation** — documents the violation; provides an opportunity to respond before becoming final; constitutes a final agency determination if not contested
- **Civil Monetary Penalty** — final agency action; Penalty Notice constitutes a final determination that a violation occurred
- **Criminal Referral** — referred to appropriate law enforcement
- **Other Administrative Actions** — licence denial/suspension/revocation; cease and desist order **(Appendix A §II)**

## Civil Penalty Amounts

### Statutory Maximums by Statute

| Statute | Civil penalty maximum per violation |
|---------|-----------------------------------|
| IEEPA | Greater of $377,700 or 2× the underlying transaction value |
| TWEA | $111,308 |
| FNKDA | $1,876,699 |
| AEDPA | Greater of $99,703 or 2× the amount a financial institution was required to retain |
| CDTA | $17,062 |

*(All amounts inflation-adjusted under the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act.)* **(Appendix A §V.B.2.a.v)**

### Reporting/Recordkeeping-Specific Penalties

| Violation | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|
| Failure to furnish records on demand (§501.602) | Up to $29,150; up to $72,876 if transaction value exceeds $500,000 |
| Late filing of required report (within 30 days of due date) | Up to $3,550 |
| Late filing of required report (more than 30 days late) | Up to $7,104 |
| Continuing late filing of blocked asset reports | Additional $1,422 per 30-day period, up to 10 years |

**(Appendix A §IV)**

### Base Penalty Matrix

The base penalty for a civil monetary penalty is determined by a 2×2 matrix:

|  | **Non-Egregious** | **Egregious** |
|--|-------------------|--------------|
| **Voluntary Self-Disclosure (VSD)** | ½ of transaction value, capped at lesser of $188,850 or ½ statutory max | ½ of applicable statutory maximum |
| **No VSD** | Applicable schedule amount, capped at lesser of $377,700 or statutory max | Applicable statutory maximum |

**Adjustments:**
- Substantial cooperation without VSD: 25–40% reduction from base
- First violation (no prior penalty or Finding of Violation in preceding 5 years): up to 25% reduction
**(Appendix A §V.B.2)**

## The 11 General Factors — Appendix A §III

OFAC weighs all of the following in determining the administrative response and penalty amount:

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| A | Willful or reckless violation of law |
| B | Awareness of conduct at issue (actual knowledge; reason to know; management involvement) |
| C | Harm to sanctions programme objectives (economic benefit to sanctioned party; US policy implications; licence eligibility; humanitarian activity) |
| D | Individual characteristics (commercial sophistication; size of operations and financial condition; volume of transactions; 5-year sanctions history) |
| **E** | **Compliance programme** — existence, nature, and adequacy of a risk-based OFAC SCP at time of apparent violation |
| **F** | **Remedial response** — corrective action taken after discovery; whether senior management was informed; whether the entity implemented or enhanced its SCP |
| G | Cooperation with OFAC — VSD; full provision of relevant information; tolling agreement; promptness of response |
| H | Timing — whether violation occurred immediately after relevant sanctions addition or programme change |
| I | Other enforcement action by federal, state, or local agencies for the same conduct |
| J | Future compliance / deterrence effect on the Subject Person and similarly situated entities |
| K | Other relevant factors on a case-by-case basis |

**(Appendix A §III)**

Factors **E** and **F** are the formal channels through which a robust SCP (including an OFAC compliance programme meeting the Framework's five components) functions as a mitigating factor.

## OFAC Compliance Framework — Five SCP Components

The OFAC Framework for Compliance Commitments identifies five components that, when fully implemented and integrated, constitute an effective Sanctions Compliance Programme:

| Component | Core requirement |
|-----------|----------------|
| 1. Management commitment | Senior management endorses the SCP; adequate resources allocated; a named OFAC compliance officer designated; culture of compliance established |
| 2. Risk assessment | Periodic, written, enterprise-wide risk assessment covering customers, products, services, geographies, and counterparties; used to calibrate internal controls |
| 3. Internal controls | Written policies and procedures; customer and counterparty identification/screening; transaction monitoring; blocking and rejecting mechanics; escalation and notification paths |
| 4. Testing and auditing | Independent testing of the SCP; transaction testing; process auditing; findings fed back into programme enhancements |
| 5. Training | Role-specific, periodic, documented training covering applicable sanctions programmes and the institution's internal controls |

**(Framework pp. 5–11)**

## Ten Root Causes of SCP Breakdowns

OFAC identified the following root causes from enforcement actions — each correlates to an aggravating factor under the General Factors analysis:

1. Lack of a formal OFAC sanctions compliance programme
2. Inadequate compliance resources or staffing
3. Decentralised compliance function without adequate oversight or coordination
4. Lack of management commitment to the SCP
5. Misinterpretation or over-reliance on licensing requirements
6. Failure to identify and mitigate jurisdictional or sanctions programme risks
7. Lack of due diligence on customers, business partners, or counterparties
8. Misconduct by management or compliance staff
9. Failure to update the SCP as the sanctions landscape or business changes
10. Inadequate software, screening tools, or technical controls

**(Framework pp. 8–11)**

## Public Disclosure of Civil Penalty Information — §501.805(d)

After conclusion of a civil penalties proceeding or informal settlement, OFAC makes the following information public **not less than monthly**:
- Name and address of the **entity** involved
- Sanctions programme involved
- Brief description of the violation
- Whether the proceeding resulted in an informal settlement or penalty
- Whether the entity **voluntarily self-disclosed**
- Amount of the penalty or agreed settlement

For proceedings involving **individuals** only, OFAC releases aggregate statistics (number of penalties/settlements, programmes, descriptions, amounts) — **individual names are not publicly released**. **(§501.805(d))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Three separate reporting deadlines, all in business days.** Blocked property: initial report within 10 business days; annual report by September 30; unblocking within 10 business days. Rejected transactions: 10 business days. All records must be retained for 10 years — twice the BSA's 5-year standard. **(§§501.601–501.604)**
- **Voluntary self-disclosure formally halves the base penalty in both egregious and non-egregious cases.** The base penalty matrix is a fixed formula, not discretionary. In a non-egregious case with VSD, the base is ½ transaction value capped at $188,850. Without VSD, it is the applicable schedule amount capped at $377,700. In an egregious case, VSD halves the applicable statutory maximum. **(Appendix A §V.B.2)**
- **An adequate SCP at the time of the violation is a formal mitigating factor under General Factor E.** OFAC explicitly considers the existence, nature, and adequacy of the SCP when determining administrative response and penalty amount. The Framework's five components define what "adequate" means. The 10 root causes define what aggravates enforcement. **(Appendix A §III.E; Framework pp. 5–11)**
- **A Penalty Notice is final agency action; ALJ hearing is available only under TWEA.** The Penalty Notice constitutes a final determination that a violation occurred. For TWEA-based penalties, respondents may request an ALJ hearing. Secretary's designee review is discretionary but is a prerequisite to judicial review under the APA. If the penalty is not paid within 30 calendar days, OFAC may refer to DOJ for collection. **(§§501.741, 501.746; Appendix A §V.A)**
- **Licensing and delisting have distinct codified procedures.** General licences are self-executing. Specific licence denial does not preclude reapplication. SDN delisting petitions go to OFAC.Reconsideration@treasury.gov. Mistaken-identity unblocking requests cite "31 CFR 501.806" and go to OFACReport@treasury.gov. OFAC's licensing decisions constitute final agency action. **(§§501.801, 501.806, 501.807)**
