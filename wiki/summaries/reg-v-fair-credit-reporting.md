# Regulation V — Fair Credit Reporting (12 CFR Part 1022)

## Overview

12 CFR Part 1022 (Regulation V) implements the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 USC 1681 *et seq.*, as amended by the Dodd-Frank Act (which transferred rulemaking authority from the FTC to the CFPB). The regulation governs how consumer report information is collected, shared, used, and corrected. It applies across four major dimensions:

1. **Affiliate marketing** — rules on when affiliate-shared eligibility information may be used for marketing solicitations
2. **Medical information** — restrictions on creditors obtaining or using medical information in credit decisions
3. **Furnisher duties** — accuracy/integrity obligations and direct dispute investigation requirements for entities that furnish data to consumer reporting agencies (CRAs)
4. **Consumer reporting agency (CRA) duties** — risk-based pricing notices, identity theft protections, free annual file disclosures, and anti-circumvention rules

**Scope exclusion:** Persons excluded from CFPB jurisdiction by CFPA §1029 (title X of Dodd-Frank) are excluded from this part. **(§1022.1)**

**Model forms:** Appendices B–O contain safe harbour model notices. Use of the correct model form constitutes compliance with corresponding content requirements.

## Subpart A — General Provisions (§§1022.1–1022.3)

**Key definitions (§1022.3):**
- **Affiliate:** A person that is related by common ownership or affiliated by corporate control. For purposes of Subpart C, common ownership/control is generally established at ≥25% of the voting shares or controlling influence.
- **Identifying information:** Name, SSN, date of birth, government-issued ID, biometric data, electronic IDs, telecommunications identifiers.
- **Identity theft:** Fraud committed using another person's identifying information without authority.
- **Identity theft report:** A report to a law enforcement agency with sufficient specificity and affirmation (subject to penalties for perjury) that the recipient reasonably determines that identity theft has occurred.
- **Medical debt information:** Debt to a medical service provider or medical device provider — includes both unpaid and paid medical bills.
- **Medical information:** Information from a healthcare provider about a consumer's physical, mental, or behavioural health condition, treatment, or prognosis. Does not include: age/gender/general demographics, or information not relating to a physical, mental, or behavioural health condition.

## Subpart C — Affiliate Marketing (§§1022.20–1022.27)

**Core prohibition (§1022.21):** A person may not use eligibility information received from an affiliate to make a marketing solicitation to a consumer unless:
1. The consumer is given a clear, conspicuous, and concise notice and a reasonable opportunity and a reasonable and simple method to opt out; and
2. The consumer has not opted out.

**Eligibility information:** Any information that would be a consumer report if the FCRA §603(d)(2)(A) exception for affiliate sharing did not apply — essentially any information shared between affiliates that bears on the consumer's creditworthiness, credit standing, or consumer behaviour. **(§1022.20(b)(3))**

**Pre-existing business relationship exception:** A person may use affiliate-shared eligibility information to solicit a consumer with whom it has a pre-existing business relationship without providing an opt-out notice. A pre-existing business relationship exists for: (a) 18 months after the last date on which a financial product/service contract between the affiliate and consumer is still in force or the last payment or deposit; or (b) 3 months after an inquiry or application by the consumer. **(§1022.21(c)(2))**

**Other exceptions (§1022.21(c)):** Employee benefit/contracted services; performing services for the affiliate; consumer-initiated communication; consumer authorisation or request; state insurance law compliance.

**Scope and duration of opt-out (§1022.22):** Opt-out covers all affiliates listed in the notice. Minimum duration is **5 years**. Consumer may opt out at any time. After all prior relationships terminate and a new relationship forms, a new opt-out notice is required.

**Notice contents (§1022.23):** 7 required items: (i) name of affiliate(s) providing the notice; (ii) list of affiliates covered; (iii) types of eligibility information; (iv) right to limit; (v) election period and renewal option; (vi) if annual notice, statement that prior opt-out remains in effect; (vii) opt-out method. May be consolidated with GLB Act privacy notice.

**Reasonable opportunity (§1022.24):** 30 days by mail; 30 days by posting at website where consumer has a product or service (if consumer agreed to electronic disclosures); at the time of an electronic transaction.

**Reasonable and simple opt-out methods (§1022.25):** Check-off box; reply form with self-addressed envelope; electronic form; toll-free number; consolidated opt-out for all FCRA/GLB rights via single toll-free number. **Not** reasonable and simple: requiring the consumer to write their own letter; requiring the consumer to call to obtain a form; requiring paper-only opt-out for a consumer who received the notice electronically.

**Delivery (§1022.26):** Reasonable expectation of actual notice = hand delivery; mail to last known address; email to consumer who agreed to electronic disclosures; web posting requiring acknowledgment. Not reasonable: sign in branch; email to non-consenting consumer; web posting without acknowledgment.

**Renewal (§1022.27):** After 5-year opt-out period expires, must provide renewal notice before resuming solicitations. Renewal notice must be clear, conspicuous, and concise and contain 8 required items including: affiliate names, description of eligibility information, statement that prior election expired/is about to expire, election period, and opt-out method. Renewal period is also minimum 5 years. Cannot shorten opt-out period by sending a renewal notice early.

## Subpart D — Medical Information (§§1022.30–1022.32, 1022.38)

### §1022.30 — Creditor Prohibition on Medical Information in Credit Decisions

**General prohibition:** A creditor may not obtain or use medical information pertaining to a consumer in connection with any determination of the consumer's eligibility, or continued eligibility, for credit, except as provided in this section. **(§1022.30(b))**

**Unsolicited medical information:** If a creditor receives unsolicited medical information without specifically requesting it, the creditor does not violate the prohibition by receiving it but may only use it to the extent that an exception in §1022.30(e) applies. **(§1022.30(c))**

**Permitted exceptions (§1022.30(e)):**
1. Power of attorney or legal representative triggered by a medical condition
2. Legal compliance (state, federal law)
3. Consumer-requested special credit programs for medical conditions — must be in a written plan that identifies the class of beneficiaries and sets standards for extending credit
4. Fraud prevention or detection
5. Medical purpose loans — to determine and verify the medical purpose and use of proceeds
6. Consumer-specifically requested the use of medical information, documented by the creditor
7. Forbearance programs triggered by a medical condition
8. Debt cancellation/suspension contracts where a medical condition is a triggering event
9. Credit insurance products where a medical condition is a triggering event
10. Where all three conditions are met: (A) medical info is in account transaction information accessed with consumer's authorisation OR relates to income/benefits from a medical condition and is relied on as a source of repayment; (B) used no less favourably than comparable non-medical information; (C) not used to determine the consumer's physical/mental/behavioural health condition as part of the credit eligibility determination

### §1022.31 — Redisclosure Limits

Any person who receives medical information about a consumer from a CRA or affiliate must not disclose that information to any other person, except to carry out the original purpose or as otherwise permitted by statute, regulation, or order. **(§1022.31(b))**

### §1022.32 — Affiliate Sharing of Medical Information

The FCRA §603(d)(2) exclusions (which allow affiliates to share transaction/experience information without triggering consumer report status) do **not** apply to communications of: (1) medical information; (2) an individualised list of consumers based on payment transactions for medical products; or (3) an aggregate list of consumers based on payment transactions for medical products. Exceptions exist for insurance/annuities (NAIC model regulation), HIPAA-authorized purposes, GLB §502(e), credit eligibility determination per §1022.30, or by Bureau order. **(§1022.32)**

### §1022.38 — CRA Duty Regarding Medical Debt (effective January 14, 2025)

A CRA may include medical debt information in a consumer report furnished to a creditor only if the CRA: (1) has reason to believe the creditor intends to use the medical debt information in a manner not prohibited by §1022.30; and (2) has reason to believe the creditor is not otherwise legally prohibited from obtaining or using the medical debt information. **(§1022.38)**

## Subpart E — Duties of Furnishers (§§1022.40–1022.43)

Covers any person that furnishes information to a CRA. Does not apply to persons who provide information solely to obtain a consumer report, persons acting as a CRA, the consumer themselves, or character reference sources.

**Accuracy** means the furnished information correctly reflects the terms/liability for the account, the consumer's performance, and identifies the appropriate consumer. **(§1022.41(a))**

**Integrity** means the information is substantiated by the furnisher's records, furnished in a form minimising likelihood of incorrect reflection in a consumer report, and includes all information whose absence would be materially misleading. **(§1022.41(d))**

**Reasonable policies and procedures (§1022.42):** Each furnisher must establish, implement, review, and periodically update written policies and procedures for the accuracy and integrity of information it furnishes, considering Appendix E guidelines.

**Direct disputes (§1022.43):** See separate concept page — [[reg-v-furnisher-duties-direct-disputes]].

## Subpart F — Duties of Users (§1022.54)

**Prescreened solicitations (§1022.54):** Any person who uses a consumer report for a written firm offer of credit or insurance not initiated by the consumer must include a prescreen opt-out notice in every written solicitation, in two parts:
- **Short notice:** Statement of right to opt out and the toll-free number; reference to the long notice. Must be larger type than principal text (minimum 12pt); on front of first page; inside a border; distinct type style.
- **Long notice:** Full FCRA §615(d) content. Heading "PRESCREEN&OPT-OUT NOTICE" in capital letters and underlined. Minimum 8pt type. Set apart from other text with blank lines and indented margins.

## Subpart H — Risk-Based Pricing Notices (§§1022.70–1022.75)

See separate concept page — [[reg-v-risk-based-pricing-notices]].

**Scope:** Personal/family/household credit (not business credit). Applies when a person uses a consumer report and grants credit on materially less favourable terms than the most favourable terms available to a substantial proportion of consumers.

**Key exceptions:** Specific terms applied for and granted before obtaining the consumer report; adverse action notice given; prescreened solicitations. Credit score disclosure alternatives (§§1022.74(d)–(f)) eliminate the risk-based pricing notice obligation.

## Subpart I — Address Discrepancies (§1022.82)

**Notice of address discrepancy:** A CRA notice sent to a user that informs of a substantial difference between the address the user provided and the address(es) in the CRA's file.

**User obligations:** Must develop and implement reasonable policies and procedures to form a reasonable belief that the consumer report relates to the consumer (e.g., comparing with CIP records, own records, or third-party sources, or verifying with the consumer). **(§1022.82(c))**

**Address furnishing obligation:** When the user can form a reasonable belief, establishes a continuing relationship with the consumer, and regularly furnishes information to the CRA from which the discrepancy notice was received, the user must furnish a confirmed consumer address to that CRA as part of the regular reporting period in which the relationship is established. **(§1022.82(d))**

## Subpart M — CRA Identity Theft Duties (§§1022.121, 1022.123)

**Active duty alert duration (§1022.121):** 12 months.

**Proof of identity (§1022.123):** CRAs must develop and implement reasonable proof of identity requirements for consumers seeking file disclosures, fraud alert placements, and credit freeze requests under FCRA §§605A, 605B, 609(a)(1). Requirements must: (i) enable the CRA to match consumers with their files; and (ii) be commensurate with the identifiable risk of harm from misidentifying the consumer. Examples include full name, address, full SSN, date of birth, copies of government-issued ID, utility bills.

## Subpart N — CRA Disclosures to Consumers (§§1022.130, 1022.136–1022.138)

**Annual file disclosure (§1022.136):** All nationwide CRAs (the three major bureaux) must jointly design, fund, implement, and operate a centralised source for consumers to request annual file disclosures. The centralised source must offer: (i) a single dedicated website; (ii) a single dedicated toll-free telephone number; (iii) a single mailing address. PII collected may be used only for: providing the disclosure, processing a simultaneous consumer transaction, legal compliance, or updating PII in existing records. No marketing or advertising until after the consumer obtains the annual file disclosure. The centralised source may not require account setup or agreement to terms/conditions.

**Specialty CRA streamlined process (§1022.137):** Nationwide specialty CRAs must have a streamlined process for annual file disclosures via a toll-free number published in telephone directories and on related websites.

**Deceptive marketing of free credit reports (§1022.138):** AnnualCreditReport.com and (877) 322-8228 are the only authorised sources for the free credit reports required by Federal law. Any advertising that promotes a "free credit report" (i.e., one that is free only if a product or service is purchased or a subscription is entered) must include medium-specific disclosures:
- **TV:** "This is not the free credit report provided by Federal law" in audio and video, visual at least 4% of vertical picture height for minimum 4 seconds
- **Radio:** Same language at first mention
- **Print:** Centered disclosure "THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY LAW" followed by AnnualCreditReport.com/(877) 322-8228 language; each letter at least half the size of the largest character
- **Web:** Boxed header "THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY LAW" with hyperlink to consumerfinance.gov/learnmore; second element referencing AnnualCreditReport.com; operational link button "Take me to the authorized source"; all in sans serif font

## Subpart O — Miscellaneous CRA Duties (§§1022.140–1022.142)

**Anti-circumvention (§1022.140):** A CRA that compiles and maintains files on consumers on a nationwide basis may not reorganise, restructure, merge, acquire, divest, or otherwise restructure its operations to avoid nationwide CRA obligations under FCRA §603(p). Exception: bona fide, arm's length transactions with unaffiliated parties.

**Reasonable charges (§1022.141):** Charges for §609 file disclosures (beyond the free annual disclosure) may not exceed the maximum charge set by the Bureau under FCRA §612(f).

**Human trafficking — adverse information blocking (§1022.142):** See below for full detail.

### §1022.142 — Human Trafficking Adverse Information Block

CRAs must block adverse items resulting from severe trafficking or sex trafficking upon receipt of consumer's trafficking documentation. Block within **4 business days**. Must notify consumer within **5 business days** if additional information is needed to complete the submission. Final determination within **25 business days**. Cannot question the facts in the trafficking documentation as grounds to decline. Notice of outcome (including a free updated consumer report) within **5 business days** of final determination. Record retention: **7 years** from submission. Must maintain written policies and procedures. **(§1022.142)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Affiliate marketing opt-out covers all affiliates listed in the notice and lasts a minimum of 5 years.** Pre-existing business relationships (18 months for active contracts/transactions; 3 months for inquiries) are exceptions that eliminate the opt-out requirement entirely. **(§§1022.21–1022.22)**
- **The medical information prohibition is a creditor rule, not a CRA rule.** Creditors cannot obtain or use medical information in credit eligibility decisions; the rule applies at the point of credit decision, not at the point of report generation. The §1022.30(e) exceptions are exhaustive — no general "necessary for underwriting" exception exists. **(§1022.30)**
- **Medical debt information at CRAs now has a gatekeeping obligation.** Since January 14, 2025, CRAs may only include medical debt in a consumer report furnished to a creditor if they have reason to believe the creditor's use is non-prohibited and not legally barred. **(§1022.38)**
- **Furnishers must investigate direct disputes — not just CRA disputes.** A consumer can send a dispute directly to the furnisher (at the address in the consumer report, a specified dispute address, or any business address), and the furnisher must investigate independently. The FCRA §611 timelines apply. **(§1022.43)**
- **Risk-based pricing notice is avoided if a credit score disclosure is provided instead.** For mortgage loans (1–4 residential units) and other consumer credit extensions, providing the credit score disclosure notice (§1022.74(d) or (e)) eliminates the risk-based pricing notice obligation. This is the common compliance path for lenders. **(§1022.74)**
- **Human trafficking blocks are mandatory within 4 business days.** CRAs cannot use questioning of the underlying trafficking facts as a basis to decline the block — only identity failure, missing victim determination, or inability to identify the adverse items are valid grounds. **(§1022.142)**
