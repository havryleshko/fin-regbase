# 12 CFR Part 1024 — Regulation X (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act)

## Overview

Regulation X implements the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA, 12 USC 2601 et seq.), which Congress enacted in 1974 to ensure that consumers receive timely disclosures in connection with residential real estate transactions and to eliminate kickbacks and referral fees that inflate settlement service costs. The CFPB inherited rulemaking authority from HUD on the designated transfer date (July 21, 2011).

The regulation is divided into three substantive subparts:
- **Subpart A** — General provisions and definitions (§§1024.1–1024.5)
- **Subpart B** — Mortgage loan applications and settlement disclosures (§§1024.6–1024.20)
- **Subpart C** — Mortgage loan servicing (§§1024.30–1024.41)

---

## Coverage and Scope

### Covered Loans — "Federally Related Mortgage Loan" (§1024.2)

Regulation X applies to any "federally related mortgage loan" — a loan secured by a first or subordinate lien on 1–4 family residential real property where at least one of the following applies:
- The loan is made by a federally regulated lender (national bank, federal savings association, credit union, or lender insured by a federal agency)
- The loan is insured, guaranteed, supplemented, or assisted by a federal agency (FHA, VA, USDA)
- The loan is intended to be sold or transferred to FNMA, FHLMC, or GNMA
- The loan is made by a creditor that made or invested in residential real estate loans aggregating more than $1,000,000 in the preceding calendar year **(§1024.2)**

### Exempt Transactions (§1024.5(b))

The following are expressly exempt from all or most Regulation X provisions:
- **Business purpose loans** — loans made primarily for business, commercial, or agricultural purposes
- **Temporary construction loans** — unless used as or converted to permanent financing by the same financial institution
- **Vacant land loans** — unless a dwelling will be constructed or placed on the land
- **Assumption without lender approval** — loan assumptions where the lender does not have approval rights
- **Loan conversions** — conversion of an existing obligation at the same institution (e.g., ARM to fixed) without a new note
- **Secondary market transfers** — purchase or transfer of a mortgage loan in the secondary market (though the origination remains subject to RESPA) **(§1024.5(b))**

## Subpart B — Mortgage Settlement Disclosures (§§1024.6–1024.20)

### Special Information Booklet (§1024.6)

Lenders must provide applicants with the CFPB's "Your Home Loan Toolkit" (previously the HUD special information booklet) **at the time of application or within 3 business days of receipt of application**. This requirement does not apply where the applicant is unlikely to qualify for financing or where the transaction is a refinancing of an existing loan with the same lender. **(§1024.6)**

### Good Faith Estimate (§1024.7)

The GFE is the pre-TRID disclosure form governing federally related mortgage loan applications not covered by the RESPA-TILA Integrated Disclosure (TRID) rules (i.e., applications received before October 3, 2015, or non-TRID-covered transactions).

**Delivery timing:** The lender must provide the GFE **within 3 business days** of receiving the application. **(§1024.7(a))**

**Binding effect:** The GFE is binding — the lender and settlement service providers identified on the GFE cannot charge fees that exceed applicable tolerances at settlement. **(§1024.7(f))**

**Changed circumstances:** A lender may issue a revised GFE within 3 business days of receiving information that constitutes a "changed circumstance" affecting the estimated charges. Changed circumstances include: information not relied upon at time of original GFE that subsequently becomes available; changes in loan terms requested by the borrower; and interest rate locks/unlocks. **(§1024.7(f)(2))**

**Tolerance tiers (§1024.7(e)):**

| Category | Tolerance |
|----------|-----------|
| Origination charges; locked-rate credit or charge; locked adjusted origination charges; transfer taxes | **Zero tolerance** — cannot increase at settlement |
| Lender-required services where lender selects provider; lender-required title/title insurance using lender-identified provider; government recording charges | **10% aggregate tolerance** |
| All other services (e.g., optional owner's title insurance, survey, pest inspection, homeowner's insurance) | **Unlimited** — may change without restriction |

A lender that underestimates a zero-tolerance charge absorbs the difference — there is no cure mechanism. **(§1024.7(e))**

### HUD-1 / HUD-1A Settlement Statement (§1024.8)

The HUD-1 (for purchase transactions) and HUD-1A (for refinance transactions with no seller) are the closing settlement statements. The borrower must receive the HUD-1 at or before settlement. The borrower has the right to inspect the HUD-1 the **business day before settlement** upon request. **(§1024.8)**

The HUD-1 must itemise all settlement charges paid by both borrower and seller. Settlement agents are responsible for its preparation. Tolerances from the GFE govern whether settlement charges are permissible. **(§1024.8(b))**

### Section 8 — Kickbacks and Unearned Fees (§1024.14)

No person shall give or accept any **fee, kickback, or thing of value** pursuant to any **agreement or understanding** (oral, written, or established by practice) that business incident to a RESPA-covered transaction will be referred to any person. No person shall give or accept any portion, split, or percentage of any charge other than for services actually performed. **(§1024.14(b)–(c))**

See [[reg-x-section-8-kickbacks-afba]] for full standards including the "thing of value" and "agreement or understanding" definitions, AfBA safe harbor, Section 8 exceptions, and civil liability framework.

### Affiliated Business Arrangements (§1024.15)

A referral by an owner of an affiliated settlement service provider is not a Section 8 violation if three conditions are met simultaneously:
1. Written disclosure at time of referral in Appendix D format
2. No requirement to use the affiliated provider (with narrow exceptions)
3. The only thing of value from the arrangement is a return on ownership interest or bona fide franchise fee **(§1024.15)**

See [[reg-x-section-8-kickbacks-afba]] for full AfBA mechanics.

### Escrow Accounts (§1024.17)

Escrow accounts for taxes and insurance are subject to quantitative caps: a cushion cannot exceed **1/6 of estimated total annual disbursements** (approximately 2 months of escrow payments). Aggregate accounting is mandatory — the servicer must compute each account as a single pool, not item by item. **(§1024.17)**

See [[reg-x-escrow-accounts]] for full limits, surplus/shortage mechanics, and statement requirements.

## Subpart C — Mortgage Servicing (§§1024.30–1024.41)

### Scope of Subpart C (§1024.30)

Subpart C applies to servicers of "federally related mortgage loans" as defined in §1024.2. Small servicer exemptions apply to servicers that service 5,000 or fewer mortgage loans, all of which the servicer (or an affiliate) originated or owns. Small servicers are exempt from §§1024.41 (loss mitigation) and the continuity of contact requirements of §1024.40. **(§1024.30(b))**

### Servicing Transfer Notices (§1024.33)

When a mortgage loan servicing right is transferred:
- **Transferor (outgoing) servicer** must notify the borrower **no later than 15 days before the effective date** of the transfer
- **Transferee (incoming) servicer** must notify the borrower **no later than 15 days after the effective date** of the transfer
- A **combined notice** may be sent at least 15 days before the effective date
- **60-day grace period:** A payment sent to the old servicer (transferor) within 60 days after the effective transfer date cannot be treated as late **(§1024.33)**

### Timely Escrow Payments (§1024.34)

The servicer must make escrow disbursements by the disbursement date — the date on which the payment is due such that the payee will not charge a late fee or penalty (or the last date a discount is available). Servicers cannot maintain a cushion merely to cover potential late fees. **(§1024.34)**

### Error Resolution (§1024.35)

Borrowers may submit written notices of error for any of 11 defined categories, including:
- Failure to accept or credit a payment made in accordance with a written agreement
- Failure to apply a payment to principal, interest, escrow, or other charges as required by the loan documents
- Imposition of a fee or charge the servicer has no reasonable basis to impose
- Failure to provide an accurate payoff balance within 7 business days of a written request
- Failure to provide accurate loss mitigation information
- Making the first foreclosure notice or filing in violation of §1024.41
- Conducting a foreclosure sale in violation of §1024.41 **(§1024.35(b))**

The servicer must acknowledge within **5 business days** and respond within **30 days** (7 business days for payoff errors; before the foreclosure sale or within 30 days for foreclosure-related errors). A 15-day extension is available if the servicer provides written notice before the deadline. The servicer cannot charge any fee as a condition of responding. For **60 days** after receiving a notice of error, the servicer cannot report adverse credit information to any consumer reporting agency on any payment that is the subject of the notice. **(§1024.35)**

See [[reg-x-mortgage-servicing]] for the full error resolution framework.

### Information Requests (§1024.36)

Borrowers may request information about the servicing of their loan. The servicer must acknowledge within **5 business days** and respond within **10 business days** (30 business days for identity of the owner of the loan). The servicer cannot charge any fee for standard information requests. A servicer may designate a specific address for information requests; if so, the servicer need only respond to requests sent to that address. **(§1024.36)**

### Force-Placed Insurance (§1024.37)

A servicer may not charge a borrower for force-placed insurance unless: (1) the servicer has sent two written notices (first ≥45 days before charging; reminder ≥30 days after first notice and ≥15 days before charging) that the borrower lacks required hazard insurance coverage; and (2) the servicer has not received proof of coverage. Charges must be bona fide and reasonable. **(§1024.37)**

### General Servicing Policies and Procedures (§1024.38)

Servicers must maintain policies and procedures reasonably designed to:
- Ensure accurate and timely transfer of borrower information in loan transfers
- Provide borrowers with accurate and timely information and documents regarding loss mitigation options
- Properly evaluate borrowers for all available loss mitigation options
- Facilitate oversight of third-party providers **(§1024.38)**

### Early Intervention (§1024.39)

Within **36 days** after a borrower becomes delinquent, the servicer must make **live contact** with the borrower (or make good-faith efforts to do so) and inform the borrower about the availability of loss mitigation options. If the borrower is still delinquent at **45 days**, the servicer must send a **written notice** providing information about loss mitigation options and identifying the continuity of contact personnel. **(§1024.39)**

### Continuity of Contact (§1024.40)

Servicers must maintain policies and procedures reasonably designed to assign a "single point of contact" (SPOC) or a team of personnel to delinquent borrowers. The SPOC must:
- Be accessible by phone to the borrower
- Have direct access to all information and personnel needed to process loss mitigation applications
- Have authority to stop foreclosure proceedings as appropriate while a loss mitigation application is being reviewed **(§1024.40)**

### Loss Mitigation — Dual Tracking Prohibition (§1024.41)

A servicer may not make the first notice or filing required for any judicial or non-judicial foreclosure process if:
- The servicer has received a **complete loss mitigation application** from the borrower **37 or more days before a scheduled foreclosure sale** **(§1024.41(f))**

The servicer must evaluate all available loss mitigation options within **30 days** of receiving a complete application. The borrower has an **appeal right** for denials of loan modification options. See [[reg-x-mortgage-servicing]] for the full dual tracking prohibition and loss mitigation timeline.

## Relationship to TRID (RESPA-TILA Integrated Disclosure)

For most closed-end consumer credit transactions secured by real property received on or after **October 3, 2015**, the CFPB's TRID rules (implementing Dodd-Frank §1098/§1100A) replaced the GFE and HUD-1 with the Loan Estimate (LE) and Closing Disclosure (CD). TRID is implemented primarily through Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026 Subpart E), not Regulation X, though RESPA's Section 8 kickback prohibition and escrow account rules remain fully operative for TRID-covered transactions. The tolerance regime in TRID mirrors the GFE tolerance structure but is codified in Reg Z, not Reg X. **(Dodd-Frank §§1098, 1100A)**

## Civil Liability and Enforcement

RESPA Section 8 violations expose violators to:
- **Criminal liability:** Up to 1 year imprisonment and a $10,000 fine per violation **(12 USC 2607(d)(1))**
- **Civil liability:** Equal to 3 times the amount of any charge paid for the settlement service, plus costs and attorneys' fees **(12 USC 2607(d)(2))**
- **Regulatory action:** CFPB administrative or civil enforcement under its Title X Subtitle E authority

The CFPB enforces Regulation X as an "enumerated consumer law" under Dodd-Frank §1002(12)(M). CFPB civil penalties of up to $1,000,000 per day for knowing violations apply in addition to RESPA's own civil liability provisions. **(12 USC 5565)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **RESPA scope is defined by "federally related mortgage loan" — nearly all 1–4 family residential mortgage originations in the US meet this definition.** Any fintech originating or servicing residential mortgage loans faces Regulation X obligations. **(§1024.2)**
- **Section 8's kickback prohibition applies to any "agreement or understanding" — no written contract is required.** A pattern or course of conduct between two companies that consistently refer business to each other can establish the requisite understanding. **(§1024.14)**
- **GFE zero-tolerance charges cannot increase at all at settlement.** Origination charges, transfer taxes, and locked-rate charges must match the GFE exactly. There is no cure mechanism for zero-tolerance violations. **(§1024.7(e))**
- **The 60-day adverse credit freeze after a notice of error is automatic.** The servicer cannot report any adverse credit information on a disputed payment for 60 days after receiving a notice of error, regardless of the outcome of the investigation. **(§1024.35)**
- **Dual tracking is prohibited once a complete loss mitigation application is received 37+ days before a foreclosure sale.** The 37-day bright line applies to the first foreclosure notice or filing — servicers cannot initiate foreclosure proceedings against a borrower who has submitted a timely complete application. **(§1024.41(f))**
