# 31 CFR Part 1010 — Bank Secrecy Act General Provisions

## Overview

31 CFR Part 1010 contains the **general provisions** of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) regulations administered by FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network) under the Department of the Treasury. It applies to all financial institutions defined in 31 USC 5312(a)(2) or (c)(1) and establishes cross-cutting obligations that apply regardless of institution type. Sector-specific requirements are contained in Parts 1020–1030 (for banks, broker-dealers, MSBs, insurance companies, etc.), which supplement but do not replace Part 1010. **(§1010.100 et seq.)**

The BSA's primary purpose is to require financial institutions to maintain records and file reports that have a high degree of usefulness in criminal, tax, or regulatory investigations. **(§1010.301)**

---

## Scope and Definitions (Subpart A — §1010.100)

### Key Entities

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Financial institution** | Banks, broker-dealers, MSBs, insurance companies, dealers in precious metals/stones, casinos, and many others (31 USC 5312(a)(2)) |
| **Money services business (MSB)** | Dealer in foreign exchange; check casher; issuer of traveler's checks/money orders; money transmitter; provider of prepaid access; seller of prepaid access. MSBs with >$1,000 in covered services per person per day in more than one transaction must register with FinCEN **(§1010.100(ff))** |
| **Structuring** | Breaking up a transaction or a series of transactions to evade reporting requirements **(§1010.100(xx))** |
| **Currency** | Coin and currency of the United States or any other country |

## Subpart B — Programs (§§1010.205–1010.230)

### AML Program Exemptions (§1010.205)

Certain financial institutions listed in 31 USC 5312(a)(2) that are subject to examination by federal functional regulators are exempt from the AML program requirement in 31 USC 5318(h)(1) — they rely on their sector-specific program rules instead.

### CDD and Beneficial Ownership Rule (§1010.230)

Covered financial institutions must identify and verify the beneficial owners of **legal entity customers** at account opening. Two prongs:

1. **Ownership prong:** Each natural person who directly or indirectly owns 25% or more of the equity interests (up to four individuals; zero if no one meets the threshold)
2. **Control prong:** One individual with significant responsibility for managing the legal entity (e.g., CEO, CFO, COO, Managing Member, General Partner, President)

**16 excluded legal entity customer categories** include: regulated financial institutions, publicly-traded companies, government entities, registered investment companies, and others. **(§1010.230(e))**

**Reliance:** A covered FI may rely on another FI's performance of these requirements for shared customers, provided the other FI is subject to a rule implementing 31 USC 5318(h) and regulated by a federal functional regulator, and enters into a contract to annually certify compliance. **(§1010.230(j))**

**Retention:** 5 years from account closing. **(§1010.230(i))**

## Subpart C — Reports Required (§§1010.300–1010.380)

Five core reporting obligations apply across all financial institutions (sector-specific parts may add additional requirements):

### Currency Transaction Reports — CTR (§1010.311)

Each financial institution (other than a casino) shall file a CTR for each deposit, withdrawal, exchange, or payment involving **more than $10,000 in currency** in a single business day. Multiple transactions aggregated if the institution has knowledge they are by or on behalf of the same person and total more than $10,000. Filed with FinCEN **within 15 days**; retained 5 years. **(§§1010.311, 1010.306, 1010.313)**

Structuring to evade CTR filing is prohibited. **(§1010.314)**

### Suspicious Activity Reports — SAR (§1010.320)

Each financial institution must refer to its sector-specific part (e.g., Part 1020 for banks) for SAR thresholds and requirements. The general provision establishes FinCEN as the receiving body.

### Form 8300 — Cash in Trade or Business (§1010.330)

Any person (not just financial institutions) who receives **more than $10,000 in currency** in the course of a trade or business in one transaction (or two or more related transactions) must report to FinCEN. Filed using Form 8300 **within 15 days**. Multiple installment payments aggregate when total exceeds $10,000. **(§1010.330)**

### Currency or Monetary Instruments Report — CMIR (§1010.340)

Any person who physically transports, mails, or ships (or causes to be transported) **more than $10,000 in currency or monetary instruments** into or out of the United States at one time must file a CMIR. Filed at time of crossing (or within 15 days of receipt for incoming shipments). Filed with Customs/CBP. **(§1010.340)**

### Report of Foreign Financial Accounts — FBAR (§1010.350)

Any **United States person** (citizen, resident, or entity) with a financial interest in or signature/other authority over a bank, securities, or other financial account in a foreign country exceeding **$10,000 aggregate at any point during the calendar year** must file an FBAR with the Commissioner of Internal Revenue by **June 30** of the following year (FinCEN Form 114). **(§1010.350)**

Exceptions: government accounts, IRA/pension accounts, certain trust beneficiaries, accounts in US military banking facilities. **(§1010.350(c)(4), (g))**

### Beneficial Ownership Information Report — BOI (§1010.380)

Implements the Corporate Transparency Act. **Reporting companies** (domestic corps/LLCs and foreign entities registered to do business in a US state) must file BOI directly with FinCEN. **(§1010.380)**

**Definition of reporting company:** Any entity that is a corporation, LLC, or other entity created or registered by filing a document with a secretary of state. **(§1010.380(c)(1))**

**23+ exemption categories** include: SEC-reporting issuers, government authorities, banks, credit unions, depository holding companies, MSBs registered with FinCEN, broker-dealers, investment companies/advisers, VC fund advisers, insurance companies, state-licensed insurance producers, CEA-registered entities, accounting firms, public utilities, financial market utilities, pooled investment vehicles, tax-exempt entities, large operating companies (>20 full-time US employees, US physical office, >$5M gross receipts/sales), subsidiaries of certain exempt entities, inactive entities. **(§1010.380(c)(2))**

**Beneficial owner:** Any individual who directly or indirectly (i) exercises substantial control over the company, or (ii) owns or controls at least 25% of the ownership interests. **(§1010.380(d))**

Substantial control includes: serving as senior officer; authority over appointment/removal of senior officers or majority of directors; directing important decisions (nature of business, major expenditures, reorganisation, compensation schemes, significant contracts, governance documents). **(§1010.380(d)(1))**

**Exceptions to "beneficial owner":** Minor children (report parent/guardian instead); nominees/intermediaries/custodians/agents acting on behalf of another; non-senior-officer employees whose control derives solely from employment; persons with only a future interest through inheritance; creditors with only predetermined debt rights. **(§1010.380(d)(3))**

**Reporting deadlines:**
- Entities formed on or after March 26, 2025: file within 30 days of formation
- Entities formed before March 26, 2025: filed by April 25, 2025
- Changes: updated report within 30 days of any change
- Corrections: corrected report within 30 days of becoming aware of inaccuracy **(§1010.380(a))**

**FinCEN identifier:** An individual or entity may obtain a FinCEN identifier and provide it to reporting companies in lieu of repeating all required identifying information. **(§1010.380(b)(4))**

**BOI confidentiality:** Information filed under §1010.380 is strictly confidential and may only be disclosed by FinCEN to: federal law enforcement/national security agencies; state/local law enforcement with court authorisation; foreign law enforcement via treaty; financial institutions (with reporting company consent) for CDD purposes; regulatory agencies supervising FIs for CDD compliance. **(§1010.955)**

**Penalties:** Civil $606/day for BOI reporting violations; criminal penalties (willful false reporting). **(§§1010.380(g), 1010.821)**

## Subpart D — Records Required (§§1010.400–1010.440)

### General Recordkeeping (§1010.410)

Financial institutions must retain:
- Records of credit extensions > $10,000 (name, address, amount, nature, date)
- Records of international fund transmittals > $10,000
- **Travel rule:** Transmittal orders ≥ $3,000 — transmitter's FI must include and pass forward transmitter/recipient info (name, address, account number, TIN, identity of recipient's FI). Intermediary FIs must pass this forward. Records retained 5 years. **(§1010.410(e)–(f))**

### Monetary Instruments — $3,000–$10,000 (§1010.415)

No financial institution may issue or sell a bank check, cashier's check, money order, or traveler's check for $3,000 or more in currency without maintaining records of the purchaser (name, address, DOB, SSN/TIN, instrument type and serial number, amount). Contemporaneous purchases totalling $3,000+ treated as one purchase. Records retained 5 years. **(§1010.415)**

### Retention Period (§1010.430)

All records required by Part 1010 must be retained for **5 years**. Records must remain accessible within a reasonable time. **(§1010.430(d))**

## Subpart E — Information Sharing (§§1010.500–1010.540)

### 314(a) — Law Enforcement Requests (§1010.520)

FinCEN may direct any financial institution, on behalf of a law enforcement agency investigating terrorist activity or money laundering, to search its records for any named individual, entity, or organisation. The FI must:
- Search for current and prior-year accounts and transactions in the preceding 6 months
- Report to FinCEN any positive match (account numbers, transaction details, identifying information)
- Designate a 314(a) point of contact
- Keep the request strictly confidential **(§1010.520(b))**

The FI may not use 314(a) information for any purpose other than reporting, account/transaction decisions, or its own BSA compliance. **(§1010.520(b)(3)(iv))**

### 314(b) — Voluntary Information Sharing (§1010.540)

Financial institutions may voluntarily share information about individuals, entities, and organisations suspected of ML or terrorist activity with other financial institutions **under a safe harbour from liability**, provided both institutions have filed a notice with FinCEN (valid for one year; renews annually). The sharing institution must verify that the recipient has also filed a notice. **(§1010.540(b))**

Information received under 314(b) may only be used for: identifying and reporting ML/terrorist activity; account/transaction decisions; BSA compliance. **(§1010.540(b)(4))**

## Subpart F — Special Standards of Diligence; Prohibitions; Special Measures (§§1010.600–1010.670)

### Definitions (§1010.605)

Key definitions for Subpart F:
- **Correspondent account:** Account established for a foreign financial institution (FFI) to receive deposits from, or make payments on behalf of, the FFI, or to handle other financial transactions of the FFI
- **Foreign shell bank:** A foreign bank without a physical presence in any country
- **Private banking account:** Account (or combination of accounts) with minimum aggregate deposit ≥$1,000,000, established for one or more non-US persons as direct or beneficial owners, administered by a relationship manager
- **Senior foreign political figure (PEP):** Current or former senior official in the executive, legislative, administrative, military, or judicial branch of a foreign government; senior official of a major foreign political party; senior executive of a foreign government-owned commercial enterprise; plus immediate family members and close associates

### Correspondent Account Due Diligence (§1010.610)

Covered financial institutions must establish a **risk-based due diligence programme** for all correspondent accounts maintained for foreign financial institutions. The programme must include:
1. Assessing ML risk based on: nature of the FFI's business, jurisdiction's AML/supervisory regime, FFI's AML record, type and anticipated activity of the account
2. Applying risk-based procedures and controls, including periodic review

**Enhanced due diligence is mandatory** for correspondent accounts held by foreign banks operating under:
- An offshore banking licence
- A banking licence issued by a FATF non-cooperative jurisdiction
- A banking licence in a jurisdiction designated by the Secretary for special measures

Enhanced DD includes: enhanced scrutiny of the account; monitoring for ML and suspicious activity; for payable-through accounts, obtaining identity of persons authorised to direct transactions and sources/beneficial owners of funds; obtaining identity of nested correspondent bank customers. **(§1010.610(b))**

### Private Banking Account Due Diligence (§1010.620)

Covered FIs must maintain a due diligence programme for private banking accounts (≥$1M, for non-US persons). Minimum requirements:
1. Ascertain identity of **all nominal and beneficial owners**
2. Determine whether any owner is a **senior foreign political figure (PEP)**
3. Ascertain source(s) of funds deposited
4. Review account activity for consistency with stated purpose and expected use

**Enhanced scrutiny for PEP accounts:** Must be reasonably designed to detect and report transactions that may involve the **proceeds of foreign corruption** (defined broadly: misappropriation, theft, embezzlement of public funds, bribery, extortion, conversion of government property). **(§1010.620(c))**

### Shell Bank Prohibition (§1010.630)

Covered financial institutions must **not** establish, maintain, administer, or manage a correspondent account in the United States for, or on behalf of, a **foreign shell bank**. **(§1010.630(a)(1)(i))**

FIs must also take reasonable steps to ensure that any correspondent account established for a foreign bank is **not being used** by that foreign bank to indirectly provide services to a foreign shell bank. **(§1010.630(a)(1)(ii))**

**Recertification safe harbour:** FI is deemed in compliance if it obtains a certification (or recertification) from the foreign bank **at least once every three years**. If certification is not obtained within 30 calendar days of account establishment, and at least every 3 years thereafter, the FI must close all accounts with that foreign bank within a commercially reasonable time. **(§§1010.630(b), (d))**

FIs must maintain records of owners of non-publicly-traded foreign banks and a US agent for service of legal process. **Records retained 5 years after termination of the correspondent relationship.** **(§1010.630(a)(2), (e))**

### Special Measures (§§1010.651–1010.664)

FinCEN has imposed entity-specific and country-level special measures under Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act prohibiting US financial institutions from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for designated institutions, and requiring enhanced due diligence to prevent indirect use:

| Designation | Section | Prohibition |
|------------|---------|------------|
| Burma (banking institutions) | §1010.651 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use |
| Commercial Bank of Syria | §1010.653 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use |
| FBME Bank, Ltd. | §1010.658 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use |
| North Korea (banking/financial institutions) | §1010.659 | No correspondent accounts; no transactions involving North Korean FIs |
| Bank of Dandong | §1010.660 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use |
| Iran (financial institutions) | §1010.661 | No correspondent accounts (unless OFAC-authorised); prevent prohibited transactions |
| Al-Huda Bank | §1010.663 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use [added July 2024] |
| Huione Group | §1010.664 | No correspondent accounts; prevent indirect use [added October 2025] |

For each, FIs must: notify correspondent account holders; take reasonable steps to identify indirect use; take risk-based additional measures; terminate accounts where indirect use detected. **(§§1010.651–1010.664)**

### Subpoena and Termination of Correspondent Relationship (§1010.670)

The Secretary or Attorney General may issue a summons or subpoena to any foreign bank with a US correspondent account for records related to that account, including records held outside the US. If the foreign bank fails to comply or to contest the summons in a US court, the covered FI must **terminate the correspondent relationship within 10 business days** of written notice from the Secretary or AG. Failure to terminate: civil penalty up to **$10,000 per day**. **(§1010.670(d), (f))**

## Subpart H — Enforcement and Penalties (§§1010.810–1010.850)

### Enforcement Authority

Overall authority delegated to the **Director, FinCEN**. Examination authority delegated to: OCC (national banks), Federal Reserve (state member banks), FDIC (state non-member banks), NCUA (credit unions), SEC (broker-dealers, investment companies), CFTC (futures commission merchants), CBP (CMIR), IRS (all FIs not otherwise covered). **(§1010.810)**

### Civil Penalties (§1010.821)

Inflation-adjusted maximum civil monetary penalties (effective January 17, 2025):

| Violation | Adjusted Maximum |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Willful BSA violations (general) | $71,545–$286,184 per violation |
| Pattern of negligent activity | $111,308 |
| Due diligence / shell bank / special measures violations | $1,776,364 per violation |
| Failure to terminate correspondent relationship | $17,765 per day |
| BOI reporting violation | $606 per day |
| Failure to register as MSB | $10,556 |

### Criminal Penalties (§1010.840)

Willful violations of BSA requirements: up to $250,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment. Pattern of illegal activity involving >$100,000 in a 12-month period: up to $500,000 and/or 10 years imprisonment. Making false, fictitious, or fraudulent statements in required reports: up to $10,000 and/or 5 years imprisonment. **(§1010.840)**

### Informant Rewards (§1010.930)

Individuals who provide original information leading to a recovery exceeding $50,000 for a BSA violation may receive a reward up to the lesser of 25% of the net fine/penalty/forfeiture or $150,000. **(§1010.930)**

## Key Points for Agents

- **Part 1010 is the cross-cutting foundation; sector-specific parts (1020–1030) layer on top.** Every financial institution subject to the BSA is bound by Part 1010. Sector parts add institution-specific SAR thresholds, AML programme requirements, and other obligations. **(§1010.300)**
- **Five distinct reporting regimes with different thresholds, recipients, and deadlines.** CTR (>$10K cash, 15 days to FinCEN); SAR (sector-specific, FinCEN); Form 8300 (>$10K cash in trade/business, 15 days to FinCEN); CMIR (>$10K crossing border, CBP at time of crossing); FBAR (>$10K foreign accounts at any point in year, IRS by June 30). **(§§1010.311, 1010.320, 1010.330, 1010.340, 1010.350)**
- **BOI reporting (§1010.380) is a CTA obligation separate from the CDD rule (§1010.230).** CDD requires FIs to collect beneficial ownership at account opening. BOI requires companies themselves to proactively disclose their beneficial owners to FinCEN. Both apply simultaneously; BOI data may be shared back to FIs for CDD purposes with company consent. **(§§1010.230, 1010.380)**
- **The shell bank prohibition is absolute; correspondent account DD is risk-based.** No US FI may provide correspondent banking to a foreign shell bank under any circumstances. Correspondent DD for legitimate foreign banks is risk-based, but EDD is mandatory for offshore-licensed, FATF non-cooperative, and specially-designated banks. **(§§1010.610, 1010.630)**
- **314(a) requests are mandatory and confidential; 314(b) sharing is voluntary with safe harbour.** FinCEN can compel record searches for named ML/terrorist suspects under 314(a). FIs may voluntarily share ML/terrorist activity information with peer FIs under 314(b) after filing annual notice — but must keep the information confidential and use it only for BSA/AML purposes. **(§§1010.520, 1010.540)**
