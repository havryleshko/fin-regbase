# COBS 2 — Conduct of Business Obligations (Sourcebook Reference)

## Structure and Application

COBS 2 sets the foundational conduct standards that underpin all other COBS obligations. Its nine substantive sections span the general duty to act in clients' best interests, pre-service information disclosure, shareholder engagement requirements, inducements (in two regimes), research payment rules, chain-of-responsibility when agents act for clients, and restrictions on optional product bundling.

**Application split — MiFID vs non-MiFID:** Many sections of COBS 2 run in parallel pairs: a non-MiFID version (applying to designated investment business for retail clients) and a MiFID/equivalent version (applying to MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business and, where specified, insurance distribution). The MiFID versions (COBS 2.2A, 2.3A) are the operative rules for most FCA-authorised investment firms providing services in financial instruments to retail clients.

## COBS 2.1 — Client's Best Interests Rule

### The Rule

**COBS 2.1.1R** — A firm must **act honestly, fairly and professionally in accordance with the best interests of its client** (the *client's best interests rule*).

Scope of application:
- **Designated investment business** carried on for a **retail client**
- **MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business** for **any client** (retail, professional, or ECP)
- **Insurance distribution** for **any client**
- For management companies: in relation to any UCITS scheme the firm manages **(COBS 2.1.1R(3))**

[Note: article 24(1) of MiFID, article 17(1) of the IDD and article 14(1)(a) and (b) of the UCITS Directive]

**COBS 2.1.1AR** — In relation to eligible counterparty business, a firm must still act honestly, fairly and professionally, taking into account the nature of the eligible counterparty and its business.

### Exclusion of Liability

**COBS 2.1.2R** — A firm must not, in any communication relating to designated investment business, seek to exclude or restrict, or rely on any exclusion or restriction of, any duty or liability it may have to a client under the regulatory system.

**COBS 2.1.3G** — This applies to any communication to a retail client relating to designated investment business. General law (including the Unfair Terms Regulations and the Consumer Rights Act) also limits a firm's ability to exclude or restrict duties or liabilities to a consumer.

### AIFMs

**COBS 2.1.4R** — A full-scope UK AIFM must, for all AIFs it manages:
1. Act honestly, fairly and with due skill care and diligence
2. Act in the best interests of the AIF and its investors
3. Treat all investors fairly
4. Not allow any investor preferential treatment unless disclosed in the AIF's instrument constituting the fund

### Relationship to Consumer Duty

The client's best interests rule (COBS 2.1.1R) is the COBS expression of Principle 6 (customers' interests) and Principle 12 (Consumer Duty) for investment business. **COBS 2.3.3G** confirms that the obligation to act honestly, fairly and professionally includes both the client's best interests rule and duties under Principles 1 (integrity), 2 (skill, care and diligence) and 6 (customers' interests). A breach of COBS 2.1.1R is simultaneously likely to be a breach of the client's best interests rule and a Principle violation.

## COBS 2.2 — Pre-Service Information Disclosure (Non-MiFID)

### Application

**COBS 2.2.-1R(2)** — Applies to designated investment business (other than MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business or insurance distribution activities) carried on for a retail client:
- For derivatives, warrants, non-readily realisable securities, non-mass market investments, P2P agreements or stock lending: the matters in COBS 2.2.1R(1)(b) only
- For retail investment products: the matters in COBS 2.2.1R(1)(a) and (d) only

### Core Obligation

**COBS 2.2.1R** — A firm must provide appropriate information in a comprehensible form to a client about:
- (a) the firm and its services
- (b) designated investments and proposed investment strategies, including appropriate guidance on and warnings of the risks associated with those investments or strategies
- (c) execution venues
- (d) costs and associated charges

so that the client is reasonably able to understand the nature and risks of the service and of the specific type of designated investment being offered, and consequently to take investment decisions on an informed basis. Information may be provided in a standardised format. **(COBS 2.2.1R)**

**COBS 2.2.2G** — A firm to which COBS 2.2.1R applies should also consider the rules on disclosing information about a firm, its services, costs and associated charges and designated investments in COBS 6.1 and COBS 14.

### Stewardship Code Disclosure

**COBS 2.2.3R** — A firm, other than a venture capital firm, which is managing investments for a professional client and is not a natural person must disclose clearly on its website (or if no website, in another accessible form):
1. The nature of its commitment to the Financial Reporting Council's Stewardship Code; or
2. Where it does not commit to the Code, its alternative investment strategy.

## COBS 2.2A — Pre-Service Information Disclosure (MiFID and Insurance Distribution)

### Application

**COBS 2.2A.1R** — Applies to a firm:
1. In relation to its MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business
2. Carrying on insurance distribution activities in relation to (a) an IBIP for any client; and/or (b) any other life policy for a retail client but as regards the matters in COBS 2.2A.2R(1)(a) and (d) only

### Core Obligation

**COBS 2.2A.2R** — A firm must provide appropriate information **in good time** to a client with regard to:
- (a) the firm and its services
- (b) for financial instruments: financial instruments, proposed investment strategies and execution venues
- (c) for IBIPs: distribution of IBIPs including appropriate guidance on and warnings of risks
- (d) all costs and related charges

Information may be provided in a standardised format. [Note: article 24(4) of MiFID and article 29(1)(b) of the IDD]

**COBS 2.2A.2AR** — For an IBIP, the firm must provide the information in good time prior to the conclusion of the contract. [Note: first paragraph of article 29(1) of IDD]

**COBS 2.2A.3R** — The information must be in a comprehensible form in such a manner that a client is reasonably able to understand the nature and risks of the investment service and of the specific type of financial instrument or life policy being offered, and consequently to take investment decisions on an informed basis. Information may be provided in a standardised format. [Note: article 24(5) of MiFID and last paragraph of article 29(1) of IDD]

### Stewardship Code and Record-Keeping

**COBS 2.2A.5R** — Must comply with COBS 2.2.3R (Stewardship Code disclosure).

**COBS 2.2A.6G** — A firm to which SYSC 9 applies must keep records of all services, activities and transactions undertaken.

**COBS 2.2A.7R** — Must keep a record of the information provided to clients in compliance with this section applicable to MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business.

## COBS 2.2B — Shareholder Rights Directive (SRD) Requirements

### Application

**COBS 2.2B.1R** — Applies to:
1. A UK MiFID investment firm providing portfolio management services to investors
2. A third country investment firm providing portfolio management services to investors
3. A UK UCITS management company
4. An ICVC that is a UCITS scheme without a separate management company
5. A full-scope UK AIFM

**COBS 2.2B.2R** — To the extent the firm is investing (or has invested) on behalf of investors in shares traded on a regulated market.

### Engagement Policy — Comply or Explain

**COBS 2.2B.5R** — A firm must either:
1. (a) develop and publicly disclose an engagement policy meeting COBS 2.2B.6R; AND (b) publicly disclose annually how it has been implemented meeting COBS 2.2B.7R; or
2. Publicly disclose a clear and reasoned explanation of why it has chosen not to comply with any of these requirements.

[Note: article 3g(1) and (1)(a) of SRD]

**COBS 2.2B.6R** — The engagement policy must describe how the firm:
1. Integrates shareholder engagement in its investment strategy
2. Monitors investee companies on: strategy; financial and non-financial performance and risk; capital structure; social and environmental impact; corporate governance
3. Conducts dialogues with investee companies
4. Exercises voting rights and other rights attached to shares
5. Cooperates with other shareholders
6. Communicates with relevant stakeholders of investee companies
7. Manages actual and potential conflicts of interests in relation to engagement

**COBS 2.2B.7R** — Annual disclosure must include: general description of voting behaviour; explanation of most significant votes; reporting on use of proxy advisors; (2) how votes cast in GMs of companies in which the firm holds shares (except insignificant votes by subject matter or holding size).

**COBS 2.2B.8R** — Disclosures must be available free of charge on the firm's website.

### Transparency to SRD Institutional Investors

**COBS 2.2B.9R** — Where a firm invests on behalf of an SRD institutional investor (whether discretionary client-by-client or through a collective investment undertaking), the firm must disclose to that investor annually how its investment strategy and implementation:
1. Complies with the arrangement referred to in COBS 2.2B.1R(2)
2. Contributes to the medium- to long-term performance of the assets of the SRD institutional investor or of the fund

Disclosure must include: key material medium-to-long-term risks; portfolio composition; turnover and turnover costs; use of proxy advisors; securities lending policy and how it supports engagement activities; whether the firm makes investment decisions based on medium- to long-term performance of investee companies including non-financial performance; conflicts of interests in connection with engagement.

## COBS 2.3 — Inducements (Non-MiFID Designated Investment Business)

### Scope

COBS 2.3 applies to designated investment business (other than MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business and IBIPs, and excluding advice/services in connection with qualifying schemes). For MiFID business, the operative rule is COBS 2.3A.

### The Rule

**COBS 2.3.1R** — A firm must not pay or accept any fee or commission, or provide or receive any non-monetary benefit, in relation to designated investment business carried on for a client other than:

**(1)** A fee, commission or NMB paid/provided to or by the client or a person on behalf of the client

**(2)** A fee, commission or NMB paid/provided to or by a third party or person acting on behalf of a third party, if:
- **(a)** The payment does not impair compliance with the firm's duty to act in the best interests of the client; AND the existence, nature and amount (or calculation method) is clearly disclosed to the client in a comprehensive, accurate and understandable manner before the service is provided — this requirement applies (for personal recommendations on retail investment products, pension transfers/conversions/opt-outs, P2P agreements) where the service includes giving a personal recommendation, giving advice/services to an employer in connection with a group personal/stakeholder pension scheme, or providing targeted support; this requirement does not apply to basic advice

**(3)** Proper fees which enable or are necessary for the provision of designated investment business, such as custody costs, settlement and exchange fees, regulatory levies or legal fees, which by their nature cannot give rise to conflicts with the firm's duties to clients

**(4)** An employer or trustee funded pension advice charge

**COBS 2.3.1AR** — COBS 2.3.1R applies to a UK UCITS management company providing collective portfolio management services, as if references to a client were references to any UCITS it manages.

### Satisfying the Disclosure Requirement

**COBS 2.3.2R** — A firm satisfies COBS 2.3.1R(2)(a)(ii) disclosure obligation if it: (1) discloses the essential arrangements relating to the fee, commission or NMB in summary form; (2) undertakes to the client that further details will be disclosed on request; and (3) honours that undertaking.

### Reasonable Non-Monetary Benefits (COBS 2.3.15G Table)

COBS 2.3.15G provides a table of 14 categories of NMBs that are capable of enhancing the quality of service to a client and being paid or received without breaching the client's best interests rule (when conditions are satisfied). These include: gifts/hospitality of reasonable value; joint marketing literature; product literature; freepost envelopes; product-specific literature; draft articles/news items; seminar/conference participation; freephone links; system and data access tools; software; travel/accommodation (for specified purposes including market research and training); training; generic technical information; and clearing/settlement administration.

**COBS 2.3.16G** — Volume overrides and similar arrangements that make benefits available to one firm but not another are more likely to impair compliance. Benefits of substantial size (such as adviser training programmes) should be made available equally across firms that are subject to adviser charging rules if provided at all.

**COBS 2.3.16B R** — The guidance on reasonable NMBs (COBS 2.3.14G–2.3.16AG) does not apply to a firm which: (1) makes personal recommendations to retail clients on retail investment products, pension transfers, conversions, opt-outs or P2P agreements and to which COBS 6.1A applies; or (2) is a retail investment product provider, platform service provider, or operator of an electronic system in relation to lending to which COBS 6.1B applies.

### Record-Keeping

**COBS 2.3.17R** — A firm must:
1. Make and keep (for at least 5 years) a record of information disclosed to the client under COBS 2.3.1R(2)(b)
2. Make and keep (for at least 5 years) a record of each benefit given to another firm which does not have to be disclosed to the client under COBS 2.3.1R(2)(b)(ii)

## COBS 2.3A — Inducements (MiFID and Insurance Distribution)

COBS 2.3A is the primary operative inducements regime for MiFID investment services. It establishes a general prohibition with defined exceptions, and imposes a **hard prohibition** on inducements for independent advice, restricted advice and portfolio management.

**This section is covered in full in [[cobs2-3a-inducements]].** Key reference points:

- **General prohibition** — COBS 2.3A.5R: must not pay/accept fees, commissions, or NMBs from/to any party other than the client, in connection with investment services or IBIP distribution
- **Exceptions** — COBS 2.3A.6R: (a) quality-enhancing with client disclosure; (b) necessary for service provision; (c) third party research per COBS 2.3B
- **Hard prohibition for independent/restricted advice and portfolio management to retail clients** — COBS 2.3A.15R: must not accept and retain any fees, commissions, monetary or NMBs from third parties (exception: acceptable minor NMBs per COBS 2.3A.19R; third party research per COBS 2.3B)
- **Acceptable minor NMBs** — COBS 2.3A.19R: 5 conditions + 12 enumerated types
- **Ongoing inducements** — COBS 2.3A.12R: inform client at least annually of actual amounts received
- **Record-keeping** — COBS 2.3A.32R: internal list of all fees/commissions/NMBs received; evidence how they enhance quality

**Relationship to COBS 2.3:** A firm providing independent or restricted advice in the course of MiFID business must comply with COBS 2.3A (not COBS 2.3). For non-MiFID designated investment business, COBS 2.3 applies. Where a firm provides both, the appropriate section applies to each activity.

## COBS 2.3B — Inducements and Research (Research Payment Accounts)

COBS 2.3B governs how MiFID investment firms may receive third party research without it constituting a prohibited inducement. Applies to firms carrying on MiFID, equivalent third country or optional exemption business.

**This section is covered in full in [[cobs2-3b-inducements-research]].** Key reference points:

- **Three compliant routes** for receiving third party research without it being an inducement — COBS 2.3B.3R: (1) direct payments from firm's own resources; (2) payments from a research payment account (RPA); (3) joint payments for research and execution services
- **RPA conditions** — COBS 2.3B.4R: funded only by specific research charge to clients not linked to transaction volumes; firm sets and regularly assesses research budget; firm fully responsible; quality regularly assessed
- **RPA governance** — COBS 2.3B.11R: budget managed solely by firm; allocation subject to senior management oversight; clear audit trail
- **12 items NOT classed as research** — COBS 2.3B.23G: including post-trade analytics, price feeds/historical data, portfolio performance services, seminar fees, corporate access services for sub-£200m market cap companies
- **Joint payments** — COBS 2.3B.25R: written policy; methodology for identifying research costs separately from execution; research provider payment allocation structure; budgeting and fair allocation obligations
- **Research must not be an execution factor** — COBS 2.3B.29R

## COBS 2.3C — Research and Execution Services (Pricing Separation)

**COBS 2.3C.1R** — Applies to an investment firm providing execution services to: (1) firms carrying on MiFID or equivalent third country business; (2) investment firms authorised under UK MiFID provisions not within (1); (3) UCITS management companies; (4) full-scope UK AIFMs; (5) small authorised UK AIFMs; (6) residual CIS operators; (8) OPS firms.

**COBS 2.3C.2R** — A firm providing execution services must:
1. Identify separate charges for its execution services that only reflect the cost of executing the transaction
2. Subject each other benefit or service (other than an acceptable minor NMB in COBS 2.3A.19R) provided to listed entities to a separately identifiable charge
3. Ensure that the supply of, and charges for, other benefits or services are not influenced or conditioned by levels of payment for execution services

**COBS 2.3C.3R** — A firm providing both execution and research services must price and supply them separately.

**COBS 2.3C.4G** — Compliance with COBS 2.3C.2R enables firms subject to COBS 2.3A.15R and 2.3A.16R (hard prohibition) to comply with the prohibition on accepting and retaining third party fees, commissions or NMBs.

## COBS 2.4 — Agent as Client and Reliance on Others

### Agent as Client

**COBS 2.4.3R** — If a firm F is aware that a person C1 with or for whom it is providing services is acting as agent for another person C2 in relation to those services, **C1 and not C2 is the client of F** in respect of that business.

Exception — C2 is the client if:
- (a) F has agreed with C1 in writing to treat C2 as its client; or
- (b) C1 is neither a firm nor an overseas financial services institution AND the main purpose of the arrangements is the avoidance of duties that F would otherwise owe to C2

**COBS 2.4.3R(3)** — Where there is a written agreement under (2)(a) covering more than one C2 represented by C1, F may discharge notification/consent/agreement requirements by sending C1 a single communication covering all C2s — except that separate risk warnings, separate confirmations under COBS 16.2/16A.3, and separate periodic statements are required for each C2.

### Reliance on Other Firms (MiFID Business)

**COBS 2.4.4R** — If F1 (in the course of MiFID or equivalent business) receives an instruction to provide an investment or ancillary service on behalf of client C through another firm F2 (which is a MiFID investment firm, third country investment firm, or investment firm subject to equivalent requirements):
- F1 may rely on: (a) any information about C transmitted to it by F2; and (b) any recommendations (other than ready-made suggestions) in respect of the service or transaction provided to C by F2
- F2 remains responsible for: (a) completeness and accuracy of info about C transmitted to F1; and (b) suitability for C of advice/recommendations provided to C
- F1 remains responsible for concluding the services or transaction based on such information/recommendations in accordance with applicable requirements

**COBS 2.4.5G** — If F1 is required to perform a suitability assessment (COBS 9A) or appropriateness assessment (COBS 10A), it may rely on such assessment performed by F2 if F2 was subject to the relevant COBS requirements in performing it.

### General Reliance on Written Information

**COBS 2.4.6R** — A firm will be taken to be in compliance with any rule in COBS or CASS that requires it to obtain information, to the extent it can show it was reasonable to rely on information provided to it in writing by another person.

**COBS 2.4.8G** — It will generally be reasonable to rely on written information from an unconnected authorised person or professional firm, unless the firm is aware or ought reasonably to be aware of any fact that would give reasonable grounds to question accuracy.

### Routing of Client Information

**COBS 2.4.9R** — Any information that a rule in COBS or CASS requires to be sent to a client may be sent to another person on the instruction of the client, so long as the recipient is not connected to the firm.

**COBS 2.4.10R** — For non-MiFID business, a firm need not send required information to a client so long as it takes reasonable steps to establish that the information has been or will be supplied by another person.

## COBS 2.5 — Optional Additional Products

### The Core Prohibition

**COBS 2.5.1R** — A firm must not enter into an agreement with a client under which a charge is, or may become, payable for an optional additional product unless the client has **actively elected** to obtain that specific product. **(COBS 2.5.1R(1))**

A firm must not impose a charge on a client for an optional additional product under an agreement entered into on or after 1 April 2016 unless the client actively elected to obtain that specific product before becoming bound to pay the charge. **(COBS 2.5.1R(2))**

A firm must not invite or induce a client to obtain an optional additional product for which a charge will or may be payable if the firm knows or has reasonable cause to suspect that:
- (a) a contravention of (1) or (2) will take place; or
- (b) the person supplying the optional additional product will act in a way that would contravene (1) or (2) if that person were a firm **(COBS 2.5.1R(3))**

### What Counts as "Active Election"

**COBS 2.5.2G** — An omission by a client is not an active election. Example: failure to change a default option such as a pre-ticked box on a website.

**COBS 2.5.1R(11)** — A client may make an active election through an intermediary in the sales process or through a person acting on behalf of the firm.

**COBS 2.5.1R(10)** — For automatic renewals on substantially the same terms, it suffices that the client actively elected before entering the initial agreement or a preceding renewal.

### Definition: Optional Additional Product

**COBS 2.5.1R(7)** — An optional additional product is a good, service or right of any description, whether or not financial in nature, that a client may obtain (or not, as the case may be) at their election **in connection with, or alongside, a designated investment.**

An additional product that is required as a condition of the transaction related to the designated investment is still an optional additional product if the client is given a choice: (a) as to the seller or supplier; or (b) as to which specific product to obtain. **(COBS 2.5.1R(8))**

**COBS 2.5.4G** — Firms must ensure that their appointed representatives comply with COBS 2.5.

## Key Points for Agents

- **Client's best interests rule (COBS 2.1.1R)** — the foundational conduct standard for all investment business. Applies to designated investment business for retail clients; applies to MiFID/equivalent business for any client; applies to insurance distribution for any client. Cannot be excluded or restricted by contract.
- **Pre-service disclosure (COBS 2.2.1R / 2.2A.2R)** — before providing services, a firm must give appropriate information about itself, the financial instrument/strategy, execution venues, and costs and charges, in a comprehensible form — sufficient for the client to understand nature and risks and take informed decisions. MiFID version requires "in good time."
- **Non-MiFID inducements (COBS 2.3.1R)** — for non-MiFID designated investment business, inducements are permitted if they (a) come from the client; or (b) from a third party IF they enhance quality AND do not impair best interests AND are clearly disclosed. Record-keeping for 5 years.
- **MiFID inducements** — governed by COBS 2.3A (covered separately in [[cobs2-3a-inducements]]). Hard prohibition applies to independent/restricted advice and portfolio management to retail clients.
- **Research payment accounts** — governed by COBS 2.3B (covered separately in [[cobs2-3b-inducements-research]]). Third party research must be paid for from firm's own resources, an RPA, or joint payments — not from execution commissions.
- **Execution/research price separation (COBS 2.3C.3R)** — a firm providing both execution and research must price and supply them separately. Non-compliance is likely to breach best execution and inducements obligations simultaneously.
- **Agent as client (COBS 2.4.3R)** — where a firm knows the counterparty is acting as agent, the agent (C1) is the client for regulatory purposes — unless there is a written agreement to treat the underlying principal (C2) as client, or the arrangements are structured to evade duties.
- **Optional additional products (COBS 2.5.1R)** — pre-ticking a box or making an add-on product a default is a breach. Active election by the client is required before any charge can be imposed for an optional product sold alongside a designated investment.
- **SRD shareholder engagement (COBS 2.2B)** — portfolio managers, UCITS managers, and AIFMs investing in listed shares must either publish an engagement policy and annual disclosure (including voting behaviour) or explain why they have chosen not to.
