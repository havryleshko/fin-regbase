# SYSC 8 — Outsourcing

## What SYSC 8 Does

SYSC 8 sets out the requirements a firm must meet when it relies on a third party to perform operational functions — in particular functions that are critical or important for the delivery of regulated activities. The chapter has two principal objectives: preventing outsourcing from creating undue operational risk, and ensuring that the FCA retains supervisory reach over functions performed outside the firm.

SYSC 8 does not prohibit outsourcing. It conditions it.

---

## Application

**Common platform firms** (MiFID investment firms and equivalent) are subject to the full rule set in SYSC 8.1 as set out in the table at **SYSC 8.1.-2G**:

| Subject | Applicable rules |
|---------|-----------------|
| General requirements | SYSC 8.1.1R; 8.1.2G; 8.1.3G |
| Scope of critical and important functions | SYSC 8.1.4AR; 8.1.5R |
| Outsourcing critical or important functions | SYSC 8.1.6-AR to 8.1.12G |

**Other firms** (non-common-platform) must take account of the general requirements rule **(SYSC 8.1.1R)** and the critical/important scope rules **(SYSC 8.1.4R; 8.1.5R)** as if they were guidance **(SYSC 8.1.1A G; 8.1.5A G)**.

**MiFID optional exemption firms and third-country firms** are treated as common platform firms for these purposes **(SYSC 8.1.-1G)**.

---

## Section 8.1.1R — General Requirements

A common platform firm must **(SYSC 8.1.1R)**:

1. When relying on a third party for the performance of operational functions **critical** for the performance of regulated, listed, or ancillary services on a continuous and satisfactory basis — take **reasonable steps to avoid undue additional operational risk**; and

2. Not outsource **important** operational functions in such a way as to **materially impair**:
   - **(a)** the quality of its internal control; or
   - **(b)** the FCA's ability to monitor the firm's compliance with all its obligations under the regulatory system.

**[Note: article 16(5) first paragraph of MiFID]**

The SYSC 4.1.1R obligation to have effective processes to manage risk applies in all cases, including where functions are outsourced to non-critical third parties — the level of scrutiny is scaled to the nature, scale, and complexity of the arrangement **(SYSC 8.1.3G)**.

---

## Section 8.1.4R / 8.1.4AR — What Is "Critical or Important"

**Non-common-platform firms (SYSC 8.1.4R):** An operational function is critical or important if a defect or failure in its performance would materially impair:
- The firm's continuing compliance with the conditions and obligations of its authorisation or other obligations under the regulatory system; or
- Its financial performance; or
- The soundness or continuity of its relevant services and activities.

**Common platform firms (SYSC 8.1.4AR):** Same test, but framed by reference to the UK law on markets in financial instruments (MiFID) and the firm's designated investment business.

---

## Section 8.1.5R — What Is NOT Critical or Important

For a common platform firm, the following are **not** critical or important regardless of how significant they feel internally **(SYSC 8.1.5R)**:

1. **Advisory services**, and other services which do not form part of the relevant services and activities — including **provision of legal advice**, training of personnel, billing, and security of premises and personnel;
2. **Purchase of standardised services** — including **market information services** and provision of **price feeds**;
3. **Recording and retention** of relevant telephone conversations or electronic communications subject to **SYSC 10A**.

This safe harbour matters: firms that classify these functions as critical impose unnecessary compliance obligations on themselves and their service providers.

---

## Section 8.1.6R / 8.1.6-AR — Full Responsibility Retained

When any firm (other than a common platform firm) outsources critical or important functions **(SYSC 8.1.6R)**, and when a common platform firm does so **(SYSC 8.1.6-AR)**, the firm **remains fully responsible** for discharging all its regulatory obligations. The outsourcing arrangement must not:

1. Result in the **delegation by senior management** (or senior personnel) of their responsibility;
2. **Alter the firm's obligations** towards its clients under the regulatory system;
3. **Undermine the conditions** the firm must comply with in order to remain authorised; or
4. Remove or modify **any conditions of authorisation**.

This is an absolute constraint — it cannot be contracted away and it does not reduce if the service provider is well-resourced or highly reputable.

---

## Section 8.1.7R — Due Skill, Care and Diligence

A common platform firm must exercise **due skill, care and diligence** when **entering into, managing, or terminating** any arrangement for the outsourcing of critical or important functions or relevant services and activities **(SYSC 8.1.7R)**. The obligation covers the full lifecycle — not just contract negotiation.

---

## Section 8.1.8R — Twelve Ongoing Conditions

A common platform firm must take necessary steps to ensure the following conditions are satisfied on an ongoing basis **(SYSC 8.1.8R)**:

| # | Condition |
|---|-----------|
| (1) | Service provider has **ability, capacity, appropriate organisational structure**, and any **authorisation required by law** to perform the outsourced functions reliably and professionally |
| (2) | Service provider carries out services effectively and in compliance with applicable law; firm must establish **methods for assessing performance** and **reviewing** services on an **ongoing** basis |
| (3) | Service provider **properly supervises** the outsourced functions and **adequately manages** the associated risks |
| (4) | Firm takes **appropriate action** if it appears the service provider is not carrying out functions effectively or in compliance |
| (5) | Firm **effectively supervises** the outsourced functions and **retains the necessary expertise and resources** to supervise the outsourced functions effectively and manage the risks |
| (6) | Service provider **discloses** to the firm any development that may have a **material impact** on its ability to carry out the outsourced functions |
| (7) | Firm is able to **terminate the arrangement with immediate effect** when this is in the interest of its clients, without detriment to the continuity and quality of service |
| (8) | Service provider **cooperates with the FCA** and any other relevant competent authority |
| (9) | Firm, its auditors, the FCA and any relevant competent authority have **effective access to data** and to the **business premises** of the service provider for effective oversight purposes |
| (10) | Service provider **protects confidential information** relating to the firm and its clients |
| (11) | Firm and service provider establish, implement and maintain a **contingency plan for disaster recovery** and **periodic testing of backup facilities** |
| (12) | **Continuity maintained** in the event of termination — either by transferring functions to another provider or by the firm performing them itself |

**Condition (5) is the retain-expertise obligation.** A firm cannot outsource a critical function and simultaneously eliminate internal capacity to supervise it. The FCA treats hollowing out of internal expertise after outsourcing as a distinct failure mode.

**Condition (7) is the immediate termination right.** This must be an actual contractual right — not a right subject to commercial notice periods that would delay action in a client-interest emergency.

---

## Section 8.1.9R — Written Agreement

A common platform firm must ensure that the respective rights and obligations of the firm and the service provider are **clearly allocated and set out in a written agreement** **(SYSC 8.1.9R)**. The agreement must specifically ensure the firm keeps:
- Its **instruction and termination rights**;
- Its **rights of information**; and
- Its **right to inspections and access to books and premises**.

**Sub-outsourcing:** The agreement must ensure that outsourcing by the service provider to a further party **only takes place with the firm's written consent** **(SYSC 8.1.9R)**. Without this clause, the service provider may sub-delegate the function without the firm's knowledge, breaking the oversight chain.

---

## Section 8.1.10R — Group Relief

Where the firm and the service provider are members of the **same group**, the firm may, for the purpose of complying with SYSC 8.1.6-AR, 8.1.7R to 8.1.10R, 8.1.11-AR, 8.1.11-BR and 8.1.11-CG, take into account the **extent to which the firm controls the service provider** or has the ability to influence its actions **(SYSC 8.1.10R)**. This permits a lighter touch within a group structure — but the underlying obligations are not disapplied; they are assessed in light of group control.

---

## Section 8.1.11R / 8.1.11-AR — FCA Information on Request

A firm (other than a common platform firm) must make available on request to the FCA all information necessary to enable the FCA to supervise compliance of the outsourced activities with the regulatory system **(SYSC 8.1.11R)**.

A common platform firm must make available on request to the FCA all information necessary to enable the FCA to supervise compliance with the requirements of the UK law on markets in financial instruments **(SYSC 8.1.11-AR)**.

---

## Section 8.1.11-BR — Third-Country Portfolio Management

Where a **common platform firm** outsources functions related to **portfolio management** provided to clients to a service provider **located in a third country**, the firm must ensure two additional conditions are satisfied **(SYSC 8.1.11-BR)**:

1. The service provider is **authorised or registered** in its home jurisdiction to provide that service and is **effectively supervised** by a regulatory body in that third country; **and**

2. There is an **appropriate cooperation agreement** between the FCA and the regulatory body of the service provider.

Both conditions must be met. The second condition is a hard gate — it cannot be satisfied by contractual workaround. The FCA publishes a list of its cooperation agreements with regulatory bodies in third countries **(SYSC 8.1.11-CG)**.

---

## Section 8.1.12G — FCA Notification

As **SUP 15.3.8G** explains, a firm should **notify the FCA** when it intends to rely on a third party for the performance of operational functions which are critical or important for the performance of relevant services and activities on a continuous and satisfactory basis **(SYSC 8.1.12G)**. This notification obligation is separate from the ongoing information access obligation in SYSC 8.1.11R/11-AR.

---

## Management Companies

A **management company** must retain the necessary resources and expertise to **monitor effectively** the activities carried out by third parties, especially the risks associated with those arrangements **(SYSC 8.1.13R; [Note: article 5(2) of the UCITS Implementing Directive])**.

UCITS management companies should also be aware that **SUP 15.8.6R** (delegation by UCITS management companies) and **COLL 6.6.15AR** (committees and delegations) contain requirements implementing article 13 of the UCITS Directive in relation to delegation **(SYSC 8.1.14G)**.

---

## Key Points for Agents

- **Full responsibility is retained — always (SYSC 8.1.6R; 8.1.6-AR).** Outsourcing a critical or important function does not transfer any regulatory obligation to the service provider. The FCA holds the firm accountable for the outsourced function as if it were performed in-house. This is not guidance — it is a hard Rule.
- **The safe harbour list matters (SYSC 8.1.5R).** Legal advice, training, billing, premises security, standardised market data services, and price feeds are not critical or important by definition. Firms that over-classify these functions create unnecessary compliance obligations. The negative test is as important as the positive definition.
- **The firm must retain expertise to supervise (SYSC 8.1.8R(5)).** After outsourcing, the firm cannot eliminate the internal capability to oversee what the service provider is doing. This is the most frequently failed SYSC 8 condition in FCA supervisory reviews. The obligation to retain expertise is permanent and does not diminish as the outsourcing relationship matures.
- **Sub-outsourcing requires the firm's written consent — this must be in the contract (SYSC 8.1.9R).** Without an explicit contractual prohibition on further delegation without consent, the service provider can sub-outsource the function to a fourth party without the firm's knowledge, and the firm loses practical oversight.
- **Third-country portfolio management outsourcing requires a cooperation agreement between FCA and the provider's regulator (SYSC 8.1.11-BR).** This is a hard gate — it cannot be satisfied by due diligence on the provider or contractual protections. If the FCA has no cooperation agreement with the regulator in the provider's jurisdiction, the outsourcing is non-compliant regardless of its commercial merits.
- **Notify the FCA before relying on a third party for critical or important functions (SYSC 8.1.12G; SUP 15.3.8G).** This is guidance, not a hard Rule, but the FCA's expectation is clear. Most fintechs treat outsourcing as a purely contractual matter and are unaware of this notification expectation.
