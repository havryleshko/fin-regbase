# CFPB Supervision and Examination Manual (March 2022)

## Document Overview

The CFPB Supervision and Examination Manual is the primary operational guide for CFPB examination staff. It sets out the examination process, rating systems, compliance management review modules, and product-by-product examination procedures for every category of consumer financial product the CFPB supervises. It is also the key reference document for supervised entities seeking to understand what the CFPB will look for in an examination.

The manual is structured as a series of independently dated modules, updated at different times. The March 2022 date reflects the most recent module update at time of ingest; individual product sections carry their own dates (e.g., Auto Finance: August 2019; Remittance Transfer: February 2022).

## Statutory Foundation

The CFPB was created by Title X of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (2010), effective 21 July 2011. Its statutory purpose is to ensure consumer financial markets are fair, transparent, and competitive. **(Dodd-Frank §1021; 12 USC 5511)**

Supervisory jurisdiction covers three categories of entity:

| Category | Statutory basis | Scope |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Non-depository covered persons | Dodd-Frank §1024 | Entities offering consumer financial products/services not subject to prudential regulation — includes fintechs, payday lenders, mortgage servicers, debt collectors |
| Large depository institutions | Dodd-Frank §1025 | Federally insured banks, thrifts, credit unions with assets >$10bn |
| Service providers | Dodd-Frank §1026 | Any service provider to a covered person, regardless of the service provider's own regulatory status |

## Two-Layer Compliance Structure

The CFPB applies a two-layer compliance framework to every supervised entity:

**Layer 1 — UDAAP (catch-all standard):** Section 1036 of Dodd-Frank (12 USC 5536) prohibits any covered person or service provider from committing unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices (UDAAPs) in connection with any consumer financial product or service. This is the broadest compliance obligation and applies to conduct not reached by any enumerated law.

**Layer 2 — Enumerated consumer laws:** Specific federal statutes administered or enforced by the CFPB, each implemented through a Regulation. The key statutes include:

| Statute | Regulation | Subject matter |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| Truth in Lending Act (TILA) | Reg Z (12 CFR 1026) | Credit cost disclosure, APR, right of rescission |
| Consumer Leasing Act (CLA) | Reg M (12 CFR 1013) | Consumer lease disclosures |
| Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) | Reg E (12 CFR 1005) | EFTs, debit card overdraft opt-in, prepaid accounts, remittances |
| Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) | Reg B (12 CFR 1002) | Credit discrimination prohibition |
| Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) | Reg V (12 CFR 1022) | Consumer reports, adverse action, risk-based pricing |
| Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) | — | Debt collection conduct |
| Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) | Reg P (12 CFR 1016) | Consumer financial privacy |
| Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) | Reg C (12 CFR 1003) | Mortgage lending data |
| Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) | Reg X (12 CFR 1024) | Mortgage settlement services |

## Supervision Principles

The CFPB applies three core principles across all examinations:

1. **Consumer risk focus** — Examinations prioritise risks to consumers, not institutional safety and soundness. The CFPB may collect information about consumer risks even where no specific legal standard has been breached, including potential UDAAPs.
2. **Data-driven** — Examination scope and scheduling are driven by consumer complaint data, market intelligence, and supervisory history.
3. **Consistency** — The same compliance standards apply to banks and non-banks offering equivalent products. A fintech offering a product that competes with a bank product faces the same CFPB requirements.

## Examination Process

### Scheduling and Risk Assessment

Examinations are not periodic. The CFPB schedules on a risk-assessed basis, taking into account: the volume and nature of consumer complaints, prior examination findings, market size and market share, and product risk to consumers. Targeted reviews (narrower scope) are used between full-scope examinations.

### CC Rating System

The CFPB uses the FFIEC Uniform Consumer Compliance Rating System (CC Rating System). Ratings run from 1 (strongest) to 5 (most serious deficiencies):

| Rating | Status | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Satisfactory | Strong performance, minor correctable weaknesses |
| 2 | Satisfactory | Adequate compliance, modest correctable weaknesses |
| 3 | Less than satisfactory | Developing weakness or inadequate systems; management needs to take action |
| 4 | Less than satisfactory | Serious compliance deficiencies requiring immediate corrective attention |
| 5 | Less than satisfactory | Critical failures pervasive throughout the institution |

Ratings are assessed across three categories:
- **Board and Management Oversight**
- **Compliance Program**
- **Violations of Law and Consumer Harm**

### Examination Outputs

- **Matters Requiring Attention (MRAs):** Directed to the board; legally significant; carry timeframes for correction. Failure to address MRAs escalates to enforcement.
- **Supervisory Recommendations (SRs):** Address CMS weaknesses not amounting to a violation; not legally enforceable but tracked across examinations.

## Compliance Management Review (CMR)

The CMR is the CFPB's standardised framework for assessing an entity's compliance management system. It applies to all supervised entities and all products. The CMR is structured as five modules:

**Module 1 — Board and Management Oversight:** Board engagement with compliance; senior management accountability; compliance function independence; change management.

**Module 2 — Compliance Program:** Four components assessed together — (i) policies and procedures; (ii) training; (iii) monitoring and audit; (iv) consumer complaint response.

**Module 3 — Service Provider Oversight:** Policies, due diligence, monitoring, and contractual protections for third-party service provider relationships.

**Module 4 — Violations of Law and Consumer Harm:** Assessed using four factors for each violation found — Root Cause, Severity, Duration, Pervasiveness.

**Module 5 — Examiner Conclusions and Wrap-Up:** Synthesis across Modules 1–4; aggregate CC Rating assignment; MRA and SR determination.

A parallel **CMR-IT** module applies the same five-module structure to technology and information systems compliance.

## UDAAP Standards

The CFPB applies the following standards when assessing UDAAPs across all product lines:

**Deceptive** (a representation, omission, act, or practice is deceptive when):
- It misleads or is likely to mislead the consumer;
- The consumer's interpretation is reasonable under the circumstances; and
- The misleading element is material.

**Unfair** (an act or practice is unfair when):
- It causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers;
- The injury is not reasonably avoidable by consumers; and
- The injury is not outweighed by countervailing benefits to consumers or to competition.

**Abusive** (an act or practice is abusive when it):
- Materially interferes with the ability of a consumer to understand a term or condition of a consumer financial product or service; or
- Takes unreasonable advantage of — (i) a lack of consumer understanding of the material risks, costs, or conditions; (ii) the consumer's inability to protect their interests in selecting or using the product; or (iii) the consumer's reasonable reliance on a covered person to act in the consumer's interests.

**(Dodd-Frank §1036; 12 USC 5536)**

## Service Provider Rule

Outsourcing to a service provider does not reduce or eliminate the covered person's compliance obligations. The covered person remains fully responsible for the service provider's compliance with Federal consumer financial law. The CFPB examines service provider oversight as a discrete CMR module and may take action against the covered person for service provider conduct.

## Enforcement Authority

The CFPB has civil enforcement authority only (no criminal authority). Enforcement actions may seek:
- Rescission or reformation of contracts
- Restitution and disgorgement
- Civil monetary penalties
- Public notification of violations
- Limitations on the entity's activities, functions, or operations

## Key Points for Agents

- **The CFPB supervises both banks and non-banks** on a level playing field. A fintech offering a consumer financial product faces the same examination standards as a bank offering the same product. **(Dodd-Frank §1025–§1026)**
- **UDAAP is the catch-all.** Every examination includes UDAAP assessment regardless of whether enumerated laws are breached. The CFPB may collect information about potential UDAAPs even where no specific legal standard has been violated. **(Dodd-Frank §1036; 12 USC 5536)**
- **The CMS is the primary examination lens.** The CFPB evaluates compliance through the five CMR modules before assessing specific violations. A weak CMS is itself an adverse finding even if no violations are found.
- **Service provider outsourcing does not transfer compliance responsibility.** The covered person must exercise ongoing due diligence and monitoring over service providers. **(Dodd-Frank §1026)**
- **Violation severity is assessed on four factors:** Root Cause, Severity, Duration, Pervasiveness. The combination of these factors determines whether a violation results in an MRA, referral for enforcement, or a lesser finding.
- **MRAs are board-directed and legally tracked.** Failure to remediate MRAs within agreed timeframes is treated as an escalation trigger for enforcement referral.
- **CC Ratings 3–5 are publicly significant** — they trigger supervisory escalation and, for depository institutions, can affect regulatory approvals. Ratings 1–2 are satisfactory; 3–5 are less than satisfactory.
