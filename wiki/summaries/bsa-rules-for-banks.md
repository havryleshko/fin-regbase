# BSA — Rules for Banks (31 CFR Part 1020)

## Overview

31 CFR Part 1020 contains the BSA rules specific to banks. It layers on top of the general provisions in Part 1010, which apply to all financial institutions. Where Part 1020 cross-references Part 1010, the Part 1010 provision governs without modification. Where Part 1020 establishes its own rules (primarily in Subparts B, C, and D), those bank-specific rules apply.

**Who is covered:** Banks — including commercial banks, savings associations, credit unions, and (for certain provisions) private banks, non-federally insured credit unions, and certain trust companies. **(§1020.210)**

**Structure:**

## Subpart A — Definitions (§1020.100)

For the purposes of §1020.220 (CIP):

**Account** means a formal banking relationship established to provide or engage in services, dealings, or other financial transactions, including a deposit account, transaction or asset account, credit account, or other extension of credit, and including safety deposit boxes, cash management, custodian, and trust services. Account does **not** include: check-cashing, wire transfer, or sale of a check or money order where no formal banking relationship is established; accounts acquired through mergers or acquisitions; or ERISA employee benefit plan accounts. **(§1020.100(a))**

**Customer** means a person that opens a new account, or an individual who opens an account for a minor or a non-legal entity (such as a civic club). Customer does **not** include: a financial institution regulated by a Federal functional regulator or a State bank regulator; a person described in the CTR exemptions (§1020.315(b)(2)–(b)(4)); or a person with an existing account where the bank has a reasonable belief it knows the true identity of the person. **(§1020.100(b))**

## Subpart B — Programs

### AML Programme — §1020.210

A bank regulated by a Federal functional regulator satisfies 31 USC 5318(h)(1) if it implements and maintains an AML programme that:

1. Complies with §§1010.610 and 1010.620 (correspondent account DD and private banking DD)
2. Includes, at a minimum:
   - **(i)** A system of **internal controls** to assure ongoing compliance
   - **(ii)** Independent **testing** for compliance by bank personnel or an outside party
   - **(iii)** Designation of an **individual or individuals** responsible for coordinating and monitoring day-to-day compliance
   - **(iv)** **Training** for appropriate personnel
   - **(v)** Appropriate **risk-based CDD procedures**, including:
     - **(A)** Understanding the nature and purpose of customer relationships to develop a **customer risk profile**
     - **(B)** Conducting **ongoing monitoring** to identify and report suspicious transactions and, on a risk basis, to maintain and update customer information — including BO info of legal entity customers as defined in §1010.230
3. Complies with the regulation of its Federal functional regulator governing such programmes **(§1020.210(a))**

Banks **without** a Federal functional regulator (e.g., private banks, non-federally insured credit unions, certain trust companies) must also obtain **board of directors approval** (or equivalent governing body approval) and make a copy of the programme available to FinCEN on request. **(§1020.210(b))**

**Note:** Element (v) — risk-based CDD — is the explicit regulatory basis for the CDD "fifth pillar" (ongoing monitoring + customer risk profiling) that FinCEN added to the AML programme requirement. It is distinct from but works in concert with the CDD rule in §1010.230.

### Customer Identification Programme — §1020.220

See full detail at: [[bsa-cip-customer-identification-programme]]

**Core requirements:**
- 4 required identification elements: **name; date of birth** (individuals only); **address**; **identification number** (TIN for US persons; for non-US persons: TIN, passport + country, alien ID card, or any government-issued document with photo evidencing nationality or residence)
- Risk-based verification: documentary and/or non-documentary
- Government list comparison required
- Customer notice required
- 5-year retention from account closure

## Subpart C — Reports

### CTR (§§1020.310–1020.314)

Cross-reference only — the CTR filing obligation, identification requirements, aggregation rules, and structuring prohibition for banks are governed by §§1010.311–1010.314 of Part 1010. **(§§1020.310–1020.314)**

### CTR Exemptions — §1020.315

Banks may exempt certain customers from CTR filing. See full detail at: [[bsa-ctr-exemptions]]

**Seven exempt person categories:**
| Category | Designation required? | Annual review? |
|----------|-----------------------|----------------|
| (b)(1) Other banks | No | No |
| (b)(2) US/state/local/tribal government | No | No |
| (b)(3) Government-authority entities | No | No |
| (b)(4) NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ listed companies | Yes — FinCEN Form 110 | Yes |
| (b)(5) ≥51% subsidiaries of listed companies | Yes — FinCEN Form 110 | Yes |
| (b)(6) Non-listed businesses | Yes — FinCEN Form 110 | Yes |
| (b)(7) Payroll customers | Yes — FinCEN Form 110 | Yes |

CTR exemption does **not** eliminate the SAR filing obligation or the monitoring system requirement.

### Bank SAR — §1020.320

See full detail at: [[bsa-bank-sar]]

**Key figures:**
- Threshold: ≥**$5,000** in funds or other assets
- Filing deadline: **30 calendar days** from initial detection; **60 days** if no suspect identified at detection
- Confidentiality: absolute — bank must refuse to produce SAR even if subpoenaed; must notify FinCEN of any subpoena
- Retention: **5 years** from SAR filing date

## Subpart D — Records

### Funds Transfer Travel Rule — §1020.410(a)

Every US office of a bank is subject to the travel rule for funds transfers of **$3,000 or more**.

**As originator's bank:** Record and retain the payment order including: originator name and address; amount; execution date; payment instructions received; identity of beneficiary's bank; and beneficiary name, address, account number, and other specific identifiers (to the extent received). **(§1020.410(a)(1)(i))**

**As intermediary bank:** Retain either the original or a copy of the payment order received and the order transmitted. **(§1020.410(a)(1)(ii))**

**As beneficiary's bank:** Retain the original or copy of the payment order. Where proceeds are delivered to the beneficiary (or their representative) in person, verify the beneficiary's identity if not an established customer. **(§1020.410(a)(1)(iii))**

**Non-established customer verification:** At origination: verify identity by examination of a document (not a bank signature card) — preferably one with name, address, and photo. For beneficiaries receiving proceeds in person: same verification requirement. **(§1020.410(a)(2)–(3))**

**Retrievability:** Originator's bank — by originator name; if established customer with account used for transfers, also by account number. Beneficiary's bank — by beneficiary name; if established customer with account used for transfers, also by account number. **(§1020.410(a)(4))**

**Exceptions — transfers not subject to the travel rule:** Both originator and beneficiary are any of: a bank; wholly owned domestic bank subsidiary; broker-dealer; wholly owned domestic broker-dealer subsidiary; futures commission merchant or introducing broker; their wholly owned domestic subsidiaries; the US; a state or local government; a federal, state, or local government agency; or a mutual fund. Also exempt: transfers where originator and beneficiary are the same person at the same bank. **(§1020.410(a)(6))**

### Other Bank Records — §1020.410(b)–(c)

Banks must also retain:
- **Certificates of deposit** sold/redeemed after May 31, 1978: taxpayer ID number within 30 days (with limited exceptions for non-resident aliens, diplomats, student/training-program aliens, school savings accounts <$10/year)
- **Signature authority documents** for each deposit or share account
- **Account statements, ledger cards, or records** for each deposit or share account
- **Checks** drawn on the bank (with exceptions for high-volume check accounts)
- **Items >$100** debited to customer accounts (not bank charges or periodic charges)
- **Items >$10,000 remitted or transferred outside the US**
- **Foreign bank checks >$10,000** paid to a non-bank drawee
- **Items >$10,000 received directly from foreign banks, brokers, or dealers in foreign exchange**
- **Receipts >$10,000 on one occasion from foreign exchange** (not through a domestic FI)
- **Deposit slips and credit tickets >$100** (recording any currency amount) **(§1020.410(c))**

## Key Points for Agents

- **The bank AML programme has five required elements — the fifth (risk-based CDD) is regulatory text, not guidance.** §1020.210(a)(2)(v) requires risk-based procedures for understanding customer relationships (building a customer risk profile) and for ongoing monitoring to maintain and update customer information including BO data. This is the explicit regulatory basis for what practitioners call the "fifth pillar" of AML compliance. **(§1020.210)**
- **CIP applies to every new account opening, with 4 mandatory data elements and a verification obligation that cannot be waived.** The verification must enable a reasonable belief that the bank knows the true identity of the customer. Failure-to-verify procedures must specify when not to open, conditions for limited use, when to close, and when to file a SAR. **(§1020.220)**
- **CTR exemption designation for non-listed businesses and payroll customers requires a documented risk-based assessment, annual review, and a monitoring system — but does not eliminate SAR obligations.** The exemption is narrowed by a detailed ineligible business list that excludes financial institution agents, gaming, real estate brokerage, pawn brokerage, investment advisory services, and others. **(§1020.315)**
- **Bank SARs have a hard 60-day outer limit from initial detection regardless of whether a suspect is identified.** The 30-day standard applies when a suspect is identified at detection; the extra 30 days (60 total) applies only when no suspect is identified on the date of detection. Ongoing schemes trigger an additional immediate telephone notification to law enforcement. **(§1020.320)**
- **The bank travel rule at §1020.410 has a broader regulated-entity exemption than the non-bank rule at §1010.410.** Banks are exempt from travel rule recordkeeping for transfers where both originator and beneficiary are banks, broker-dealers, FCMs, government entities, or mutual funds. The non-bank travel rule (§1010.410) has a narrower inter-bank exemption. **(§1020.410(a)(6))**
