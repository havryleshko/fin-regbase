# 12 CFR Part 1016 — Regulation P (Privacy of Consumer Financial Information)

## Overview

Regulation P implements the privacy provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLB Act, 15 USC 6804(a)(1)(A)) as administered by the CFPB for financial institutions within its rulemaking jurisdiction. The regulation was transferred to the CFPB on the designated transfer date (December 21, 2011) from predecessor agencies and last amended by CFPB-2016-0032 (83 FR 40958, August 17, 2018).

The regulation governs the treatment of **nonpublic personal information (NPI)** about consumers by financial institutions. It:
1. Requires financial institutions to provide notice to customers about privacy policies and practices **(§1016.1(a)(1))**
2. Describes the conditions under which NPI may be disclosed to nonaffiliated third parties **(§1016.1(a)(2))**
3. Provides a method for consumers to prevent disclosure by "opting out," subject to express exceptions **(§1016.1(a)(3))**

---

## Scope and Coverage (§1016.1(b))

Regulation P applies to nonpublic personal information about **individuals who obtain financial products or services primarily for personal, family, or household purposes**. It does not apply to information about companies or individuals obtaining financial products or services for business, commercial, or agricultural purposes.

**Covered financial institutions** include any institution for which the CFPB has rulemaking authority under GLB Act § 504(a)(1)(A). Key exclusions from "you" under this part **(§1016.3(s)(2)):**
- Motor vehicle dealers described in 12 USC 5519(a)
- Entities subject to SEC rulemaking authority (broker-dealers, registered investment advisers, registered investment companies)
- Entities subject to CFTC rulemaking authority

**FERPA interaction:** An institution of higher education that is also a financial institution under this part and that complies with FERPA is deemed to comply with Regulation P. **(§1016.1(b)(2)(ii))**

## Key Definitions (§1016.3)

### Consumer vs. Customer — the Critical Distinction

| Term | Definition | Notice obligation |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Consumer** | Individual who obtains or has obtained a financial product or service for personal/family/household purposes | Notice required only before NPI is disclosed to a nonaffiliated third party (unless an exception applies) |
| **Customer** | Consumer who has a **continuing relationship** with the institution | Initial notice at or before the relationship is established; annual notice during the relationship |

A "continuing relationship" exists when the consumer has a deposit or investment account, a loan, insurance product, investment product in a custodial capacity, home mortgage brokerage agreement, personal property lease, or obtains financial advisory services for a fee. A consumer does **not** have a continuing relationship when transactions are isolated (e.g., single ATM withdrawal at another institution's ATM, purchase of a cashier's check, isolated airline ticket purchase). **(§1016.3(j))**

### Nonpublic Personal Information (NPI) (§1016.3(p))

NPI means:
- **Personally identifiable financial information** — any information a consumer provides to obtain a financial product or service, resulting from a transaction, or otherwise obtained in connection with providing a financial product or service; and
- **Any list or grouping** of consumers derived from personally identifiable financial information that is not publicly available.

NPI does **not** include publicly available information (government records, widely distributed media, disclosures required by law) or consumer lists/groupings not derived from non-publicly-available personally identifiable financial information. **(§1016.3(p)(2), (r))**

**Personally identifiable financial information** includes: application information; account balances, payment history, overdraft history; the fact that an individual is or has been your customer; information about a consumer disclosed in a manner indicating they are your customer; loan/credit account servicing information; internet cookie data; and consumer report information. **(§1016.3(q))**

### Nonaffiliated Third Party (§1016.3(o))

Any person except: (i) your affiliate; or (ii) a person jointly employed by you and a company that is not your affiliate (although that company is a nonaffiliated third party).

## Subpart A — Privacy and Opt Out Notices

### Initial Privacy Notice (§1016.4)

**Customers** must receive a clear and conspicuous initial privacy notice:
- At or before the time the customer relationship is established; or
- Within a reasonable time if establishing the relationship is not at the customer's election (e.g., loan servicing rights acquired from another institution) or if providing the notice would substantially delay the customer's transaction **(§1016.4(e))**

**Consumers who are not customers** must receive an initial notice before NPI about them is disclosed to any nonaffiliated third party — unless only §§1016.14 or 1016.15 exceptions apply. **(§1016.4(a)(2))**

**Existing customers** who obtain a new product or service: the prior notice satisfies the initial notice obligation if it remains accurate with respect to the new product/service. **(§1016.4(d))**

### Annual Privacy Notice (§1016.5)

Customers must receive a clear and conspicuous annual notice **at least once in any 12-consecutive-month period** during the continuation of the customer relationship. The 12-month period may be defined by the institution on a consistent basis. **(§1016.5(a))**

**Annual notice exception (§1016.5(e)) — important safe harbour:** An institution need not deliver an annual notice if:
1. It shares NPI only under §§1016.13 (service provider/joint marketing), 1016.14 (transaction processing), or 1016.15 (other exceptions); **AND**
2. It has not changed its policies and practices with respect to disclosing NPI from what was disclosed in the most recent privacy notice.

If the institution subsequently changes its practices to fall outside the exception, it must deliver a notice. If a revised notice is not required under §1016.8, the institution must deliver the annual notice within 100 days of the change. **(§1016.5(e)(2))**

### Required Contents of Privacy Notices (§1016.6)

Every initial, annual, and revised notice must include, as applicable:
1. Categories of NPI collected
2. Categories of NPI disclosed
3. Categories of affiliates and nonaffiliated third parties to whom NPI is disclosed
4. Categories of NPI disclosed about former customers
5. If disclosing under §1016.13 (service provider/joint marketing): a separate statement of the categories of NPI and the categories of companies contracted with
6. Explanation of the consumer's opt-out right under §1016.10(a) and opt-out methods
7. Any disclosures required under FCRA section 603(d)(2)(A)(iii) (affiliate marketing opt-out)
8. Policies and practices for protecting confidentiality and security of NPI
9. Any additional disclosure the institution wishes to make **(§1016.6(a))**

**Simplified notice:** Institutions that do not share NPI (other than under §§1016.14/1016.15) may simply state that fact without listing categories. **(§1016.6(c)(5))**

**Model privacy form (Appendix to Part 1016):** Use of the model form in the Appendix constitutes compliance with §§1016.6 and 1016.7 notice content requirements. The model form is not required, but institutions using it gain regulatory safe harbour. **(§1016.2(a))**

### Opt Out Notice (§1016.7)

Where an opt-out right exists under §1016.10, the institution must provide a clear and conspicuous opt-out notice that accurately explains the right. The notice must state: (i) that NPI is disclosed or will be disclosed to a nonaffiliated third party; (ii) that the consumer has the right to opt out; and (iii) a reasonable means to opt out. **(§1016.7(a))**

**Reasonable opt-out means** include: designated checkboxes; reply forms with a mailing address; electronic opt-out (e-mail, website form); toll-free telephone number. **Unreasonable means:** requiring the consumer to write their own letter; providing only a checkbox on the initial notice but not on subsequent opt-out notices. **(§1016.7(a)(2)(ii)–(iii))**

**Joint accounts:** If two or more consumers jointly obtain a product, the institution may provide a single opt-out notice. One joint consumer may opt out on behalf of the other, but the institution cannot require all joint consumers to opt out before implementing any opt-out direction. **(§1016.7(d)–(f))**

### Revised Privacy Notices (§1016.8)

Before disclosing NPI to a new category of nonaffiliated third party, or disclosing a new category of NPI to nonaffiliated third parties, or disclosing NPI about former customers in a new way, the institution must provide:
1. A revised notice accurately describing the new practices
2. A new opt-out notice
3. A reasonable opportunity to opt out before disclosure begins **(§1016.8(a))**

### Delivering Notices (§1016.9)

Notices must be provided so each consumer can reasonably be expected to receive **actual notice**, in writing or (if the consumer agrees) electronically. Posting a sign in a branch or generally publishing advertisements does not satisfy the requirement. For electronic delivery, the institution must post the notice on the electronic site and require acknowledgment as a necessary step to obtaining the financial product/service. **(§1016.9(a)–(b))**

## Subpart B — Limits on Disclosures

### Limits on Disclosure to Nonaffiliated Third Parties (§1016.10)

An institution may not, directly or through an affiliate, disclose NPI about a consumer to any nonaffiliated third party unless:
1. The institution has provided the consumer with an initial notice (§1016.4)
2. The institution has provided the consumer with an opt-out notice (§1016.7)
3. The consumer has been given a reasonable opportunity to opt out (at least 30 days to respond after mailing, 30 days after electronic acknowledgment)
4. The consumer has not opted out **(§1016.10(a))**

The opt-out prohibition applies to all NPI about the consumer, including NPI collected before the opt-out direction was received, regardless of when it was collected. **(§1016.10(b))**

**Partial opt-out:** Institutions may allow consumers to select specific categories of NPI or specific nonaffiliated third parties from which they wish to opt out. **(§1016.10(c))**

### Limits on Redisclosure and Reuse (§1016.11)

**Information received under an exception** (§§1016.14/1016.15): the third party receiving NPI under an exception may only disclose the information back to the institution's affiliates, to its own affiliates (to the extent the institution could), and to carry out the purpose for which the exception applied. The third party may not use the information for its own marketing purposes. **(§1016.11(a), (c))**

**Information received outside an exception:** The recipient may disclose the information only to the originating institution's affiliates, to its own affiliates (to the same extent), and to any other person if the disclosure would have been lawful if made directly by the originating institution. **(§1016.11(b))**

### Account Number Prohibition (§1016.12)

An institution must **not** disclose, directly or through an affiliate, an account number or similar access code to any nonaffiliated third party for use in telemarketing, direct mail marketing, or marketing through electronic mail. This prohibition admits only two narrow exceptions:
1. Disclosure to an agent or service provider solely to perform marketing **for the institution's own products or services**, provided the agent/service provider is not authorised to directly initiate charges to the account
2. Disclosure in connection with a private label credit card program or affinity/similar program where participants are identified to the customer at enrollment **(§1016.12(a)–(b))**

An account number in encrypted form does not fall within this prohibition as long as the recipient is not provided a means to decode it. **(§1016.12(c)(1))**

## Subpart C — Exceptions

### Service Provider and Joint Marketing Exception (§1016.13)

The opt-out requirement in §§1016.7 and 1016.10 **does not apply** when NPI is disclosed to a nonaffiliated third party that performs services for or on behalf of the institution, **provided:**
1. The institution has provided the initial notice (§1016.4); **AND**
2. The institution has a **written contract** with the third party prohibiting the third party from disclosing or using the information other than to carry out the purposes for which the information was disclosed, including use under an exception in §§1016.14/1016.15 in the ordinary course of business **(§1016.13(a))**

Services may include **joint marketing** of the institution's own products or services or products/services offered under joint agreements between the institution and one or more other financial institutions. A "joint agreement" is a written contract. **(§1016.13(b)–(c))**

### Transaction Processing and Servicing Exception (§1016.14)

The initial notice, opt-out notice, and service provider exception requirements **do not apply** when NPI is disclosed as necessary to effect, administer, or enforce a consumer-requested transaction, or in connection with:
1. Servicing or processing a financial product or service
2. Maintaining or servicing the consumer's account with a private label credit card program
3. A proposed or actual securitisation, secondary market sale, or similar transaction **(§1016.14(a))**

"Necessary to effect, administer, or enforce a transaction" includes: carrying out the transaction product or service; administering benefits or claims; providing confirmation or record; recognising incentives/bonuses; underwriting insurance; payment card transaction processing and clearing. **(§1016.14(b))**

### Other Exceptions (§1016.15)

The initial notice, opt-out, and service provider requirements also do not apply when NPI is disclosed:
1. With the consumer's consent or at the consumer's direction
2. To protect confidentiality/security, prevent fraud, for institutional risk control, to persons holding a legal/beneficial interest, to fiduciaries/representatives of the consumer
3. To insurance rate advisory organisations, guaranty funds, compliance assessors, attorneys, accountants, and auditors
4. In connection with law enforcement disclosures (RFPA 1978, federal banking examiners, state insurance examiners, FTC, self-regulatory organisations, public safety investigations)
5. To consumer reporting agencies (FCRA)
6. In connection with a proposed or actual sale, merger, transfer, or exchange of all or part of a business
7. To comply with federal, state, or local laws; judicial process; or government regulatory authority **(§1016.15(a))**

## Subpart D — Relation to Other Laws

### FCRA (§1016.16)

Nothing in Regulation P modifies, limits, or supersedes the FCRA. No inference should be drawn from Regulation P provisions regarding whether information is transaction or experience information under FCRA section 603. The affiliate marketing opt-out under FCRA section 624 operates independently of the Regulation P opt-out framework. **(§1016.16)**

### State Law (§1016.17)

Regulation P does not supersede state statutes, regulations, or interpretations **except to the extent they are inconsistent**, and then only to the extent of the inconsistency. A state law affording consumers **greater protection** than Regulation P is not inconsistent with Regulation P. State laws providing greater protection are preserved and may be enforced alongside the federal floor. **(§1016.17)**

## Opt-Out Duration and Mechanics

| Mechanic | Rule |
|----------|------|
| Duration | Permanent until consumer revokes in writing or, if agreed, electronically **(§1016.7(i)(1))** |
| Relationship termination | Opt-out direction survives; applies to NPI collected during or related to the relationship **(§1016.7(i)(2))** |
| New customer relationship | A new relationship with the same institution does **not** automatically reset the prior opt-out direction **(§1016.7(i)(2))** |
| Implementation | As soon as reasonably practicable after receipt **(§1016.7(g))** |
| Continuing right | Consumer may exercise the right to opt out at any time **(§1016.7(h))** |
| Revocation | Consumer revokes opt-out in writing or, if agreed, electronically |

## Key Points for Agents

- **The consumer/customer distinction drives notice obligations.** Customers with continuing relationships require initial + annual notices. One-time transactors (consumers) only require a notice before NPI is disclosed to a nonaffiliated third party (and only if an exception does not apply). **(§§1016.3(e), (i), (j); 1016.4–1016.5)**
- **The annual privacy notice exception eliminates the annual notice burden for many institutions.** If all sharing is under §§1016.13 (service provider/joint marketing), 1016.14 (transaction processing), or 1016.15 (other), AND policies have not changed, the annual notice is not required. **(§1016.5(e))**
- **Three disclosure tiers with different opt-out status:** (1) sharing with nonaffiliated third parties for marketing purposes — full notice + opt-out required; (2) service provider/joint marketing sharing — no opt-out required but written contract with use restriction mandatory; (3) transaction processing and other §§1016.14/1016.15 exceptions — no notice or opt-out required at all. Affiliate sharing is not restricted by Regulation P (FCRA affiliate marketing opt-out in §1022.20–1022.27 applies separately). **(§§1016.10, 1016.13, 1016.14, 1016.15)**
- **The account number prohibition is absolute with only two narrow exceptions.** Account numbers and similar access codes cannot be disclosed to nonaffiliated third parties for telemarketing, direct mail, or email marketing, regardless of opt-out status. **(§1016.12)**
- **Opt-out directions are permanent and survive relationship termination.** They apply to NPI collected before and during the relationship. A new customer relationship with the same institution does not automatically reset a prior opt-out. **(§1016.7(i))**
