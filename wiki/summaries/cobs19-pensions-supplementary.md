# COBS 19 — Pensions Supplementary Provisions

## What COBS 19 Does

COBS 19 imposes a specialist layer of requirements on top of the general suitability rules in COBS 9 wherever a firm deals with **safeguarded pension benefits** — primarily defined benefit (DB) schemes, schemes with guaranteed annuity rates (GARs), and similar arrangements where the client gives up a guaranteed income stream for flexible access. The chapter also governs **open market options** at retirement and the governance of **workplace pension schemes** through independent governance committees (IGCs).

COBS 19.1 (the pension transfer advice chapter) is one of the most heavily prescriptive sections in the FCA Handbook. The FCA's starting point is explicit: **transfers away from safeguarded benefits are presumed unsuitable**.

## COBS 19.1 — Advice on Pension Transfers and Conversions

### Application (COBS 19.1.-1AR)

COBS 19.1 applies to a firm which:
- Advises on, or arranges, a **pension transfer** or **pension conversion** from a safeguarded benefit scheme to a scheme with flexible benefits; or
- Advises on a **pension opt-out** — where a client is eligible to join an occupational pension scheme with safeguarded benefits and is advised not to do so.

The GAR-only exception: where the **only** safeguarded benefit involved is a **guaranteed annuity rate**, several requirements are disapplied (particularly around APTA, TVC, and the full PTS check regime — though the general suitability requirement still applies).

---

### Pension Transfer Specialist (COBS 19.1.1AR)

A firm providing advice on whether to transfer or convert safeguarded benefits must ensure the **advice is given or checked by a pension transfer specialist (PTS)** **(COBS 19.1.1AR)**.

The PTS checking role **(COBS 19.1.1BG)** requires the PTS to:
- Check the **entirety** of the advice (not just the technical analysis);
- Confirm the recommendation is **suitable**; and
- Confirm this in **writing** before the advice is given to the client.

A PTS cannot simply rubber-stamp another adviser's analysis — the checking obligation is substantive.

---

### Personal Recommendation Required Before Arrangement (COBS 19.1.1CR)

Before a firm can arrange a pension transfer or conversion from safeguarded benefits, it must **(COBS 19.1.1CR)**:

1. Determine the proposed receiving arrangement;
2. Carry out an **appropriate pension transfer analysis (APTA)** and produce a **transfer value comparator (TVC)**;
3. Ensure the client understands the advice, risks, and features of the proposed arrangement;
4. Obtain evidence that the client **can demonstrate they understand the risks** of proceeding; and
5. Make a **personal recommendation** on whether to proceed.

**Abridged advice cannot satisfy point (5).** Only a full personal recommendation can support an arrangement **(COBS 19.1.7C)**.

---

### APTA — Mandatory Methodology (COBS 19.1.2BR / 19.1.2CR)

The appropriate pension transfer analysis must be carried out **in accordance with Annex 4A** (for transfers involving non-GAR safeguarded benefits) and **Annex 4C** (format requirements) **(COBS 19.1.2BR)**.

A firm may use an **alternative analysis** (e.g. stochastic cashflow modelling) **only if** the projected outcomes at the **50th percentile are no less conservative** than those produced by the Annex 4A methodology **(COBS 19.1.2CR)**. This is the hard gate for robo-advice and automated pension transfer tools: they cannot use a proprietary methodology that produces more favourable transfer-recommended outcomes than the FCA's mandated approach.

---

### TVC — Transfer Value Comparator (COBS 19.1.3AR)

The TVC compares the transfer value with the **cost of buying equivalent future income benefits** using a pension annuity in the open market **(COBS 19.1.3AR)**. The TVC:
- Must be in the format prescribed in **Annex 5**;
- Must include variants for post-normal retirement age and early retirement where applicable; and
- Must be provided to the client as part of the suitability report.

The TVC is a standardised comparator — it is not an expression of the adviser's view but a mandated disclosure of what it would cost the client to replicate the benefits they are giving up.

---

### Presumption Against Transfer (COBS 19.1.6G)

This is the defining principle of DB transfer advice:

> **A firm should start by assuming that a transfer, conversion, or opt-out will NOT be suitable for a retail client** **(COBS 19.1.6G(2))**.

The firm must **clearly demonstrate, on the basis of contemporary evidence**, that the transfer is in the client's best interests **(COBS 19.1.6G(3))**. The burden of justification lies on the firm — not on the client to disprove suitability.

**Seven safeguarded-benefit risk factors** must be considered when assessing whether a transfer from safeguarded benefits is suitable **(COBS 19.1.6G(4))**:

1. The loss of a guaranteed income for life (and any dependant's income);
2. The loss of guaranteed increases in income in payment;
3. The loss of certainty about the value of income in retirement;
4. The loss of scheme-specific protections (e.g. enhanced tax-free cash, early retirement rights);
5. The risk of the client drawing down funds unsustainably;
6. Tax implications;
7. The impact on means-tested benefits.

#### Qualifying Scheme Bias (COBS 19.1.6G(7–11))

Where a **qualifying scheme (QS)** — typically a CDC scheme or master trust with value-for-money characteristics set by regulation — is available to the client:

- The firm **must start by assuming the QS default arrangement is more suitable** than a transfer to a non-QS arrangement **(COBS 19.1.6G(7))**.

Three factors which are **explicitly insufficient on their own** to justify a non-QS transfer **(COBS 19.1.6G(9))**:
1. The client wanting **wider investment options** than the QS offers;
2. The transfer being **more than 12 months before decumulation** — time to adjust investment strategy within the QS; and
3. The client **entering decumulation in the next 12 months** without having decided how to access their funds.

---

### Evidence of Client Understanding (COBS 19.1.1CR(5); COBS 19.1.9AR)

Before finalising the personal recommendation, the firm must **obtain evidence that the client can demonstrate they understand the risks** of proceeding **(COBS 19.1.1CR(5))**.

The firm must make a clear record of **(COBS 19.1.9AR)**:
- **(a)** The evidence showing the client demonstrated understanding and the steps taken to obtain it; **or**
- **(b)** If the client **could not** demonstrate understanding and the firm did not change to a recommendation against transfer — the steps taken, and clear evidence and explanation of how the firm satisfied itself on reasonable grounds that the same personal recommendation was still appropriate.

Records under COBS 19.1.9AR must be **retained indefinitely**.

---

### Arranging Without Advising (COBS 19.1.7C)

A firm that **arranges** (but does not itself advise on) a pension transfer or conversion must **(COBS 19.1.7C)**:

1. Confirm from the **advising firm** that the client received a **full personal recommendation** — not abridged advice. Abridged advice **can never** support an arrangement, and the arranging firm must not even request confirmation of abridged advice;

2. If the advising firm's recommendation was **NOT to transfer**: warn the client that the recommendation is against the transfer; ask whether the client **understands the consequences** of proceeding against advice; and **refuse to arrange** and refer back to the advising firm if the client does not demonstrate understanding; and

3. **Retain records indefinitely** of all steps taken under this rule.

The mandatory refusal power is absolute — the arranging firm cannot proceed on an execution-only basis where it knows the advising firm recommended against the transfer and the client has not demonstrated understanding.

---

### Suitability Report Content (COBS 19.1.8G)

Where a suitability report is provided in accordance with COBS 9.4.2AR (see COBS 9 summary), it should additionally include **(COBS 19.1.8G)**:

1. A summary of the **advantages and disadvantages** of the personal recommendation;
2. An analysis of the **financial implications** (if the recommendation is to opt out);
3. A summary of the **key outcomes from the APTA** (if the recommendation is to transfer or convert); and
4. A summary of any other **material information**.

The one-page summary required by COBS 9.4.2AR must be at the front of the suitability report and include the charges comparison (ceding arrangement vs proposed arrangement vs default QS).

## COBS 19.1A — Abridged Advice

### What Abridged Advice Is

Abridged advice is a **simplified initial assessment** that allows a firm to give a retail client a limited recommendation — specifically, a recommendation to **remain in their ceding arrangement** — without carrying out the full APTA and TVC **(COBS 19.1A.6R prohibits APTA/TVC in abridged advice)**. It is a triage tool, not an advice route to a transfer.

A firm giving abridged advice must either **(COBS 19.1A.3R)**:
1. Make a personal recommendation that the **client remains in their ceding arrangement**; or
2. Inform the client it is **unable to take a view** on whether transfer is in their best interests without undertaking full pension transfer or conversion advice, check whether the client wants full advice and that the client understands its cost, and (where relevant) make the client aware of the implications for adviser charges if they are in serious ill-health or serious financial difficulty.

**A PTS must give or check abridged advice (COBS 19.1A.7R).**

**Abridged advice can never support an arrangement (COBS 19.1A.10R).** A firm must not arrange a transaction where only abridged advice has been given.

### Charging for Abridged Advice (COBS 19.1A.12G)

A firm may provide abridged advice free of charge. If it charges for abridged advice and the client then proceeds to full advice, the firm **must offset the abridged advice charge** against the full advice charge — it cannot charge twice for what is in substance the same service **(COBS 19.1A.12G(2))**.

## COBS 19.1B — Ban on Contingent Charging

### The Prohibition (COBS 19.1B.3R)

A firm providing full pension transfer or conversion advice must ensure that its adviser charges, employer/trustee-funded advice charges, and remuneration **do not vary depending on**:
- Whether the firm makes a personal recommendation to transfer or convert;
- Whether the client effects a pension transfer or conversion; or
- Whether funds derived from a transfer or conversion are included in an ongoing advice arrangement.

**Contingent charging** — where the firm only receives payment if the client transfers — is prohibited because it creates an incentive to recommend transfers regardless of suitability **(COBS 19.1B.2G)**.

### Examples of Unacceptable Practices (COBS 19.1B.7E)

These are evidential provisions — contravention tends to establish breach of the ban:

1. Charges that are higher when the recommendation is to transfer than when it is not;
2. Higher ongoing charges where transferred funds are invested vs. non-transferred funds;
3. Purporting to charge the same for transfer advice vs. non-transfer advice but not taking reasonable steps to enforce payment when the advice is not to transfer;
4. Charging less for other services if the client is advised not to transfer;
5. Subsequently varying charges so they become dependent on the outcome or whether the client effects a transfer.

### Exceptions (COBS 19.1B.9R)

A firm may charge on a contingent basis where it has satisfied itself, on reasonable grounds and adequate supporting evidence, that the client is **unable to pay** for non-contingent full advice without using funds that are not reasonably available, and is either:
- **(1)** Suffering from **serious ill-health** (particular medical condition with life expectancy below 75 in most cases **(COBS 19.1B.11G)**); or
- **(2)** Experiencing **serious financial difficulty** (continuing to pay domestic bills and credit commitments is a heavy burden and the client has missed payments for any credit commitments and/or domestic bills in any three or more of the last six calendar months **(COBS 19.1B.13R)**) **and** would be able to access their pension fund immediately after a transfer.

Where a firm charges contingently under an exception, the total charges must be **no higher than if charged on a non-contingent basis (COBS 19.1B.10R)** and records must be **retained indefinitely (COBS 19.1B.17R)**.

## COBS 19.4 — Open Market Options

### Obligation to Provide an Open Market Options Statement (COBS 19.4.5AR)

A firm operating a retail client's personal pension scheme, stakeholder pension scheme, FSAVC, retirement annuity contract, or pension buy-out contract must give the client an open market options (OMO) statement:

- **Within two months** after the client reaches **50 years of age**; and
- **Between four and ten weeks before** each birthday at five-year intervals after the client's 50th birthday.

Additional triggers require an OMO statement where the client requests a retirement quotation, signals intention to access pension savings, or requests to access savings for the first time.

### OMO Statement — Mandatory Contents (COBS 19.4.6AR / 19.4.6CR)

All OMO statements (other than the age-50 statement) must include:
- A **single page summary document** (max one A4 side **(COBS 19.4.6B)**);
- A **fact sheet** (MoneyHelper fact sheet or equivalent);
- **Appropriate retirement risk warnings**;
- A statement about whether guarantees apply; and
- Information to enable the client to make an informed decision about exercising open market options.

The single page summary must include the client's name, intended retirement date, current pension savings value, early exit charge warning, guarantee statement, and a **clear and prominent statement about the availability of pensions guidance** (MoneyHelper) with contact details and access methods **(COBS 19.4.6CR(10))**.

### Retirement Risk Warnings (COBS 19.4.8B–19.4.8J)

The firm must prepare personalised retirement risk warnings before providing the OMO statement. Process:
1. **Prepare** warnings by identifying the main risk factors relevant to retail clients' exercise of open market options **(COBS 19.4.8C)**;
2. **Identify** which warnings to give to this specific client using information held, identifying which risk factors are most likely to be present **(COBS 19.4.8E)**; if unclear whether a risk factor is present, assume it is present **(COBS 19.4.8F)**;
3. **Present** warnings on a **single A4 side** **(COBS 19.4.8J)**; warnings provided between four and ten weeks before the client reaches 55 must include a clear and prominent statement that accessing pension savings at this point may not be the best option **(COBS 19.4.8H)**.

### Signposting Pensions Guidance (COBS 19.4.16R)

When communicating with a retail client about their pension scheme or contract, the firm must (unless an exception applies):
- Refer to the availability of **pensions guidance** (MoneyHelper);
- Offer information on how to access pensions guidance; and
- Include a **recommendation** that the client seeks appropriate guidance or advice.

Actively discouraging a retail client from using pensions guidance — for example by leading the client to believe it is unnecessary or obscuring the statement about its availability — is likely to contravene the client's best interests rule and Principles 6 and 7 **(COBS 19.4.17G)**.

### Six-Week Reminder (COBS 19.4.9R)

At least **six weeks before** the retail client's intended retirement date the firm must:
1. Remind the client about the OMO statement;
2. Tell the client what sum will be available to exercise open market options;
3. Provide a clear and prominent statement recommending use of pensions guidance; and
4. Recommend that the client seeks appropriate guidance or advice.

The reminder must not include financial promotions for pension decumulation products **(COBS 19.4.10R)**.

## COBS 19.5 — Independent Governance Committees (IGCs)

### Obligation to Establish an IGC (COBS 19.5.2R)

A firm operating a **relevant scheme** (workplace personal pension scheme) in which there are at least two relevant policyholders, or a firm offering a **pathway investment**, must establish an **IGC** unless:
- It has established a **governance advisory arrangement** instead **(COBS 19.5.3R)**; or
- Another firm in its group has an IGC that covers the relevant scheme.

### IGC's Terms of Reference — Minimum Requirements (COBS 19.5.5R)

The IGC must act **solely in the interests of relevant policyholders**. Its mandatory terms of reference include:

- **Value for money assessment** of the relevant scheme and/or pathway investment by reference to: (a) level of charges and costs; (b) investment performance; and (c) quality of services. The IGC must compare the scheme against **scheme comparators** selected from other employer pension arrangements;
- **ESG and stewardship review**: where the firm has an investment strategy or makes investment decisions with material impact on returns, the IGC will consider and report on the adequacy and quality of the firm's policies on ESG financial considerations, non-financial matters, and stewardship;
- **Escalation**: the IGC will raise concerns with the firm's governing body and, where concerns are unaddressed, will escalate as appropriate;
- **Quorum**: at least three members, majority independent; and
- **Annual report**: the Chair produces an annual report covering the IGC's value for money opinion, adequacy of ESG/stewardship policies, and the IGC's overall assessment.

## Key Points for Agents

- **Presumption against transfer — start negative (COBS 19.1.6G(2–3)).** A firm advising on pension transfers or conversions from safeguarded benefits must start by assuming the transfer will NOT be suitable. The burden is on the firm to demonstrate on contemporary evidence that it IS in the client's best interests. This is the defining feature of DB transfer advice and cannot be contracted away or reversed by the client asserting they want to transfer.

- **Qualifying scheme bias is a separate and additional presumption (COBS 19.1.6G(7–11)).** Where a qualifying scheme is available, the firm must also assume the QS default is more suitable than a non-QS transfer. Three things that are explicitly insufficient on their own to rebut this: wanting wider investment options; being more than 12 months from decumulation; entering decumulation in the next 12 months without having decided how to access funds.

- **APTA mandatory methodology gate — alternative approaches are permitted only if 50th-percentile outcomes are no less conservative than Annex 4A (COBS 19.1.2BR/2CR).** Firms cannot use stochastic modelling or other approaches that produce more favourable transfer-recommended outcomes than the FCA's prescribed methodology. This is the hard constraint on automated and robo-advice tools in the DB transfer space.

- **Arranging without advising carries a mandatory refusal obligation (COBS 19.1.7C).** Where the advising firm recommended against transfer, the arranging firm must warn the client, ask whether they understand the consequences, and refuse to arrange if they do not demonstrate understanding. Abridged advice can never support arrangement — the arranging firm may not even ask the advising firm to confirm abridged advice. Records indefinitely.

- **Evidence of client understanding is a pre-recommendation gate, not just a disclosure (COBS 19.1.1CR(5); COBS 19.1.9AR).** Before finalising the personal recommendation, the firm must obtain evidence that the client can demonstrate they understand the risks. If the client cannot demonstrate understanding and the firm still recommends the transfer, the firm must document how it satisfied itself on reasonable grounds that the recommendation was still appropriate. Records indefinitely.

- **Contingent charging is banned — the charge structure must not vary with the outcome (COBS 19.1B.3R).** A firm that charges more (or only charges) when the client transfers creates a structural conflict of interest that is prohibited. Two narrow exceptions apply: serious ill-health and serious financial difficulty — both require adequate supporting evidence, are subject to the no-more-than-non-contingent-equivalent cap, and require indefinite record retention.

- **OMO statement triggers and six-week reminder are hard Rules (COBS 19.4.5AR; 19.4.9R).** Any firm operating a personal pension scheme or similar must send an OMO statement at age 50, at five-year intervals thereafter, and on specific event triggers. The six-week-before-retirement reminder is a standalone requirement distinct from the OMO statement. Neither may include financial promotions for pension decumulation products.
